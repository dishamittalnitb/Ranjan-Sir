# Chapter 23: Probability [FORMATIVE ONLY]

## Concept Summary

This formative-only topic introduces empirical probability:

1. **History**: Brief history of probability from games of chance to modern mathematical theory. Contributions of Cardano, Fermat, Pascal, and others.

2. **Empirical Probability**: Based on actual experiments and observed data rather than theoretical calculations. Also called experimental or observed probability.

3. **Repeated Experiments**: Conducting an experiment many times and recording outcomes. The relative frequency of an event approximates its probability as the number of trials increases.

4. **Formula**: 
   - P(E) = (Number of trials in which E occurred) / (Total number of trials)
   
5. **Activity-Based Learning**: A large amount of time should be devoted to group and individual activities. Experiments may involve:
   - Tossing coins
   - Rolling dice
   - Drawing cards
   - Drawing objects from a bag
   - Spinning spinners

6. **Connection to Statistics**: Experiments can be drawn from real-life situations and examples from the statistics chapter.

## Key Formulas/Theorems

**Empirical Probability**: P(E) = (Number of times event E occurs) / (Total number of trials)

**Properties**:
- 0 <= P(E) <= 1
- P(impossible event) = 0
- P(certain event) = 1
- P(E) + P(not E) = 1
