# Chapter 18: The Lost Child

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The story highlights the deep, instinctive bond between a child and his parents, suggesting that material attractions pale in comparison to the security and love of family. It also portrays the beauty and chaos of an Indian village fair.

**Summary:** A young boy goes to a spring fair with his parents. On the way, he is fascinated by everything he sees — toys at the wayside shops, sweetmeats (burfi, gulab jamun, jalebi), balloons, a snake-charmer's flute, and a roundabout swing. Each time, his parents call him away or refuse, and he obediently follows. Attracted by the roundabout, he finally turns to ask for a ride — but his parents are nowhere to be found. The boy panics, runs around crying, and eventually a kind stranger finds him. The stranger tries to console him with all the things the boy had earlier wanted — the roundabout, balloons, sweets — but the boy refuses everything, crying only "I want my mother, I want my father!" The story ends with the heartbreaking image of the lost child, who no longer cares for any material pleasure.

## Character List

- **The Lost Child (unnamed):** An innocent, curious little boy; his desires shift from material things to reunion with parents.
- **The Child's Parents (Mother and Father):** Protective and disciplining; represent security and home.
- **The Kind Stranger:** A compassionate man who finds the lost child and tries to console him.

## Key Quotes

1. "I want my father. I want my mother."
2. "He took the child to the roundabout and walked round and round with him."
3. "The child simply cried and cried, 'I want my mother. I want my father.'"
4. "He could not be tempted by any of the things the stranger offered him."
