# Chapter 13: Constitutional Design

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Discuss and describe the situation that led to the creation of the Indian Constitution.
- Enumerate the essential features that need to be kept in mind while drafting a constitution.
- Examine the guiding values that created the Indian constitution.
- Comprehend the roles and responsibilities as citizens of India.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **What is a Constitution?**: A constitution is a set of fundamental rules and principles according to which a country is governed. It defines the structure of government, distribution of powers, rights of citizens, and procedures of law-making.
2. **Need for a Constitution**: Ensures rule of law, limits the powers of the government, protects fundamental rights, provides a framework for governance, and expresses the aspirations of the people.
3. **South Africa's Constitution**: After apartheid ended (1994), South Africa adopted a new constitution based on equality, dignity, and multiculturalism. Nelson Mandela played a key role in this transition.
4. **Making of the Indian Constitution**:
   - The Constituent Assembly was formed in 1946, with Dr. Rajendra Prasad as its President and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar as the Chairman of the Drafting Committee.
   - The Constitution took 2 years, 11 months, and 18 days to draft.
   - Adopted on 26 November 1949 (celebrated as Constitution Day).
   - Came into force on 26 January 1950 (celebrated as Republic Day).
5. **Guiding Values of the Indian Constitution**:
   - **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic**: These are the core values enshrined in the Preamble.
   - **Justice** (social, economic, political), **Liberty** (of thought, expression, belief, faith, and worship), **Equality** (of status and opportunity), and **Fraternity** (assuring dignity of the individual and unity and integrity of the nation).
6. **Key Features**: Parliamentary form of government, federal structure with unitary bias, fundamental rights, fundamental duties, directive principles of state policy, independent judiciary, universal adult franchise.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Constitution**: A body of fundamental principles according to which a state is governed.
- **Preamble**: An introductory statement in a constitution that outlines the core values and guiding principles.
- **Constituent Assembly**: A body of representatives elected/selected to draft a country's constitution.
- **Sovereign**: Independent and free from external control.
- **Secular**: Equal treatment of all religions; no state religion.
- **Socialist**: Aimed at reducing social and economic inequality.
- **Democratic**: Government by the people, through their elected representatives.
- **Republic**: A state where the head of state is elected, not a hereditary monarch.
- **Apartheid**: A system of institutionalized racial segregation and discrimination in South Africa.
- **Universal Adult Franchise**: The right of all adults (18+ years) to vote without discrimination.
