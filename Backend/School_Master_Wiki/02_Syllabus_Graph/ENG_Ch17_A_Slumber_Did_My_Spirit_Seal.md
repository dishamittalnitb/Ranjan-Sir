# Chapter 17: A Slumber Did My Spirit Seal

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The poem is one of Wordsworth's celebrated "Lucy Poems." It deals with the theme of death, loss, and the finality of mortality. It contrasts the speaker's earlier illusion of permanence and immortality with the harsh realization that the beloved (Lucy) is now part of the earth, beyond human fears and the passage of time.

**Summary:** The poem consists of two quatrains. In the first stanza, the speaker recalls a time when he lived in a "slumber" (an illusion or dream), believing the beloved (Lucy) was protected from the passage of time and mortality — "She seemed a thing that could not feel / The touch of earthly years." In the second stanza, reality hits: she has died. She now has no motion, no force, no hearing, no sight. She has become part of the earth's natural cycle — rolled round with rocks, stones, and trees in the earth's diurnal (daily) motion. The dead girl is now one with nature, beyond the human world.

## Literary Devices

- **Rhyme Scheme:** ABAB CDCD (alternating rhyme in both quatrains).
- **Alliteration:** "spirit seal," "rolled round"
- **Assonance:** "fears," "years"
- **Paradox:** The speaker was "asleep" (spiritually unaware) when he thought she was alive/beyond death; now he is "awake" to her death.
- **Imagery:** "rocks and stones and trees" — vivid natural imagery of the earth.
- **Contrast:** Stanza 1 (illusion of immortality) vs. Stanza 2 (reality of death and absorption into nature).
- **Tone:** First stanza is calm/ignorant; second stanza is stark, detached yet deeply moving.

## Key Quotes

1. "A slumber did my spirit seal; / I had no human fears"
2. "She seemed a thing that could not feel / The touch of earthly years."
3. "No motion has she now, no force; / She neither hears nor sees"
4. "Rolled round in earth's diurnal course, / With rocks, and stones, and trees."
