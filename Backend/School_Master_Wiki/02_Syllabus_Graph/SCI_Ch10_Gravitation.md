# Chapter 10: Gravitation

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Gravitation
- The force of attraction between any two objects in the universe due to their masses.
- Gravitation is a universal force; it acts between any two objects with mass.

### 1.2 Universal Law of Gravitation
- Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres.
- **F = G (m1 x m2) / r^2**
  - Where G = Universal Gravitational Constant = 6.67 x 10^-11 N m^2 / kg^2.
- This is true for all objects, irrespective of size, distance, or location.

### 1.3 Gravity (Force of Gravitation of the Earth)
- The force of attraction exerted by the Earth on objects near its surface.
- Gravity = mg (product of mass and acceleration due to gravity).
- Importance of the universal law of gravitation:
  - Binds us to the Earth.
  - Explains motion of planets around the Sun, and moon around Earth.
  - Explains ocean tides.

### 1.4 Acceleration due to Gravity (g)
- The acceleration produced in a body due to the gravitational pull of the Earth.
- **g = G M / R^2** (M = mass of Earth, R = radius of Earth).
- Value of g on Earth's surface: approximately **9.8 m/s^2** (CGS: 980 cm/s^2).
- g varies with: altitude (decreases with height), depth (decreases), latitude (poles: max; equator: min).

### 1.5 Mass and Weight
| Property | Mass | Weight |
|----------|------|--------|
| **Definition** | Amount of matter in a body | Force with which Earth pulls the body |
| **Nature** | Scalar | Vector |
| **SI Unit** | kg | Newton (N) |
| **Measurement** | Beam balance | Spring balance |
| **Constancy** | Same everywhere | Varies with g (location-dependent) |
| **Formula** | m | W = mg |

- Weight of an object on the Moon = (1/6)th of its weight on Earth (since Moon's g ~ 1.63 m/s^2 ~ 1/6th of Earth's g).

### 1.6 Free Fall
- When an object falls solely under the influence of Earth's gravity (no air resistance or other forces).
- A freely falling body experiences acceleration equal to g.
- Equations of motion for free fall (replace a with g, s with h):
  - v = u + gt
  - h = ut + (1/2)gt^2
  - v^2 = u^2 + 2gh
- All objects, irrespective of mass, fall with same acceleration (g) in the absence of air resistance (Galileo's observation).

## 3. Board Exam Weightage & Question Pattern

Falls under **Unit III: Motion, Force and Work (27 marks)**.
