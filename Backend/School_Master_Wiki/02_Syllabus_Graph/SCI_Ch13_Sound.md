# Chapter 13: Sound

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Nature of Sound
- Sound is a form of energy that produces a sensation of hearing.
- Produced by vibrating objects; propagated as longitudinal mechanical waves.
- Requires a material medium (solid, liquid, or gas) for propagation - cannot travel through vacuum.

### 1.2 Propagation of Sound
- Sound travels as a longitudinal wave: particles of the medium vibrate parallel to the direction of wave propagation.
- **Compression:** Region of high pressure and high density.
- **Rarefaction:** Region of low pressure and low density.
- Compressions and rarefactions travel through the medium in the form of a wave.

### 1.3 Characteristics of Sound Waves
| Property | Definition | Unit |
|----------|-----------|------|
| **Wavelength (lambda)** | Distance between two consecutive compressions or rarefactions | metre (m) |
| **Frequency (nu)** | Number of oscillations per second (equal to frequency of vibrating source) | Hertz (Hz) |
| **Time Period (T)** | Time taken for one complete oscillation. T = 1/nu | second (s) |
| **Amplitude (A)** | Maximum displacement of a particle from its mean position | metre (m) |
| **Speed (v)** | Distance travelled by the wave per unit time. v = nu x lambda | m/s |

### 1.4 Speed of Sound
- Speed depends on the medium: solid > liquid > gas.
- Speed in air at 0 degree C ~ 331 m/s; at 25 degree C ~ 346 m/s.
- Factors affecting speed in air: Temperature (increases with temperature) and humidity (increases with humidity). Speed is independent of pressure at constant temperature.

### 1.5 Range of Hearing in Humans
- Audible range for human ear: **20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz)**.
- **Infrasonic sound:** Below 20 Hz (e.g., earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, elephants, whales).
- **Ultrasonic sound:** Above 20 kHz (e.g., bats, dolphins, SONAR).

### 1.6 Ultrasound and Its Applications
- Ultrasound: Sound waves with frequency > 20 kHz.
- **Applications:**
  - SONAR (Sound Navigation and Ranging): To detect underwater objects, submarines, icebergs, and measure sea depth.
  - Medical imaging (ultrasonography): To view internal organs, monitor foetal development.
  - Cleaning delicate objects (ultrasonic cleaners).
  - Detection of cracks and flaws in metal blocks.
  - Echolocation by bats.

### 1.7 Reflection of Sound
- Sound bounces off a surface (like light). Follows laws of reflection:
  - Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection.
  - Incident sound, reflected sound, and normal all lie in the same plane.

### 1.8 Echo
- Repetition of sound due to reflection from a surface.
- Condition for hearing an echo: Minimum time gap of 0.1 s between original sound and reflected sound.
- Minimum distance of reflecting surface: d = v x t / 2 = 344 x 0.1 / 2 = 17.2 m (approx.).
- **Reverberation:** Persistence of sound due to multiple reflections; reduced by sound-absorbing materials in auditoriums.

### 1.9 SONAR
- **S**ound **N**avigation **A**nd **R**anging.
- Used to measure distance, depth, or locate objects underwater.
- **Principle:** Ultrasonic waves transmitted, echo received; distance calculated using d = v x t / 2.
