# Chapter 04: A Truly Beautiful Mind

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The chapter is a biographical sketch of Albert Einstein, celebrating not just his scientific genius but also his humanitarian vision. It emphasizes that a truly beautiful mind combines intellectual brilliance with compassion, peace advocacy, and a sense of global citizenship.

**Summary:** The chapter traces Einstein's life from his birth in Ulm, Germany in 1879. As a child, he was considered slow and his mother thought he was "a freak" due to his unusually large head. He disliked school's regimentation and left at age 15. He later studied in Switzerland, worked as a technical expert at the patent office in Bern, and developed the Special Theory of Relativity (1905) followed by the General Theory of Relativity (1915), including the famous equation E=mc2. He won the Nobel Prize in Physics (1921). When Nazis came to power in Germany (1933), Einstein emigrated to the USA. Shaken by the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, he wrote a letter to the United Nations proposing a world government. He spent his later years advocating for peace and democracy.

## Character List

- **Albert Einstein:** The central figure; a scientific genius, Nobel laureate, and passionate advocate for peace and world government.
- **Einstein's Mother:** Thought young Albert was unusual; played the piano and encouraged his musical interests.
- **Mileva Maric:** Einstein's first wife, a fellow student at Zurich University; described as intelligent and supportive.
- **Elsa:** Einstein's second wife and cousin.

## Key Quotes

1. "Imagination is more important than knowledge."
2. "The arms race is a disaster for mankind."
3. "A letter to the United Nations proposed the formation of a world government."
4. "He called himself a citizen of the world, not of any particular nation."
