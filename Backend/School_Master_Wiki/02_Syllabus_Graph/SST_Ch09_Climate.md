# Chapter 09: Climate

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Analyse and infer the effect of monsoon winds on rainfall of the Indian subcontinent.
- Analyse the temperatures between plateau region, Himalayan region, desert region, and coastal region.
- Enumerate and summarise the reasons for the wide difference between temperatures at different geographical locations of India.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Climate vs Weather**: Climate is the long-term average of weather conditions over a large area; weather is the day-to-day state of atmosphere. India has a monsoon-type climate.
2. **Climatic Controls**: Latitude, altitude, distance from the sea (continentality), pressure and wind systems, ocean currents, and relief features influence climate.
3. **Factors Affecting India's Climate**:
   - Latitude: Tropic of Cancer divides India; tropical climate in the south, subtropical in the north.
   - Altitude: Himalayas block cold Central Asian winds; mountains cause orographic rainfall.
   - Pressure and Winds: Seasonal reversal of winds -- southwest monsoon (summer) and northeast monsoon (winter).
   - Westerly Disturbances: Bring winter rainfall to northwest India.
4. **The Monsoon**: India's climate is dominated by the monsoon winds caused by differential heating of land and sea. The monsoon sets in early June and withdraws by mid-September.
   - Arabian Sea Branch: Brings heavy rainfall to western coast.
   - Bay of Bengal Branch: Brings rain to northeast and moves westward across Ganga plains.
5. **Seasons of India**:
   - **Cold Weather Season (Winter)**: December to February. Low temperatures in north; moderate in south. Northeast trade winds bring rainfall to Tamil Nadu coast.
   - **Hot Weather Season (Summer)**: March to May. High temperatures; "loo" (hot, dry wind) in north; pre-monsoon showers ("mango showers") in Kerala/Karnataka.
   - **Advancing Monsoon (Rainy Season)**: June to September. Southwest monsoon brings widespread rainfall.
   - **Retreating Monsoon (Post-Monsoon)**: October-November. Clear skies, high humidity, cyclones on eastern coast.
6. **Distribution of Rainfall**: Highly uneven. Mawsynram (Meghalaya) receives the highest rainfall in the world. The Thar Desert and leeward side of Western Ghats (rain-shadow regions) receive scanty rainfall.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Monsoon**: A seasonal reversing wind accompanied by changes in precipitation, derived from the Arabic word "mausim."
- **Orographic/Relief Rainfall**: Rainfall caused when moist air rises over mountains.
- **Rain-Shadow Region**: The leeward (downwind) side of a mountain that receives little rainfall.
- **Jet Streams**: Narrow bands of strong wind in the upper atmosphere that influence monsoon.
- **Loo**: Hot, dry, gusty wind that blows over northern and western India during summer.
- **Mango Showers**: Pre-monsoon rainfall in coastal Kerala and Karnataka that helps mango ripening.
- **El Nino**: A periodic warming of Pacific Ocean waters that affects global weather, including weakening the Indian monsoon.
- **October Heat**: Hot and humid conditions in October after the monsoon withdrawal.
