# Chapter 34: Federalism

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Infer how federalism is being practised in India.
- Analyse the policies and politics that have strengthened federalism in practice.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **What is Federalism?**: A system of government in which power is divided between a central authority and various constituent units. Both levels enjoy independent authority.
2. **Key Features of Federalism**:
   - Two or more levels (tiers) of government.
   - Each level governs the same citizens but has its own jurisdiction in specific matters.
   - The constitution guarantees the existence and authority of each tier.
   - Changes in fundamental provisions require consent of both levels.
   - An independent judiciary resolves disputes between levels.
3. **Federalism in India**:
   - India is a "holding together" federation (not "coming together" like the USA).
   - Union of States: India is an indestructible union of destructible states (unlike the USA where states are indestructible).
   - Constitution clearly demarcates the distribution of powers:
     - **Union List** (97 subjects): Defence, foreign affairs, currency, banking, railways -- only the Union can legislate.
     - **State List** (66 subjects): Police, agriculture, public health, local government -- only states can legislate.
     - **Concurrent List** (47 subjects): Education, forests, trade unions, marriage -- both can legislate; in case of conflict, Union law prevails.
     - **Residuary Subjects**: All remaining powers go to the Union.
4. **Union Territories**: Territories that do not have the status of a full state and are directly governed by the Centre (e.g., Delhi, Chandigarh, Lakshadweep).
5. **Special Status and Asymmetric Federalism**: Some states enjoy special provisions under Article 371. Jammu & Kashmir previously enjoyed special status under Article 370 (abrogated in 2019).
6. **Decentralization in India (Third Tier)**: The 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments (1992) established:
   - **Rural Local Government (Panchayati Raj)**: Gram Panchayat (village level), Panchayat Samiti (block level), Zila Parishad (district level). 1/3rd seats reserved for women.
   - **Urban Local Government**: Municipalities and Municipal Corporations. Nagar Panchayats, Municipal Councils, and Municipal Corporations.
7. **Strengthening Federalism**: Coalition governments, linguistic reorganization of states (1956), rise of regional parties, and the role of the judiciary in interpreting federal provisions have strengthened Indian federalism.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Federalism**: A system of government in which power is constitutionally divided between a central authority and constituent political units.
- **Unitary System**: A system with a single, central government that holds all power; subordinate units exist at its discretion.
- **Union List**: Subjects on which only the central/union government can legislate.
- **State List**: Subjects on which only state governments can legislate.
- **Concurrent List**: Subjects on which both union and state governments can legislate.
- **Residuary Subjects**: Subjects not mentioned in any list -- the Union government legislates on these.
- **Panchayati Raj**: A three-tier system of rural local self-government in India.
- **Linguistic States**: States formed on the basis of language (after the States Reorganisation Act, 1956).
- **Decentralization**: The transfer of power from central and state governments to local governments.
