# Chapter 15: No Men Are Foreign

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The poem is a powerful plea for universal brotherhood, peace, and harmony. It argues that all humans are fundamentally the same — sharing the same earth, body, emotions, and experiences — and that war is a self-destructive act that harms the entire shared humanity. The central message is "unity in diversity" and the rejection of artificial divisions based on nationality, race, or religion.

**Summary:** The poet declares that no human beings are foreign or strange. Underneath every uniform (of any nation), the same human body breathes. All people walk upon the same earth, will one day be buried in the same earth. They share the same sun, air, water, food, and harvests. Their hands do the same labor. They have eyes that sleep and wake, and "strength that can be won by love." The poet then warns that when we hate, condemn, or take up arms against others, we defile the very earth and air that belong to all. War and hatred are a betrayal of our shared humanity.

## Literary Devices

- **Rhyme Scheme:** Free verse with occasional couplets; the poem relies on repetition and anaphora for structure.
- **Anaphora:** "Remember, no men are foreign..." (opening and closing refrain); "Remember..." used repeatedly.
- **Repetition:** "Remember" is the anchoring word; "the same" is repeated to underline unity.
- **Metaphor:** "Hell of fire" refers to the consequences of war; "peaceful harvests" symbolize shared prosperity.
- **Contrast:** "Land our brothers walk upon is like the land we walk upon" — emphasizing sameness.
- **Tone:** Didactic, persuasive, urgent, compassionate.

## Key Quotes

1. "Remember, no men are strange, no countries foreign / Beneath all uniforms, a single body breathes"
2. "They, too, aware of sun and air and water, / Are fed by peaceful harvests, by war's long winter starv'd."
3. "Their hands are ours, and in their lines we read / A labour not different from our own."
4. "Whenever we are told to hate our brothers... / It is ourselves we shall betray."
5. "Remember, we who take arms against each other / It is the human earth that we defile."
