# Chapter 27: Our Environment

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Ecosystem
- An ecosystem is a functional unit of nature where living organisms interact among themselves and with their physical environment.
- **Components of an ecosystem:**
  - **Biotic (Living):** Producers (autotrophs - plants, algae), Consumers (heterotrophs - herbivores, carnivores, omnivores), Decomposers (saprophytes - bacteria, fungi).
  - **Abiotic (Non-living):** Sunlight, temperature, water, soil, air, minerals.

### 1.2 Food Chain and Food Web
- **Food chain:** A linear sequence of organisms where each organism is eaten by the next one.
  - Grass --> Grasshopper --> Frog --> Snake --> Hawk/Eagle
  - **Trophic levels:** Each step in the food chain (Producer = T1, Primary Consumer = T2, Secondary Consumer = T3, Tertiary Consumer = T4).
- **Food web:** A network of interconnected food chains. More stable than a single food chain because if one organism is affected, alternative food sources are available.

### 1.3 Energy Flow in an Ecosystem
- Energy enters the ecosystem through producers (photosynthesis - conversion of solar energy to chemical energy).
- Energy flows **unidirectionally** (from Sun --> Producers --> Consumers --> Decomposers).
- **10% Law (Lindeman's Law):** Only about 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next. The rest is lost as heat during respiration, digestion, etc.
  - This explains why food chains generally have only 3-4 trophic levels (insufficient energy beyond that).
  - It explains why vegetarian diets provide more energy than meat-based diets (shorter food chain).

### 1.4 Environmental Problems
| Problem | Cause | Impact |
|---------|-------|--------|
| **Depletion of Ozone Layer** | Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) released from refrigerators, ACs, aerosol sprays | UV radiation reaches Earth's surface, causing skin cancer, cataracts, damage to immune system, harm to crops and marine life |
| **Waste Production** | Rapid urbanisation, consumerism, industrial growth | Pollution (land, water, air), health hazards, loss of biodiversity |
| **Climate Change** | Greenhouse gases (CO2, CH4, N2O) from burning fossil fuels, deforestation | Global warming, rising sea levels, extreme weather events |
| **Water Pollution** | Industrial effluents, sewage, agricultural run-off (fertilisers, pesticides) | Toxic water, eutrophication, loss of aquatic life |
| **Air Pollution** | Vehicular emissions, industrial smoke, burning of fossil fuels and crop residue | Respiratory diseases, acid rain, smog |

### 1.5 Ozone Layer and Ozone Depletion
- **Ozone (O3):** A molecule of three oxygen atoms; present in the stratosphere (10-50 km altitude).
- **Function:** Absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV-B and UV-C) radiation from the Sun.
- **Ozone depletion:** Caused primarily by CFCs. CFCs release chlorine atoms in the stratosphere, which catalytically destroy ozone.
  - Cl + O3 --> ClO + O2; ClO + O --> Cl + O2 (Net: 2O3 --> 3O2, Cl is a catalyst).
- **Ozone hole:** Severe thinning of the ozone layer over Antarctica.
- **Montreal Protocol (1987):** International agreement to phase out ozone-depleting substances (CFCs, halons). CFCs being replaced by HFCs (hydrofluorocarbons).

### 1.6 Waste Production and Their Solutions
#### Types of Waste
| Type | Biodegradable Waste | Non-Biodegradable Waste |
|------|--------------------|-------------------------|
| **Definition** | Substances that can be broken down by biological processes (microorganisms - bacteria, fungi) | Substances that cannot be broken down by natural biological processes |
| **Decomposition Time** | Fast (days to months) | Very slow / never (years to centuries) |
| **Examples** | Food waste, paper, wood, cotton, wool, agricultural waste, animal dung, dead plants/animals | Plastics, metals, glass, synthetic fibres (nylon, polyester), pesticides (DDT), e-waste, nuclear waste |
| **Environmental Impact** | Can be composted; produces useful manure; if not managed, can produce foul smell and methane | Accumulates in the environment; causes pollution; enters food chain (biomagnification); clogs drains |

#### Waste Management Solutions
- **3R Principle: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle.**
  - **Reduce:** Minimise waste generation (e.g., avoid single-use plastics, use cloth bags).
  - **Reuse:** Use items multiple times before discarding (e.g., glass bottles, envelopes).
  - **Recycle:** Convert waste into usable materials (e.g., paper recycling, metal recycling, plastic recycling).
- **Composting:** Decomposing biodegradable waste into manure.
- **Biogas production:** Anaerobic decomposition of organic waste to produce biogas (methane).
- **Sewage treatment:** Treatment of wastewater before discharge.
- **Proper e-waste disposal:** Separate collection and scientific recycling.
- **Segregation at source:** Separate bins for biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste.

### 1.7 Biological Magnification (Biomagnification)
- The increase in the concentration of harmful non-biodegradable substances (e.g., pesticides like DDT, heavy metals like mercury) at successive trophic levels in a food chain.
- The organism at the highest trophic level accumulates the highest concentration.
- **Example:** Water (0.003 ppb DDT) --> Phytoplankton (0.04 ppm) --> Zooplankton (0.5 ppm) --> Small Fish (2 ppm) --> Large Fish (25 ppm) --> Fish-eating Bird (124 ppm).
- This is why using non-biodegradable pesticides is harmful.

## 2. Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Ecosystem** | A functional unit where living organisms interact with each other and the physical environment. |
| **Food Chain** | A linear sequence showing who eats whom in an ecosystem. |
| **Food Web** | A network of interconnected food chains. |
| **Trophic Level** | Each step/position occupied by an organism in a food chain. |
| **10% Law** | Only ~10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next (Lindeman). |
| **Biodegradable** | Substances that can be broken down by biological action of microorganisms. |
| **Non-Biodegradable** | Substances that cannot be broken down by microorganisms. |
| **Ozone Layer** | A layer of ozone (O3) in the stratosphere that protects Earth from harmful UV radiation. |
| **Biomagnification** | Accumulation of non-biodegradable substances at higher trophic levels. |
| **CFCs** | Chlorofluorocarbons; compounds responsible for ozone depletion. |
