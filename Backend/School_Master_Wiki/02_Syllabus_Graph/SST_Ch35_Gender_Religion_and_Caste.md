# Chapter 35: Gender, Religion and Caste

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Examine the role and differences of Gender, Religion and Caste in practicing Democracy.
- Analyse that different expressions based on these differences are healthy or otherwise in a democracy.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Gender and Politics**:
   - **Gender Division**: A form of social division in which roles are stereotyped. Women face discrimination in education, employment, and decision-making.
   - **Women's Political Representation**: Women's participation in public/political life is low.
     - In India, women's literacy is 65.46% compared to male 82.14% (2011).
     - Women hold less than 15% of seats in Lok Sabha.
     - One-third reservation in local bodies (73rd and 74th Amendments) has increased women's participation.
     - Demand for 33% reservation in Parliament and State Legislatures (Women's Reservation Bill -- Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam passed in 2023).
   - **Feminist Movements**: Movements demanding equal rights for women in all spheres.
2. **Religion, Communalism, and Politics**:
   - **Religion and Politics**: Religion influences politics in many ways -- through moral values, identity politics, and through the state's engagement with religious institutions.
   - **Communalism**: The belief that people belonging to different religions cannot live as equal citizens within one nation; the ideology that one religion's interests are opposed to another's.
     - **Forms**: Prejudice and stereotypes, political mobilization on religious lines, communal violence (riots, pogroms).
   - **Secular State**: The Indian Constitution provides for a secular state (no official religion, freedom of religion, equality of all religions, prohibition of discrimination on religious grounds).
   - **Positive vs Negative Use**: Using religion for caste-based politics is divisive (communalism). However, religion can also champion moral values in politics (e.g., human rights, social justice).
3. **Caste and Politics**:
   - **Caste System**: An hereditary social stratification in India. Caste-based discrimination continues (untouchability in some areas despite being illegal).
   - **Caste in Politics**: Caste plays a significant role in elections (caste-based voting blocs), candidate selection, and formation of caste-based parties and organizations.
   - **Politics in Caste**: Politics also affects caste -- caste groups have politicized and mobilized to demand rights and recognition (e.g., OBC reservations, Dalit assertion through parties like BSP).
   - Erosion of caste hierarchies in urban areas and through education, but caste identity remains politically significant.
4. **Healthy vs Unhealthy Expressions**:
   - Differences based on gender, religion, and caste are normal in a democracy; acknowledging them helps in addressing inequalities.
   - Unhealthy when these differences become the basis for discrimination, violence, and divisive politics (communalism, casteism).

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Gender Division**: The unequal roles and responsibilities assigned to men and women by society.
- **Patriarchy**: A social system in which men hold primary power and dominate in roles of political leadership, moral authority, and control of property.
- **Feminism**: A movement advocating for equality of women in all spheres of life.
- **Communalism**: An ideology that organizes people on religious lines and considers one religion as superior to others.
- **Secular State**: A state that has no official religion and treats all religions equally.
- **Caste Hierarchy**: The graded system of social ranking in the traditional caste structure.
- **Adivasis**: Indigenous/tribal communities of India.
- **OBC (Other Backward Classes)**: A collective term for castes that are educationally and socially disadvantaged.
