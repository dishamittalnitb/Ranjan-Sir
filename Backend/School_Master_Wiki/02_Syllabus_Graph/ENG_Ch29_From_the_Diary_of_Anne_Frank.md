# Chapter 29: From the Diary of Anne Frank

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The chapter presents Anne Frank's intimate thoughts as a Jewish teenager in hiding during the Holocaust. It explores themes of loneliness, self-discovery, the power of writing, and the resilience of the human spirit in the face of persecution.

**Summary:** The extract begins with Anne's reflections on why she writes — she feels alone and believes "paper has more patience than people." She describes her family background: born in Frankfurt, emigrating to Holland in 1933 after Hitler's rise, and going into hiding in Amsterdam in July 1942. Anne names her diary "Kitty" and treats it as a true friend. She then gives a candid portrait of her class at school — describing her talkative nature (earning her the title "an incorrigible chatterbox"), her relationship with her strict math teacher Mr. Keesing, and the humorous essays she writes as punishment for talking (including one about three ducklings that finally wins the teacher over). The chapter ends on a light note, but carries the weight of knowing the tragic fate ahead.

## Character List

- **Anne Frank:** A 13-year-old Jewish girl; intelligent, witty, introspective, and surprisingly mature; writes to her diary "Kitty."
- **Mr. Keesing:** Anne's math teacher; initially strict and annoyed by Anne's chattering but appreciates her creativity.
- **Anne's Parents and Sister Margot:** Mentioned in the background; the family is in hiding.

## Key Quotes

1. "Paper has more patience than people."
2. "I can't bring myself to talk about anything but ordinary everyday things."
3. "Mr. Keesing had a good laugh at my arguments."
4. "I am not so much of a chatterbox as I seem."
