## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Natural Vegetation**: Plant communities that have grown naturally without human aid and have been left undisturbed for a long time. India has diverse vegetation due to varied climate and relief.
2. **Major Vegetation Types in India**:
   - **Tropical Evergreen Forests**: Found in heavy rainfall areas (Western Ghats, Andaman & Nicobar, NE India). Trees: ebony, mahogany, rosewood. Dense, multi-layered.
   - **Tropical Deciduous Forests (Monsoon Forests)**: Most widespread. Divided into moist deciduous (rainfall 100-200 cm, trees: teak, sal, shisham) and dry deciduous (rainfall 70-100 cm, trees: tendu, palas). Shed leaves in dry season.
   - **Tropical Thorn Forests and Scrubs**: Arid regions (Rajasthan, Gujarat). Trees: acacias, palms, cacti.
   - **Montane Forests**: Himalayan foothills to alpine; trees: conifers (pine, deodar, fir) at higher altitudes.
   - **Mangrove Forests (Tidal Forests)**: Found in coastal deltas, especially Sundarbans (Ganga-Brahmaputra delta). Sundari tree is characteristic. Adapt to saline water.
3. **Wildlife of India**: India is rich in biodiversity -- home to about 90,000 animal species. Includes:
   - Mammals: Bengal tiger, Asiatic lion, elephant, one-horned rhinoceros, leopard, deer.
   - Birds: Peacock (national bird), hornbill, sarus crane.
   - Reptiles: King cobra, gharial, turtles.
4. **Conservation**: Endangered species include the tiger, one-horned rhinoceros, snow leopard, Great Indian Bustard. Protection through Wildlife Protection Act (1972), Project Tiger (1973), Biosphere Reserves, National Parks, and Wildlife Sanctuaries.
5. **Reasons for Depletion**: Deforestation, urbanization, agriculture, overgrazing, forest fires, developmental projects.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Natural Vegetation**: Plants that grow naturally in a region without human intervention.
- **Ecosystem**: A community of living organisms interacting with their physical environment.
- **Biosphere Reserves**: Protected areas aimed at conserving biodiversity while allowing sustainable human use.
- **Endangered Species**: Species at risk of extinction.
- **Mangroves**: Salt-tolerant trees/shrubs growing in coastal inter-tidal zones.
- **Wildlife Sanctuary**: A protected area for the conservation of wildlife where limited human activity may be allowed.
- **National Park**: A protected area designated for wildlife conservation where human activity is strictly regulated.
- **Biodiversity**: The variety of plant and animal life in a particular habitat.
