# Chapter 40: Amanda!

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The poem highlights the conflict between a child's need for freedom and an adult's constant scolding and control. It critiques overbearing parenting and suggests that excessive restrictions drive children into fantasy worlds of escape, which impacts their mental and emotional development.

**Summary:** The poem alternates between two voices. The adult voice (likely the mother) continuously nags a girl named Amanda: Don't bite your nails, don't hunch your shoulders, sit up straight, finish your homework, clean your room, don't eat chocolate. Each time, Amanda's internal voice retreats into a rich fantasy world — she imagines herself as a mermaid drifting blissfully in the green sea, an orphan roaming freely, and Rapunzel living alone in a tower. In her mind, these fantasy lives offer her the peace, solitude, and freedom that her real life denies. The final stanza shows Amanda sulking and the adult asking why she is so moody — perfectly capturing the adult's failure to understand the child's inner world.

## Literary Devices

- **Rhyme Scheme:** Free verse with structured stanzas. The adult voice uses AABB (couplets), while Amanda's fantasy stanzas rhyme ABAB or AAA.
- **Contrast:** The adult's nagging voice vs. Amanda's silent, dreamy fantasies.
- **Alliteration:** "Stop that slouching," "freedom is sweet"
- **Metaphor:** Amanda's fantasies are metaphors for her desire for freedom — mermaid (carefree life), orphan (no one to control her), Rapunzel (peaceful solitude).
- **Imagery:** Vivid fantasy images — "emerald sea," "languid emerald sea," "tranquil and rare."
- **Tone:** Adult voice is sharp and commanding; Amanda's voice is dreamy, peaceful, yearning.

## Key Quotes

1. "Don't bite your nails, Amanda! / Don't hunch your shoulders, Amanda!"
2. "I am an orphan, roaming the street. / I pattern soft dust with my hushed, bare feet."
3. "I am Rapunzel, I have not a care; / life in a tower is tranquil and rare."
4. "Anyone would think that I nagged at you, Amanda!"
