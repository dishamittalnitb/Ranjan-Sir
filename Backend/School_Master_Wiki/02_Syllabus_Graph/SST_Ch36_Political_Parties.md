# Chapter 36: Political Parties

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Understand the process of parties getting elected.
- Know the significance of the right to vote and exercise the duties as citizens of a nation.
- Examine the role, purpose, and number of Political Parties in Democracy.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **What is a Political Party?**: A political party is an organized group of people who have the same ideology and who come together to contest elections and hold power in the government.
2. **Functions of Political Parties**:
   - Contest elections by putting up candidates.
   - Formulate policies and programmes and present them to voters (manifesto).
   - Form and run governments.
   - Play the role of opposition, critiquing the government's policies.
   - Shape public opinion by raising and highlighting issues.
   - Provide people access to government machinery and welfare schemes.
3. **Necessity of Political Parties**: Without parties, elected representatives would be independent and accountable to no one, leading to chaos. Parties provide a policy framework and aggregating interests.
4. **Party Systems**:
   - **One-party system**: Only one party controls (e.g., China).
   - **Two-party system**: Two major parties alternate in power (e.g., USA, UK).
   - **Multi-party system**: Several parties compete; coalition governments are common (e.g., India).
5. **National Parties in India**: Parties with a presence in multiple states. Recognized by the Election Commission:
   - **Indian National Congress (INC)**: Founded 1885; oldest party; centrist; led the freedom movement.
   - **Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)**: Founded 1980; cultural nationalism/Hindutva; currently in power at the centre.
   - **Communist Party of India (Marxist) - CPI(M)**: Founded 1964; left ideology; strong in Kerala, West Bengal, Tripura.
   - **Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP)**: Founded 1984; represents Dalits and marginalized communities; strong in UP.
   - **National People's Party (NPP)**: From Meghalaya.
   - **Aam Aadmi Party (AAP)**: Founded 2012; anti-corruption platform.
   - Other national parties as recognized by ECI.
6. **State Parties (Regional Parties)**: Parties with influence in one or few states. Examples: DMK, AIADMK (Tamil Nadu), TMC (West Bengal), Shiv Sena, NCP (Maharashtra), BJD (Odisha), TRS/BRS (Telangana), RJD, JD(U) (Bihar), SP (UP), SAD (Punjab).
7. **Challenges to Political Parties**:
   - Lack of internal democracy (power concentrated in a few leaders).
   - Dynastic succession.
   - Money and muscle power in elections.
   - Lack of meaningful choice (parties have similar policies on many issues).
8. **Reforms**:
   - Anti-defection law (1985) to prevent switching parties.
   - Mandatory disclosure of assets and criminal cases by candidates.
   - Supreme Court order on the use of NOTA.
   - Suggestions for state funding of elections and internal democratic processes.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Political Party**: An organized group aiming to gain power through elections and implement its policies.
- **Manifesto**: A public declaration of the party's policies and programmes before elections.
- **Ruling Party**: The party that wins the majority and forms the government.
- **Opposition Party**: The party (or parties) that does not form the government but keeps a check on the ruling party.
- **Coalition**: An alliance of two or more parties to form a government.
- **National Party**: A party recognized by the Election Commission with a presence across multiple states.
- **State Party (Regional Party)**: A party recognized in one or a few states.
- **Defection**: When an elected representative switches from one party to another.
- **Anti-Defection Law**: A law disqualifying members who defect from their party.
