# Chapter 14: Electoral Politics

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Analyse the implications of power of vote and power of recall.
- Summarise the essential features of the Indian Electoral system.
- Examine the rationale for adopting the present Indian Electoral System.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Why Elections?**: Elections are the method by which citizens choose their representatives. They make democracy representative, accountable, and legitimate. Elections are held at regular intervals.
2. **Types of Elections in India**:
   - **General Elections (Lok Sabha)**: Held every 5 years to elect Members of Parliament.
   - **State Assembly Elections (Vidhan Sabha)**: Held every 5 years to elect MLAs.
   - **By-Elections**: Held to fill vacant seats between general elections.
   - **Local Body Elections**: For Panchayats and Municipalities.
3. **Essential Features of the Indian Electoral System**:
   - Universal Adult Franchise (18+ years).
   - Independent Election Commission (Article 324).
   - Secret ballot/Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs).
   - First-Past-the-Post (FPTP) system.
   - Model Code of Conduct.
   - Reserved constituencies for SCs and STs.
   - Multi-party system.
4. **Voter's Power**: Each vote has equal value. Voting empowers citizens to influence government policies. The "power of recall" allows voters to remove representatives before their term ends (not available at the national/state level in India, but exists in some local bodies).
5. **Challenges to Free and Fair Elections**: Money and muscle power, criminalization of politics, use of caste and religion, booth capturing, voter intimidation, electoral malpractices.
6. **Reforms**: Introduction of EVMs, VVPAT (Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail), Model Code of Conduct, stricter anti-defection laws, and NOTA (None of the Above) option.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Election**: A formal decision-making process by which a population chooses individuals to hold public office.
- **Universal Adult Franchise**: The right of all adult citizens (18 years and above) to vote.
- **Constituency**: A geographical area whose voters elect a representative.
- **FPTP (First-Past-the-Post)**: The candidate with the highest number of votes in a constituency wins.
- **Election Commission**: An autonomous constitutional authority responsible for administering elections in India.
- **Model Code of Conduct**: A set of guidelines issued by the Election Commission for political parties and candidates during elections.
- **Booth Capturing**: Illegally taking control of a polling booth and casting fraudulent votes.
- **NOTA**: "None of the Above" -- an option allowing voters to reject all candidates.
- **VVPAT**: Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail -- provides physical verification of the vote cast electronically.
