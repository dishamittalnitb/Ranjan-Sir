# Chapter 22: Nationalism in India

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Illustrate various facets of Nationalistic movements that ushered in the sense of Collective Belonging.
- Evaluate the effectiveness of the strategies applied by Gandhiji and other leaders in the movements organised by him.
- Summarise the effects of the First World War that triggered the two defining movements (Khilafat & Non-Cooperation Movement) in India.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Impact of World War I on India**: Increased taxes, price rise, forced recruitment, crop failures, and famine (1918-19) fueled discontent. The Rowlatt Act (1919) allowed detention without trial, leading to nationwide protests.
2. **Gandhiji's Entry and Satyagraha**: Mahatma Gandhi returned from South Africa (1915) and introduced Satyagraha -- non-violent resistance. First satyagraha movements in Champaran (1917), Kheda (1917), and Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918).
3. **Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919)**: General Dyer ordered firing on peaceful gathering in Amritsar; hundreds killed. Outraged the nation.
4. **Khilafat and Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22)**:
   - Khilafat Movement: Indian Muslims protested against the British treatment of the Turkish Khalifa.
   - Gandhiji combined Khilafat with Non-Cooperation: boycott of British institutions, law courts, schools, elections, foreign goods. Congress session at Nagpur (1920) ratified the programme.
   - Withdrawn after Chauri Chaura incident (1922) when protest turned violent in Gorakhpur, UP.
5. **Civil Disobedience Movement (1930-34)**:
   - Started with Gandhiji's Dandi March (Salt March, 1930) -- 240 miles from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi.
   - Broke salt law; led to mass civil disobedience across India.
   - Boycott of foreign cloth, liquor; women participated widely.
   - Withdrawn and resumed intermittently; ended by 1934.
6. **Differing Strands within the Movement**:
   - Swaraj Party (CR Das, Motilal Nehru): wanted to enter councils.
   - Revolutionaries: Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru.
   - Dalit movement: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar -- demanded separate electorates; Poona Pact (1932) provided reserved seats.
   - Muslim League: led by Muhammed Ali Jinnah; sought separate electorates and later a separate nation.
7. **Sense of Collective Belonging**: Through cultural processes -- national flag, national song (Vande Mataram), revival of folklore, reinterpretation of history, and symbols like Bharat Mata.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Satyagraha**: Gandhiji's method of non-violent resistance based on truth and moral force.
- **Rowlatt Act (1919)**: A repressive law allowing the British government to imprison anyone without trial.
- **Khilafat Movement**: An Indian Muslim movement supporting the Ottoman Sultan's position as Khalifa.
- **Non-Cooperation Movement**: A mass movement led by Gandhiji asking Indians to withdraw cooperation from the British.
- **Civil Disobedience**: The refusal to comply with certain laws as a form of peaceful political protest.
- **Poona Pact (1932)**: An agreement between Gandhiji and Dr. Ambedkar providing reserved seats for Depressed Classes.
- **Separate Electorates**: A system where candidates are elected by and from a specific community only.
- **Simon Commission (1928)**: A British commission to review India's constitutional progress; boycotted because it had no Indian member.
