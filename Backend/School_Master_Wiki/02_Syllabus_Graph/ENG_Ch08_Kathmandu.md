# Chapter 08: Kathmandu

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** This travelogue by Vikram Seth captures the vibrant, chaotic, and spiritual essence of Kathmandu, Nepal's capital city. It explores contrasts — life and death, sacred and profane, serenity and bustle — and reflects on the universal human search for meaning through travel and cultural immersion.

**Summary:** Vikram Seth describes his visit to Kathmandu, focusing on two main landmarks: the Pashupatinath Temple (dedicated to Lord Shiva) and the Baudhnath Stupa (a Buddhist shrine). At Pashupatinath, the atmosphere is one of noisy chaos — priests, hawkers, devotees, tourists, cows, monkeys, and pigeons all mingle. Only Hindus are allowed inside the temple. A group of saffron-clad Westerners struggle for entrance. The river Bagmati flows nearby, polluted by offerings and ashes of cremation. In contrast, Baudhnath Stupa exudes calm and stillness; Tibetan Buddhist monks chant and worshippers walk in silence. Seth roams the bustling market streets of Kathmandu — narrow alleys filled with flutes, postcards, cosmetics, copper pots, and Buddhist rosaries. He is particularly drawn to a flute seller, whose repetitive, meditative playing captivates him. The chapter ends with Seth's decision to leave by train and bus back to Delhi, with the image of the flute lingering in his mind.

## Character List

This is a first-person travelogue; no fictional characters. The narrator is **Vikram Seth** himself. Notable figures described include:
- The flute seller near the hotel, playing his instrument meditatively.
- Priests, tourists, hawkers, and monks at the shrines.
- Westerners in saffron robes at Pashupatinath.

## Key Quotes

1. "At Pashupatinath there is an atmosphere of 'febrile confusion'."
2. "All this while, a small shrine half protrudes from the stone platform on the river bank."
3. "This is a haven of quietness in the busy streets around."
4. "In his hand was a pole with an attachment at the top... and on it fifty or sixty flutes."
5. "To hear any flute is to be drawn into the commonality of all mankind."
