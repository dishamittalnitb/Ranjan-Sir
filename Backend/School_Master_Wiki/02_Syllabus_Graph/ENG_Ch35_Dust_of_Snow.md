# Chapter 35: Dust of Snow

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The poem illustrates how a small, seemingly insignificant event in nature can transform a person's mood and outlook. It highlights the healing power of nature and the idea that hope and joy can be found in unexpected, tiny moments.

**Summary:** The poem is a brief eight-line lyric. The speaker describes standing under a hemlock tree when a crow shakes down a "dust of snow" upon him. This simple, mundane action "has given my heart / A change of mood" and saved "some part / Of a day I had rued." In other words, a moment of natural beauty — snow falling from a tree — lifted the poet's spirits and prevented the rest of the day from being ruined.

## Literary Devices

- **Rhyme Scheme:** ABAB CDCD (alternating rhyme in two quatrains).
- **Alliteration:** "shook down on me," "saved some part"
- **Symbolism:** "Crow" (often a bad omen/symbol of doom), "Hemlock tree" (associated with poison/Socrates' death) — both negative symbols produce a positive effect, subverting expectations. "Dust of snow" = small positive change.
- **Metaphor:** "Dust of snow" = a momentary, light, transformative experience.
- **Tone:** Melancholic at start, shifting to a quiet, grateful uplift.

## Key Quotes

1. "The way a crow / Shook down on me / The dust of snow / From a hemlock tree"
2. "Has given my heart / A change of mood"
3. "And saved some part / Of a day I had rued."
