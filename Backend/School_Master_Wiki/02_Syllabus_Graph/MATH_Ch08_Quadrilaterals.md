# Chapter 08: Quadrilaterals (Class IX)

## Concept Summary

This chapter studies the properties and classification of quadrilaterals, particularly parallelograms:

1. **Definitions**: Quadrilateral, sides, vertices, diagonals. Types: parallelogram, rectangle, square, rhombus, trapezium, kite.

2. **Diagonal Property** (provable): A diagonal of a parallelogram divides it into two congruent triangles.

3. **Opposite Sides** (stated without proof): In a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal. (Converse also true.)

4. **Opposite Angles** (stated without proof): In a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal. (Converse also true.)

5. **Parallelogram Criterion** (stated without proof): A quadrilateral is a parallelogram if a pair of its opposite sides is parallel and equal.

6. **Diagonals Bisect** (stated without proof): In a parallelogram, the diagonals bisect each other. (Converse also true.)

7. **Mid-Point Theorem** (stated without proof): In a triangle, the line segment joining the mid-points of any two sides is parallel to the third side and is half of it. (Converse also true.)

## Key Formulas/Theorems

**Properties of a Parallelogram**:
1. A diagonal divides it into two congruent triangles.
2. Opposite sides are equal.
3. Opposite angles are equal.
4. Diagonals bisect each other.

**Special Quadrilaterals**:
- **Rectangle**: A parallelogram with all angles 90 degrees. Diagonals are equal.
- **Rhombus**: A parallelogram with all sides equal. Diagonals are perpendicular bisectors.
- **Square**: A parallelogram with all sides equal and all angles 90 degrees.

**Mid-Point Theorem**: In triangle ABC, if D and E are mid-points of AB and AC, then DE || BC and DE = (1/2)BC.
