# Chapter 28: Two Stories About Flying

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** This chapter contains two stories, both about flying but with different core messages. Part I ("His First Flight") deals with overcoming fear through necessity and instinct — the moment a young seagull learns to trust its wings. Part II ("The Black Aeroplane") is a mysterious, surreal story about a pilot guided to safety by a phantom plane, exploring themes of faith, the unknown, and divine intervention.

**Summary (Part I - His First Flight):** A young seagull sits alone on a ledge, afraid to fly, while his parents and siblings have already taken wing and are fishing in the sea below. Despite their encouragement and taunting, he cannot overcome his fear. Finally, his mother flies toward him with a fish but does not come close enough — hunger drives him to dive at the fish, and in that moment he discovers he can fly. He soars joyfully over the ocean with his family.

**Summary (Part II - The Black Aeroplane):** A pilot is flying his Dakota aircraft from Paris to England on a clear night. Suddenly, he encounters an enormous storm cloud. Taking a risk, he flies into the cloud, where his instruments fail and he loses all radio contact. In the darkness, he sees another black aeroplane flying nearby with no lights on its wings. The mysterious pilot waves at him and guides him through the storm to a safe landing at a runway. When the pilot asks the control tower about the other plane, they tell him there was no other aircraft in the sky that night.

## Character List

- **Young Seagull:** The protagonist of Part I; timid and afraid to fly but finally overcomes his fear.
- **Mother Seagull:** Uses hunger as motivation to push her chick to fly.
- **The Pilot (Narrator of Part II):** A Dakota pilot who encounters an impossible situation and is mysteriously guided to safety.
- **The Mysterious Pilot:** An enigmatic figure in the black aeroplane who guides the narrator through the storm; likely a supernatural or divine presence.

## Key Quotes

1. "He felt certain that his wings would never support him."
2. "With a loud scream he fell outwards and downwards into space."
3. "I saw the runway... and I landed and was safe."
4. "No other aeroplanes were flying tonight."
