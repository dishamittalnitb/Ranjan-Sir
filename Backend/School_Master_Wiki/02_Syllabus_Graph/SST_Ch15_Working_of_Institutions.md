# Chapter 15: Working of Institutions

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Examine the roles, responsibilities, and interdependency of all the 3 organs of the Government.
- Appreciate the parliamentary system of executive's accountability to the legislature.
- Summarise and evaluate the rule of law in India.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Three Organs of Government**:
   - **Legislature**: Makes laws (Parliament at the centre, State Legislatures at the state level).
   - **Executive**: Implements and enforces laws (President, Prime Minister & Council of Ministers, Bureaucracy).
   - **Judiciary**: Interprets laws and resolves disputes (Supreme Court, High Courts, District Courts).
2. **Parliament**: The supreme legislative body of India. Consists of the President, Lok Sabha (House of the People), and Rajya Sabha (Council of States).
   - **Lok Sabha**: Directly elected by people; maximum 552 members; term of 5 years. The more powerful house in money matters and controlling the executive.
   - **Rajya Sabha**: Elected by state legislative assemblies; maximum 250 members; permanent house with 1/3rd retiring every 2 years.
3. **The Executive**:
   - **Political Executive**: Elected for a specific period (President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers). Answerable to the people.
   - **Permanent Executive (Civil Services/Bureaucracy)**: Career officials who remain in office regardless of government changes.
   - **President of India**: Head of the State, nominal executive. Appoints the Prime Minister. Has emergency powers.
   - **Prime Minister**: Head of the government, real executive. Leads the Council of Ministers.
4. **Parliamentary System**: The executive (Council of Ministers) is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. If the government loses a confidence vote, it must resign. This ensures executive accountability.
5. **The Judiciary**:
   - Independent and integrated judicial system.
   - **Supreme Court**: Apex court; original and appellate jurisdiction; guardian of the Constitution.
   - **Judicial Review**: Power to declare laws unconstitutional if they violate the Constitution.
   - **Public Interest Litigation (PIL)**: A mechanism allowing any public-spirited person to approach the court for the enforcement of fundamental rights.
6. **Checks and Balances**: Each organ of government has checks on the others to prevent misuse of power. The legislature controls the executive; the judiciary checks both.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Legislature**: The law-making body of the government.
- **Executive**: The branch of government responsible for implementing laws.
- **Judiciary**: The system of courts that interprets and applies laws.
- **Lok Sabha (House of the People)**: The lower house of Parliament, directly elected by the people.
- **Rajya Sabha (Council of States)**: The upper house of Parliament, representing the states.
- **Parliamentary System**: A system where the executive is responsible to the legislature.
- **Judicial Review**: The power of the judiciary to examine the constitutionality of laws and executive orders.
- **PIL (Public Interest Litigation)**: Legal action initiated in court for the enforcement of public interest.
- **Collective Responsibility**: The principle that the entire Council of Ministers is responsible to the Lok Sabha.
- **Impeachment**: The process of removing the President or judges for violation of the Constitution.
