# Chapter 38: Development

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Enumerate and examine the different processes involved in setting developmental Goals.
- Analyse and infer how the per capita income depicts the economic condition of the nation.
- Evaluate the development goals with reference to their efficacy, implemental strategies, relevance to current requirements of the nation.
- Compare the per capita income of some countries and infer reasons for the variance.
- Analyse the multiple perspectives on the need of development.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **What is Development?**: Development means improvement in the quality of life and living standards. It includes higher income, better education and health, reduced poverty, and greater freedom.
2. **Different People, Different Goals**:
   - Development goals vary: a farmer wants better irrigation and MSP; a landless labourer wants regular work and higher wages; an industrialist wants infrastructure and cheap power.
   - Development is multi-dimensional and what is development for one may be destructive for another.
3. **Income and Other Goals**:
   - Income is a key measure of development, but not the only one.
   - People also desire non-material goals: equal treatment, freedom, security, respect, clean environment.
   - A job may give high income but without security or family time -- it may not be considered developmental.
4. **National Development**: Measuring a country's development is complex. Common indicators include:
   - **Per Capita Income**: Average income per person (total national income / population).
   - **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)**: Total value of goods and services produced within a country.
   - **HDI (Human Development Index)**: Combines per capita income, life expectancy, and literacy (developed by UNDP).
   - **BMI (Body Mass Index)**: Indicator of nutritional status.
   - **Infant Mortality Rate (IMR)**: Number of infant deaths per 1000 live births.
   - **Literacy Rate** and **Net Attendance Ratio** for education.
5. **Comparing Countries and States**:
   - Per capita income criteria: World Bank classifies countries as high-income, upper-middle-income, lower-middle-income, and low-income countries.
   - India is classified as a lower-middle-income country.
   - Within India, Kerala has higher HDI (better health and education) despite lower per capita income than Punjab or Haryana.
6. **Sustainability of Development**: Development that degrades the environment is not sustainable. Overuse of groundwater, pollution, and deforestation have long-term costs. Sustainable development is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Development**: The process of economic and social transformation to improve quality of life.
- **Per Capita Income**: Total national income divided by total population.
- **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)**: The total monetary value of all goods and services produced within a country in one year.
- **HDI (Human Development Index)**: A composite index measuring average achievement in health, education, and income.
- **IMR (Infant Mortality Rate)**: Number of deaths of infants under 1 year per 1,000 live births.
- **Literacy Rate**: Proportion of the population aged 7+ that can read and write.
- **Net Attendance Ratio**: Percentage of children of official school age attending school.
- **BMI (Body Mass Index)**: A measure of body fat based on height and weight, used to assess undernourishment.
- **Sustainable Development**: Development that meets present needs without compromising the ability of future generations.
- **World Bank**: An international financial institution that provides loans and grants for development.
