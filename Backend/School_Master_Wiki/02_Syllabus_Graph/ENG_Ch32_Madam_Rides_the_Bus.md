# Chapter 32: Madam Rides the Bus

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The story explores a young girl's innocent curiosity, her desire for independence and adventure, and her first confrontation with the reality of death. It beautifully captures the contrast between the wide-eyed wonder of childhood and the sobering truths of adult life.

**Summary:** Valli, an eight-year-old girl living in a village, is fascinated by the bus that passes through her street daily. She saves every coin she finds, forgoing peppermints, toys, and even a ride on the merry-go-round to accumulate sixty paise — the fare for a bus ride to the town and back. One afternoon, while her mother naps, she boards the bus alone. She enjoys every moment — the sights on either side, a young cow running ahead of the bus, the chatter of fellow passengers. In town, she refuses to get down, afraid to explore alone. On the return trip, she sees the same young cow lying dead on the road after an accident. The sight deeply affects her; she becomes quiet and thoughtful. Back home, she later reflects that what's meant to happen, happens — showing a newfound maturity. Her mother and aunt remain unaware of her secret adventure.

## Character List

- **Valli (Valliammai):** An 8-year-old girl; curious, determined, independent, and thrifty; embarks on a secret bus journey.
- **The Bus Conductor:** A kind, jovial man who notices Valli is traveling alone and treats her with gentle amusement.
- **Valli's Mother:** Unaware of Valli's adventure; represents the protected domestic world.
- **Valli's Aunt:** Minor character; conversation partner for Valli's mother.

## Key Quotes

1. "The most fascinating thing of all was the bus."
2. "Valli would stare wistfully at the people who got on and off the bus."
3. "She had saved sixty paise — the exact fare."
4. "The cow was lying sprawled in a pool of blood, dead."
5. "What's meant to happen, happens."
