# Chapter 41: The Trees

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The poem is a powerful allegory for liberation — both of nature and of the self. It describes trees breaking free from their confinement within a house and returning to the forest, symbolizing the breaking of boundaries, rebellion against confinement, and the unstoppable force of nature reclaiming its rightful place.

**Summary:** The poet describes the trees that have been kept inside a house (as potted plants or decorations). At night, these trees struggle to break free — their roots work to disengage from the floor, their leaves strain toward the glass, and branches shuffle under the roof. The trees "move out" into the forest, which has meanwhile been "empty" all day without them. As the trees leave, the poet notes that she sits inside, writing "long letters," barely noticing the "moon" — which she describes as "broken like a mirror" reflecting the escaping trees. The poem ends with a quiet, almost eerie acceptance of the trees' departure, emphasizing the silence after the escape.

## Literary Devices

- **Rhyme Scheme:** Free verse — no fixed rhyme scheme; the poem relies on imagery and rhythm.
- **Personification:** The trees are personified as conscious beings struggling to escape; "the trees are stumbling forward into the night."
- **Metaphor:** Trees = oppressed beings or suppressed aspects of nature/self seeking freedom. The house = artificial confinement/civilization. The forest = natural freedom.
- **Imagery:** "small twigs stiff with exertion," "long cramped boughs," "moon is broken like a mirror."
- **Simile:** "The moon is broken like a mirror."
- **Symbolism:** The empty forest symbolizes a natural world robbed of its essence; the departing trees symbolize liberation and return to authenticity.
- **Tone:** Urgent, restless, liberating, meditative.

## Key Quotes

1. "The trees inside are moving out into the forest, / the forest that was empty all these days."
2. "All night the roots work / to disengage themselves from the cracks / in the veranda floor."
3. "The trees are stumbling forward / into the night."
4. "The moon is broken like a mirror, / its pieces flash now in the crown / of the tallest oak."
