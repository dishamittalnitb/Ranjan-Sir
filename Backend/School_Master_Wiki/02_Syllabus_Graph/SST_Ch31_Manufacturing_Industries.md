# Chapter 31: Manufacturing Industries

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Enumerate the impact of manufacturing industries on the environment and develop strategies for sustainable development of the manufacturing sector.
- Differentiate between various types of manufacturing industries based on their input materials, processes, and end products, and analyse their significance in the Indian economy.
- Analyse the relation between the availability of raw material and location of the industry.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Role of Manufacturing**: Manufacturing transforms raw materials into finished goods with higher value. It is the backbone of economic development, contributing to employment, income, and GDP (about 17% of GDP).
2. **Classification of Industries**:
   - **On the basis of raw materials**: Agro-based (textiles, sugar, jute, vegetable oil), Mineral-based (iron & steel, cement, aluminium, petrochemicals).
   - **On the basis of role**: Basic (iron & steel, aluminium) and Consumer (toothpaste, TV, automobiles).
   - **On the basis of ownership**: Public sector (BHEL, SAIL), Private sector (TISCO, Reliance), Joint sector (Oil India Ltd.), Cooperative sector (AMUL, sugar cooperatives).
   - **On the basis of size**: Large-scale and Small-scale industries.
3. **Key Industries**:
   - **Textile Industry**: Largest and oldest. Cotton textiles: mainly in Mumbai (Manchester of India), Ahmedabad, Surat, Kanpur, Coimbatore. Jute textiles: mainly in West Bengal along the Hugli river.
   - **Iron and Steel Industry**: Basic/heavy industry. Key plants: Jamshedpur (TISCO -- oldest), Bhilai (with Russian collaboration), Bokaro, Durgapur, Rourkela, Vijayanagar, Salem.
   - **Chemical and Fertilizer Industry**: Important for agriculture and industrial growth.
   - **Cement Industry**: India is the second-largest cement producer. Plants near limestone deposits.
   - **IT and Electronics**: Bengaluru is India's Silicon Valley. Software Technology Parks (STPs): Noida, Gandhinagar, Mumbai, Pune, Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Chennai, Thiruvananthapuram.
   - **Automobile Industry**: Gurugram, Chennai, Pune, Jamshedpur; significant contributor to manufacturing GDP.
4. **Industrial Location Factors**: Availability of raw materials, power, labour, market, transport, capital, and government policies influence industrial location. Industries tend to agglomerate in clusters.
5. **Environmental Degradation by Industries**:
   - Air pollution (smoke, gases like SO2, CO, NOx).
   - Water pollution (effluents, heavy metals, thermal pollution).
   - Land degradation (solid waste, chemical dumping).
   - Noise pollution.
6. **Sustainable Measures**:
   - Reduce, Reuse, Recycle (3R principle).
   - Rainwater harvesting and water recycling.
   - Use of cleaner fuels (natural gas, renewable energy).
   - Effluent treatment plants (ETPs).
   - Strict environmental laws and monitoring by State Pollution Control Boards.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Manufacturing**: The process of converting raw materials into finished goods using machines and labour.
- **Agro-based Industry**: Industries that use agricultural raw materials (e.g., textiles, sugar).
- **Mineral-based Industry**: Industries that use minerals as raw materials (e.g., iron & steel, cement).
- **Public Sector**: Industries owned and operated by the government.
- **Private Sector**: Industries owned and operated by private individuals/companies.
- **Cooperative Sector**: Industries owned and managed by cooperatives of producers/suppliers.
- **Aluminium Smelting**: The extraction of aluminium from its ore (bauxite). India is a major producer.
- **ETP (Effluent Treatment Plant)**: A facility for treating industrial wastewater before discharge.
