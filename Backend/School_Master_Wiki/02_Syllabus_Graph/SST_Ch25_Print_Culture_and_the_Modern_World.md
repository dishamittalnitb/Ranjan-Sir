# Chapter 25: Print Culture and the Modern World

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Enumerate the development of Print from its beginnings in East Asia to its expansion in Europe and India.
- Compare and contrast the old tradition of handwritten manuscripts versus print technology.
- Summarise the role of Print Revolution and its impact.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Beginnings of Print in East Asia**: Woodblock printing originated in China (about 594 AD). Japan adopted it (AD 768-770) -- the Buddhist Diamond Sutra is the oldest printed book.
2. **Print in Europe**:
   - Johann Gutenberg invented the movable-type printing press in the 1430s (Germany).
   - The Gutenberg Bible (1448) was the first major printed book.
   - Printing press spread rapidly across Europe; about 200 million books by the early 17th century.
3. **The Print Revolution**:
   - Reduced the cost and time of producing books.
   - Made reading accessible to wider audiences.
   - Spread ideas of reformers (Martin Luther's 95 Theses, 1517) and Enlightenment philosophers (Voltaire, Rousseau).
   - Facilitated the spread of scientific knowledge.
4. **Religious Debates and Print**:
   - Print challenged the authority of the Church; Protestant Reformation was fueled by printed material.
   - Counter-Reformation: Catholic Church used print for its own propaganda.
   - Fear of print led to censorship by Church and monarchs (Index of Prohibited Books, 1558).
5. **Print in India**:
   - Printing press arrived in Goa in the 1550s (Portuguese missionaries).
   - First Indian language press: Tamil (1579), Malayalam, Bengali, and other vernaculars.
   - James Augustus Hickey started "Bengal Gazette" (1780), the first Indian newspaper.
   - Gangadhar Bhattacharya published "Bengal Gazette" in Bengali.
6. **Print and Social Reform in India**:
   - Reformers like Raja Rammohan Roy ("Sambad Kaumudi") used print to debate Sati, widow remarriage, and caste issues.
   - Mirat-ul-Akhbar: Persian journal by Rammohan Roy.
   - Anti-colonial newspapers: "Kesari" (Bal Gangadhar Tilak), "The Hindu", "Amrita Bazar Patrika".
7. **Print and Nationalism**: Vernacular press connected Indians across regions, promoted nationalism, and criticized colonial policies. The Vernacular Press Act (1878) was used by the British to censor Indian-language newspapers, but was opposed and later repealed.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Woodblock Printing**: A technique where text or images are carved into a block of wood, inked, and pressed onto paper.
- **Movable Type**: Individual metal characters that can be rearranged to print different texts.
- **Print Revolution**: The dramatic change brought about by the invention and spread of printing technology.
- **Manuscript**: A book or document written by hand.
- **Censorship**: The suppression or prohibition of speech, writing, or other forms of communication.
- **Vernacular Press Act (1878)**: A British colonial law restricting the publishing of newspapers in Indian languages.
- **Index of Prohibited Books**: A list of books banned by the Catholic Church.
- **Almanac**: An annual publication containing calendar, weather forecasts, and other useful information; popular early printed material.
- **Penny Magazine**: An affordable publication aimed at the working class in 19th-century Britain.
