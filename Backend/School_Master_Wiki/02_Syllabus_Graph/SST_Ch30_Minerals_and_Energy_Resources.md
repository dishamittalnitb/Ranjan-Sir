# Chapter 30: Minerals and Energy Resources

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Enumerate the impact of manufacturing industries on the environment and develop strategies for sustainable development of the manufacturing sector.
- Differentiate between various types of manufacturing industries based on their input materials, processes, and end products, and analyse their significance in the Indian economy.
- Analyse the relation between the availability of raw material and location of the industry.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Minerals**: A homogenous, naturally occurring substance with a definable internal structure. Minerals are essential for industrial development. India is rich in mineral resources.
2. **Mode of Occurrence**:
   - **Igneous and Metamorphic Rocks**: Major metallic minerals like iron ore, copper, gold.
   - **Sedimentary Rocks**: Coal, petroleum, limestone.
   - **Alluvial Deposits**: Placer deposits of gold, platinum, tin.
   - **Ocean Waters**: Salt, magnesium, bromine.
3. **Classification of Minerals**:
   - **Metallic Minerals**: Ferrous (iron ore, manganese, chromite) and Non-ferrous (copper, bauxite, lead, zinc, gold, silver). India exports substantial amounts of ferrous minerals.
   - **Non-Metallic Minerals**: Mica, limestone, gypsum; mica is used in the electrical industry, and limestone is for cement.
4. **Major Mineral Belts and Deposits**:
   - Iron Ore: Odisha-Jharkhand belt (Mayurbhanj, Keonjhar), Durg-Bastar-Chandrapur belt (Bailadila), Bellary-Chitradurga-Tumkur belt (Karnataka), Maharashtra-Goa belt.
   - Manganese: Largest reserves in Odisha, then Karnataka.
   - Bauxite: Odisha, Gujarat, Jharkhand, Maharashtra.
   - Copper: Balaghat (MP), Khetri (Rajasthan), Singhbhum (Jharkhand).
   - Mica: Jharkhand, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh.
   - Limestone: Almost all states.
5. **Energy Resources**:
   - **Conventional Sources**:
     - Coal: Most abundant fossil fuel. Major coal fields: Raniganj (WB), Jharia, Bokaro (Jharkhand), Talcher (Odisha), Neyveli (TN -- lignite).
     - Petroleum: Mumbai High, Bassien (offshore), Digboi, Naharkatiya (Assam), Ankaleshwar, Kalol (Gujarat).
     - Natural Gas: Along with petroleum and independent reservoirs; HBJ Pipeline.
     - Electricity: Thermal (coal-based), Hydroelectric, Nuclear.
   - **Non-Conventional Sources**: Solar, wind (largest wind farm cluster in Tamil Nadu), biogas, tidal, geothermal. India is aggressively expanding renewable energy capacity.
6. **Conservation of Minerals**: Minerals are non-renewable and finite. Need for recycling, use of substitutes, and efficient extraction technology. Sustainable mining practices are crucial.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Minerals**: Naturally occurring inorganic substances with a defined chemical composition and crystal structure.
- **Ore**: A naturally occurring material from which a mineral can be profitably extracted.
- **Ferrous Minerals**: Minerals containing iron.
- **Non-Ferrous Minerals**: Minerals not containing iron (copper, bauxite, etc.).
- **Rat-Hole Mining**: Local tribal mining practice in Meghalaya where narrow tunnels are dug by individuals.
- **Placer Deposits**: Accumulation of valuable minerals formed by gravity separation during sedimentary processes.
- **Lignite**: Low-grade brown coal with high moisture content, found in Neyveli (TN).
- **Thermal Power**: Power generated from heat (coal, oil, gas, nuclear).
- **Hydroelectric Power**: Electricity produced by harnessing the energy of flowing water.
- **Geothermal Energy**: Heat energy from within the earth.
