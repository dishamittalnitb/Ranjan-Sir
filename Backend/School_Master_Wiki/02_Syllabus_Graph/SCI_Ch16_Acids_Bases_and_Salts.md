# Chapter 16: Acids, Bases and Salts

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Acids
- Substances that furnish H+ ions (hydronium ions H3O+ in aqueous solution).
- **Properties:** Sour taste, turn blue litmus red, conduct electricity in aqueous solution.
- **Strong acids:** Completely ionise (HCl, H2SO4, HNO3).
- **Weak acids:** Partially ionise (CH3COOH, carbonic acid H2CO3).
- **Dilute acid:** Contains less acid per unit volume.
- **Concentrated acid:** Contains more acid per unit volume.

### 1.2 Bases
- Substances that furnish OH- ions in aqueous solution.
- **Properties:** Bitter taste, soapy feel, turn red litmus blue, conduct electricity in aqueous solution.
- **Alkali:** A water-soluble base (e.g., NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)2).
- **Strong bases:** Completely ionise (NaOH, KOH).
- **Weak bases:** Partially ionise (NH4OH, Mg(OH)2).

### 1.3 Indicators
| Indicator | Acid | Base | Neutral |
|-----------|------|------|---------|
| **Litmus** | Red | Blue | - |
| **Methyl Orange** | Red | Yellow | Orange |
| **Phenolphthalein** | Colourless | Pink | Colourless |
| **Turmeric (natural)** | Yellow (no change) | Reddish-brown | Yellow |
| **Red Cabbage** | Red/Pink | Green | Purple |

### 1.4 Chemical Properties of Acids and Bases
#### Reactions of Acids:
- Acid + Metal --> Salt + Hydrogen gas (e.g., Zn + 2HCl --> ZnCl2 + H2)
- Acid + Metal Carbonate --> Salt + Water + CO2 (e.g., Na2CO3 + 2HCl --> 2NaCl + H2O + CO2)
- Acid + Metal Hydrogen Carbonate --> Salt + Water + CO2 (e.g., NaHCO3 + HCl --> NaCl + H2O + CO2)
- Acid + Base --> Salt + Water (Neutralisation) (e.g., HCl + NaOH --> NaCl + H2O)
- Acid + Metal Oxide --> Salt + Water (e.g., CuO + 2HCl --> CuCl2 + H2O)

#### Reactions of Bases:
- Base + Metal (Al, Zn, Sn only) --> Salt + Hydrogen gas (e.g., 2NaOH + Zn --> Na2ZnO2 + H2)
- Base + Non-metallic Oxide --> Salt + Water (e.g., 2NaOH + CO2 --> Na2CO3 + H2O)

### 1.5 pH Scale
- Measures the acidic or basic nature of a solution.
- Scale: 0 to 14. **pH = 7** (neutral); **pH < 7** (acidic); **pH > 7** (basic).
- **Note from syllabus:** The definition relating to logarithm is not required.
- **Concept:** pH is inversely related to H+ ion concentration. Lower pH means higher H+ concentration.

#### Importance of pH in Everyday Life:
| Context | Relevance |
|---------|-----------|
| **Human body** | pH 7.0-7.8 (blood ~7.4). Acid rain/indigestion occurs when stomach produces too much HCl (antacids like Mg(OH)2 used). |
| **Agriculture** | Soil pH (6.5-7.5 ideal for most crops). Acidic soil treated with quicklime/slaked lime. |
| **Tooth decay** | pH < 5.5 causes tooth enamel (calcium phosphate) to decay. Bacteria produce acids from sugars. |
| **Digestive system** | HCl in stomach (pH ~1-2) aids digestion. |
| **Plant/animal stings** | Bee sting (acidic - treated with baking soda); Wasp sting (basic - treated with vinegar). |

### 1.6 Preparation and Uses of Important Salts
| Compound | Chemical Formula | Preparation | Key Uses |
|----------|-----------------|------------|----------|
| **Sodium Hydroxide (Caustic Soda)** | NaOH | Chlor-alkali process: NaCl (brine) electrolysis | Soap, detergent, paper |
| **Bleaching Powder** | CaOCl2 | Ca(OH)2 + Cl2 --> CaOCl2 + H2O | Bleaching textiles, disinfecting water, oxidising agent |
| **Baking Soda** | NaHCO3 | NaCl + H2O + CO2 + NH3 --> NH4Cl + NaHCO3 (Solvay process) | Antacid, baking (release CO2 on heating), soda-acid fire extinguisher |
| **Washing Soda** | Na2CO3.10H2O | 2NaHCO3 --heat--> Na2CO3 + H2O + CO2; then recrystallised | Glass, soap, detergent, paper, water softening |
| **Plaster of Paris** | CaSO4.1/2 H2O | CaSO4.2H2O (gypsum) --373K--> CaSO4.1/2H2O + 1.5H2O | Plaster casts for fractures, making statues, fireproofing |

### 1.7 Water of Crystallisation
- Fixed number of water molecules present in the crystal structure of a salt.
- E.g., CuSO4.5H2O (blue), FeSO4.7H2O (green), Na2CO3.10H2O (washing soda).
- On heating, the water of crystallisation is lost, and the salt becomes anhydrous (often changes colour).
