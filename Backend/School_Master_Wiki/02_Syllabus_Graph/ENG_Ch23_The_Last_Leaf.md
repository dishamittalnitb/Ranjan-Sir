# Chapter 23: The Last Leaf

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The story is a moving tribute to the power of hope, art, and selfless sacrifice. It demonstrates that genuine friendship and art can save a life. The "last leaf" becomes a symbol of hope and the will to live. The story also contains O. Henry's trademark surprise twist ending.

**Summary:** Sue and Johnsy are two young artists sharing a studio in Greenwich Village, New York. Johnsy falls ill with pneumonia during a severe winter. The doctor tells Sue that Johnsy has lost her will to live and has only one chance in ten. Johnsy has fixated on an ivy vine outside her window, believing she will die when the last leaf falls. Despite a stormy night, one leaf remains on the vine the next morning. Seeing this, Johnsy realizes she has been foolish and regains the will to live; she recovers. The story's twist is that the "last leaf" was actually a painting — old Behrman, a failed artist who lived downstairs, had braved the storm to paint the leaf on the wall, sacrificing his own life to pneumonia but giving Johnsy hope.

## Character List

- **Johnsy (Joanna):** A young artist who loses hope for life and ties her survival to the falling ivy leaves.
- **Sue:** Johnsy's devoted friend and roommate; caring and practical, tries to keep Johnsy's spirits up.
- **Behrman:** An elderly, unsuccessful painter who talks of creating a masterpiece but never does — until his final act of painting the last leaf, which becomes his true masterpiece.
- **The Doctor:** Provides medical guidance and emphasizes the importance of mental will in recovery.

## Key Quotes

1. "When the last leaf falls, I must go, too."
2. "Some day I will paint a masterpiece, and we shall all go away."
3. "It is a sin to want to die."
4. "The last leaf is still there... It never fluttered or moved... It's Behrman's masterpiece."
5. "He painted it there the night that the last leaf fell."
