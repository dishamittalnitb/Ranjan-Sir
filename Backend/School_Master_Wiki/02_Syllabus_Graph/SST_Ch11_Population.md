# Chapter 11: Population

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Analyse and infer the reasons behind the uneven distribution of population in India with specific reference to UP and Rajasthan, and Mizoram and Karnataka.
- Enlist the factors that affect the population density.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **India's Population**: According to the 2011 Census, India's population was 1.21 billion. India is the second most populous country after China, accounting for 17.5% of the world's population on just 2.4% of the world's land area.
2. **Population Distribution**: Highly uneven. Uttar Pradesh is the most populous state (about 16% of India's population in 2011). Sikkim and Lakshadweep have the smallest populations. Almost half of India's population lives in five states: UP, Maharashtra, Bihar, West Bengal, and Andhra Pradesh.
3. **Population Density**: Average density is 382 persons per sq km (2011). Bihar (1,106/sq km) has the highest density; Arunachal Pradesh (17/sq km) the lowest.
4. **Factors Affecting Population Distribution**:
   - **Physical Factors**: Relief (plains are densely populated, mountains are sparse), climate (moderate climate attracts more people), soil fertility, availability of water.
   - **Economic Factors**: Industrialization, urbanization, mineral resources, transportation networks.
   - **Social and Cultural Factors**: Historical settlements, cultural preferences, political stability.
5. **Population Growth**: India has experienced rapid growth since 1951 due to high birth rates and declining death rates (due to improved healthcare). Growth rate declined from 21.54% (1991-2001) to 17.64% (2001-2011).
6. **Age Composition**: India has a young population. Categories: Children (0-14 years: about 35%), Working Age (15-59 years: about 59%), Aged (60+ years: about 7%). A large working-age population is a "demographic dividend."
7. **Sex Ratio**: Number of females per 1000 males. India's sex ratio (2011): 940. Kerala has the highest (1084); Haryana has among the lowest. Child sex ratio (0-6 years) has declined to 919.
8. **Literacy Rate**: 74.04% overall (2011). Male literacy: 82.14%; Female literacy: 65.46%. Kerala has the highest literacy rate (94%); Bihar the lowest.
9. **Occupational Structure**: Primary (agriculture, mining: about 54.6%), Secondary (manufacturing: about 3.7%), Tertiary (services: about 41.7%). There has been a gradual shift towards tertiary sector.
10. **NPP (National Population Policy) 2000**: Aims to provide free and compulsory education up to 14 years, reduce infant mortality rate, and promote delayed marriage for girls.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Population Density**: Number of people living per unit area (usually per sq km).
- **Birth Rate**: Number of live births per thousand persons per year.
- **Death Rate**: Number of deaths per thousand persons per year.
- **Migration**: Movement of people across regions. Internal (within the country) and International (between countries).
- **Demographic Dividend**: The economic growth potential from a large proportion of working-age population.
- **Sex Ratio**: The number of females per 1000 males in a population.
- **Literacy Rate**: Percentage of population aged 7 years and above who can read and write with understanding.
- **Age Composition**: The distribution of population into different age groups.
- **Census**: An official count of population conducted every 10 years.
