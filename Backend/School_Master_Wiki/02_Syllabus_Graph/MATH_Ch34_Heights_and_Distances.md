# Chapter 34: Heights and Distances (Class X)

## Concept Summary

This chapter applies trigonometry to practical measurement problems:

1. **Angle of Elevation**: The angle between the horizontal line of sight and the line of sight to an object above the horizontal (looking up).

2. **Angle of Depression**: The angle between the horizontal line of sight and the line of sight to an object below the horizontal (looking down).

3. **Problem Solving Method**:
   - Draw a clear diagram labeling known and unknown quantities
   - Identify right triangles
   - Apply appropriate trigonometric ratios (sin, cos, tan)
   - Problems should not involve more than two right triangles
   - Angles of elevation/depression should be 30 degrees, 45 degrees, and 60 degrees only

4. **Typical Problems**:
   - Height of a building/tower/tree
   - Distance from an object
   - Length of a shadow
   - Height of a pole
   - Two-angle problems (observing from two different positions)

## Key Formulas/Theorems

**Trigonometric Ratios Used**:
- tan A = Opposite / Adjacent (most commonly used)
- sin A = Opposite / Hypotenuse
- cos A = Adjacent / Hypotenuse

**Standard Angle Values** (30, 45, 60 degrees):

| Angle | sin | cos | tan |
|-------|-----|-----|-----|
| 30 deg | 1/2 | sqrt(3)/2 | 1/sqrt(3) |
| 45 deg | 1/sqrt(2) | 1/sqrt(2) | 1 |
| 60 deg | sqrt(3)/2 | 1/2 | sqrt(3) |
