# Chapter 19: The Adventures of Toto

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** This humorous story by Ruskin Bond celebrates the playful, mischievous, and endearing nature of animals, specifically monkeys. It highlights the chaos and joy a pet can bring into a household, and subtly comments on the idea that some creatures simply cannot be domesticated.

**Summary:** The narrator's grandfather, a great animal lover, buys a baby monkey named Toto from a tonga-driver for five rupees. Toto is kept secretly from the narrator's grandmother, first in a closet tied to a peg, then in a servant's room. Toto's tail serves as his third hand, and he wreaks havoc everywhere — tearing wall plaster, breaking dishes, tormenting the family donkey, taking baths in boiling water (almost scalding himself), and eating a whole dish of pulao meant for the family. Eventually, Toto's mischief becomes unbearable. Grandfather is forced to sell him back to the same tonga-driver for three rupees.

## Character List

- **Toto:** A mischievous, clever, and destructive baby monkey with a bright, mischievous smile and a useful tail.
- **Grandfather:** An animal lover who collects pets; buys Toto but ultimately has to part with him.
- **The Narrator (Ruskin Bond):** A boy who observes and recounts Toto's antics.
- **Grandmother:** Always opposed to Grandfather's pet collection; represents order in the household.

## Key Quotes

1. "Toto was a pretty monkey. His eyes sparkled with mischief."
2. "He would have torn the peg out of the wall if he had been left there much longer."
3. "He was obviously intent on boiling himself alive."
4. "Toto threw the empty dish at the grandmother."
