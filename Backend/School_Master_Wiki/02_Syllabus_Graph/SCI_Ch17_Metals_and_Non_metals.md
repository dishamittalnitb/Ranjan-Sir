# Chapter 17: Metals and Non-metals

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Physical Properties of Metals and Non-metals
| Property | Metals | Non-metals |
|----------|--------|------------|
| **Lustre** | Shiny (lustrous) | Dull (non-lustrous, except iodine and graphite) |
| **Malleability** | Malleable (can be beaten into sheets) | Non-malleable (brittle) |
| **Ductility** | Ductile (can be drawn into wires) | Non-ductile |
| **Conductivity (heat/electricity)** | Good conductors | Poor conductors (except graphite) |
| **Sonorosity** | Sonorous (produce sound when struck) | Non-sonorous |
| **State at room temperature** | Mostly solids (except Mercury - liquid, Gallium/Cesium - low MP) | Solids (C, S, P, I), Liquid (Br), Gases (O, N, H, Cl) |
| **Tensile strength** | High | Low |

### 1.2 Chemical Properties of Metals
- **Reaction with oxygen:** Metals form basic oxides (e.g., 4Na + O2 --> 2Na2O; 4Al + 3O2 --> 2Al2O3). Some form amphoteric oxides (Al2O3, ZnO).
  - Non-metals form acidic or neutral oxides (e.g., C + O2 --> CO2; S + O2 --> SO2).
- **Reaction with water:** Reactive metals (Na, K) react vigorously with cold water (2Na + 2H2O --> 2NaOH + H2); Mg reacts with hot water; Al, Zn, Fe react with steam.
- **Reaction with acids:** Metal + Dilute Acid --> Salt + H2 (e.g., Zn + 2HCl --> ZnCl2 + H2). Cu, Ag, Hg, Au do NOT react with dilute acids.
- **Reaction with salt solutions:** More reactive metal displaces less reactive metal from its salt solution (e.g., Fe + CuSO4 --> FeSO4 + Cu).

### 1.3 Reactivity Series (Activity Series)
Metals arranged in decreasing order of reactivity:

K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au

(Mnemonic: Kangaroo Na Ca Mg Al Zoo Fe Pb H Cu Hg Ag Au - Potassium, Sodium, Calcium...)

- Highly reactive (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al): React vigorously with oxygen/water.
- Moderately reactive (Zn, Fe, Pb): React slowly.
- Less reactive (Cu, Hg, Ag, Au): Do not displace H from acids.

### 1.4 Ionic Compounds
- Formed by transfer of electrons from metal to non-metal atoms.
- **Formation:** Metal loses electron(s) --> Cation (+). Non-metal gains electron(s) --> Anion (-). Electrostatic attraction holds ions together.
- **Properties:**
  - Crystalline solids at room temperature.
  - High melting and boiling points (strong electrostatic forces).
  - Soluble in water but NOT in organic solvents (e.g., kerosene).
  - Conduct electricity in molten/aqueous state (free ions), but NOT in solid state.
  - e.g., NaCl (sodium chloride), MgCl2 (magnesium chloride), CaO (calcium oxide).

### 1.5 Basic Metallurgical Processes
The steps involved in the extraction of metals from their ores:

| Step | Description | Example (for Zn) |
|------|------------|-----------------|
| **1. Concentration of ore** | Removal of gangue (impurities) from the ore. Methods: gravity separation, magnetic separation, froth flotation. | ZnS (zinc blende) concentrated by froth flotation. |
| **2. Extraction of metal** | Converting concentrated ore into metal oxide (roasting/calcination) and then reducing to impure metal. | 2ZnS + 3O2 --roasting--> 2ZnO + 2SO2; ZnO + C --reduction--> Zn + CO |
| **3. Refining (purification)** | Obtaining pure metal from impure metal. Method: Electrolytic refining. | Impure Zn refined by electrolysis. |

**Key terms:**
- **Ore:** Mineral from which a metal can be extracted economically.
- **Gangue:** Unwanted earthy/rocky impurities in an ore.
- **Roasting:** Heating ore in presence of excess air (for sulphide ores).
- **Calcination:** Heating ore in absence/limited supply of air (for carbonate ores).
- **Flux:** Substance added to remove gangue as slag (e.g., CaO for acidic impurities).
- **Thermite reaction:** Highly exothermic reaction of Al with metal oxides (Fe2O3 + 2Al --> Al2O3 + 2Fe + Heat). Used for welding railway tracks.

### 1.6 Corrosion and Its Prevention
- **Corrosion:** Slow degradation of metals due to reaction with environment (air, moisture, acids).
  - **Rusting of Iron:** 4Fe + 3O2 + 2xH2O --> 2Fe2O3.xH2O (hydrated ferric oxide, reddish-brown).
  - **Tarnishing of Silver:** 2Ag + H2S --> Ag2S + H2 (black coating).
  - **Green coating on Copper:** 2Cu + CO2 + O2 + H2O --> CuCO3.Cu(OH)2 (basic copper carbonate).

- **Prevention Methods:**
  - **Painting / Oiling / Greasing:** Covers surface to prevent contact with air/moisture.
  - **Galvanisation:** Coating iron with zinc (zinc acts as sacrificial metal). E.g., galvanised iron pipes.
  - **Electroplating:** Coating with less reactive metal (e.g., chromium, nickel, tin).
  - **Alloying:** Mixing metals to improve corrosion resistance (e.g., stainless steel = Fe + Cr + Ni).
  - **Sacrificial protection:** Connecting to a more reactive metal (e.g., Mg block attached to ship hull).

### 1.7 Alloys
- Homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or a metal and a non-metal.
- Examples: Brass (Cu + Zn), Bronze (Cu + Sn), Solder (Pb + Sn), Stainless Steel (Fe + Cr + Ni + C), Amalgam (Hg + any metal).

## 2. Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Malleability** | Ability to be beaten into thin sheets. |
| **Ductility** | Ability to be drawn into wires. |
| **Reactivity Series** | Arrangement of metals in decreasing order of reactivity. |
| **Ionic Bond** | Electrostatic attraction between cation and anion formed by transfer of electrons. |
| **Ore** | Naturally occurring mineral from which metal can be extracted profitably. |
| **Gangue** | Earthy impurities associated with an ore. |
| **Roasting** | Heating sulphide ore in presence of air. |
| **Calcination** | Heating carbonate ore in absence/limited air. |
| **Corrosion** | Gradual destruction of metals by reaction with the environment. |
| **Galvanisation** | Coating iron with zinc to prevent rusting. |
| **Alloy** | Homogeneous mixture of metals (or metal + non-metal). |
