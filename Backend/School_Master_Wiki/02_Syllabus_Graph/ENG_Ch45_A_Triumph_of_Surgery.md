# Chapter 45: A Triumph of Surgery

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The story humorously illustrates the dangers of overindulgence and the importance of a disciplined, healthy lifestyle — for both pets and people. It emphasizes that love should not translate into harmful pampering and that sometimes "tough love" is the most caring act.

**Summary:** Tricki, a small dog, is pampered and overfed by his wealthy mistress, Mrs. Pumphrey. He becomes hugely fat, listless, and refuses to eat his regular meals. The veterinary surgeon, Mr. Herriot, realizes Tricki's only illness is gluttony. He takes Tricki to his surgery, where he puts him on a strict regimen — no food for two days, only water, then controlled meals and lots of exercise with other dogs. Tricki recovers rapidly, running and playing joyfully. Mrs. Pumphrey, believing Tricki is receiving advanced surgical care, sends eggs, wine, and brandy as "supplements." Herriot and his partners happily consume the gifts. When Mrs. Pumphrey collects a transformed, healthy Tricki, she tearfully thanks Herriot for "a triumph of surgery" — not realizing there was no operation at all.

## Character List

- **Mr. Herriot (Narrator):** A sensible veterinary surgeon who uses a no-nonsense approach to cure Tricki.
- **Mrs. Pumphrey:** Tricki's wealthy, overindulgent mistress who "loves" her dog to the point of harming him.
- **Tricki:** A small, greedy dog who recovers through diet and exercise, not medicine.
- **Tristan & Other Dogs:** Herriot's assistant and the household dogs who help Tricki socialize and exercise.

## Key Quotes

1. "He had become hugely fat, like a bloated sausage with a leg at each corner."
2. "Tricki's only fault was greed."
3. "I could see that the real cure would be to get him out of the house for a period."
4. "I think I know a cure for you, old lad."
5. "A triumph of surgery!"
