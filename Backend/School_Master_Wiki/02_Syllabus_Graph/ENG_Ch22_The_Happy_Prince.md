# Chapter 22: The Happy Prince

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The story is a poignant tale about compassion, selfless love, and the true meaning of happiness. It contrasts the superficial beauty of wealth and status with the deeper beauty of sacrifice and charity. The story also critiques social inequality and the indifference of the powerful to the suffering of the poor.

**Summary:** The statue of the Happy Prince stands high above the city, gilded in gold leaf with sapphire eyes and a ruby on his sword hilt. He appears beautiful and happy but is now sad, seeing the misery and poverty of the city's people. A little swallow, delayed on his migration to Egypt, takes shelter at the prince's feet. The prince asks the swallow to help the needy: first, he gives his ruby to a poor seamstress whose son is ill; then, one sapphire eye to a young playwright starving in a garret; the other sapphire eye to a match-girl who has lost her matches. The swallow, though increasingly cold, stays to distribute the prince's gold leaf to the poor. Eventually, winter kills the swallow at the prince's feet. The prince's now-leaden, shabby heart breaks. The mayor and town councillors, seeing the ugly statue, tear it down. In heaven, God sends an angel to bring the two most precious things in the city — the swallow's body and the prince's broken leaden heart.

## Character List

- **The Happy Prince (Statue):** A gilded statue who, once ignorant of suffering in life, now sees the city's misery and sacrifices all his beauty to help the poor.
- **The Swallow:** A brave, loyal bird who delays migration to help the prince, ultimately dying from cold.
- **The Mayor and Town Councillors:** Selfish, shallow officials who care only about appearances.
- **The Poor (Seamstress, Playwright, Match-girl):** Represent the suffering masses in the city.
- **God / Angel:** Divine acknowledgment of the prince and swallow's sacrifices.

## Key Quotes

1. "I am covered with fine gold. You must take it off, leaf by leaf, and give it to my poor."
2. "It is curious, but I feel quite warm now, although it is so cold."
3. "You have chosen rightly, for in my garden of Paradise this little bird shall sing for evermore."
4. "Dear Prince, I must leave you, but I will never forget you."
5. "Swallow, Swallow, little Swallow... stay with me one night longer."
