# Chapter 18: Carbon and its Compounds

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Versatile Nature of Carbon
Carbon forms a very large number of compounds due to:
- **Catenation:** The ability to form bonds with other carbon atoms, forming long chains, branched chains, and rings.
- **Tetravalency:** Carbon has 4 valence electrons, allowing it to form 4 covalent bonds.
- **Ability to form multiple bonds:** Carbon can form single (C-C), double (C=C), and triple (CtripleC) bonds.
- **Small size:** Carbon atom is small, enabling it to bond with many elements.

### 1.2 Covalent Bonding
- Formed by sharing of electrons between atoms (typically between non-metals).
- Each atom contributes one or more electrons to the shared pair.
- **Properties of covalent compounds:**
  - Usually liquids or gases at room temperature (or soft solids).
  - Low melting and boiling points (weak intermolecular forces).
  - Do NOT conduct electricity (no ions).
  - Generally insoluble in water, soluble in organic solvents.

### 1.3 Allotropes of Carbon
| Allotrope | Structure | Properties |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| **Diamond** | Each C bonded to 4 other C (tetrahedral, 3D network) | Hardest natural substance; high MP; non-conductor; transparent |
| **Graphite** | Each C bonded to 3 other C (hexagonal, layered); one free electron per atom | Soft and slippery; conducts electricity; used in pencils, electrodes |
| **Fullerenes** | C60 (buckminsterfullerene); cage-like molecules (soccer ball shape) | Research applications in nanotechnology |

### 1.4 Hydrocarbons
Compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen.

| Type | Bonding | General Formula | Suffix | Example |
|------|---------|----------------|--------|---------|
| **Alkanes (Saturated)** | All single bonds (C-C) | CnH2n+2 | -ane | Methane (CH4), Ethane (C2H6), Propane (C3H8) |
| **Alkenes (Unsaturated)** | At least one double bond (C=C) | CnH2n | -ene | Ethene (C2H4), Propene (C3H6) |
| **Alkynes (Unsaturated)** | At least one triple bond (CtripleC) | CnH2n-2 | -yne | Ethyne (C2H2), Propyne (C3H4) |

### 1.5 Homologous Series
A series of organic compounds having the same functional group, same general formula, and similar chemical properties, with successive members differing by -CH2- group.

**Characteristics:**
- Same functional group.
- Same general formula.
- Each member differs by -CH2- (molecular mass difference of 14 u).
- Gradation in physical properties (MP, BP increase with molecular mass).
- Similar chemical properties.

### 1.6 Nomenclature of Carbon Compounds
Follows IUPAC rules:
1. Identify the longest continuous carbon chain (parent chain).
2. Number the chain to give the functional group/substituent the lowest number.
3. Name the substituents (side groups) alphabetically.
4. Suffix indicates the functional group.

#### Functional Groups (as listed in syllabus):
| Functional Group | Name Prefix/Suffix | Example |
|-----------------|-------------------|---------|
| **Halogen (-X: -Cl, -Br, -I)** | Chloro-, Bromo-, Iodo- | Chloromethane (CH3Cl) |
| **Alcohol (-OH)** | -ol | Methanol (CH3OH), Ethanol (C2H5OH) |
| **Aldehyde (-CHO)** | -al | Methanal (HCHO), Ethanal (CH3CHO) |
| **Ketone (-CO-)** | -one | Propanone (CH3COCH3) |

### 1.7 Chemical Properties of Carbon Compounds
| Reaction Type | Description | Example |
|--------------|-------------|---------|
| **Combustion** | Burning in oxygen; produces CO2 + H2O + Heat/Light | CH4 + 2O2 --> CO2 + 2H2O + Heat |
| **Oxidation** | Addition of oxygen; alcohols oxidised to acids | C2H5OH + 2[O] --KMnO4/H+--> CH3COOH + H2O |
| **Addition (unsaturated)** | Addition across double/triple bond (catalytic hydrogenation) | C2H4 + H2 --Ni catalyst--> C2H6 |
| **Substitution (saturated)** | Replacement of H by another atom (halogenation in sunlight) | CH4 + Cl2 --sunlight--> CH3Cl + HCl |

### 1.8 Ethanol and Ethanoic Acid (Properties and Uses Only)
| Property | Ethanol (C2H5OH) | Ethanoic Acid (CH3COOH) |
|----------|-----------------|------------------------|
| **Common name** | Ethyl alcohol | Acetic acid |
| **Functional group** | -OH (alcohol) | -COOH (carboxylic acid) |
| **Physical state** | Colourless liquid; characteristic smell | Colourless liquid; pungent vinegar-like smell |
| **Solubility** | Soluble in water | Soluble in water |
| **Reaction with Na** | 2C2H5OH + 2Na --> 2C2H5ONa + H2 | 2CH3COOH + 2Na --> 2CH3COONa + H2 |
| **Reaction with NaHCO3** | No reaction | CH3COOH + NaHCO3 --> CH3COONa + H2O + CO2 |
| **Esterification** | With carboxylic acid forms ester (catalyst: conc. H2SO4) | With alcohol forms ester |
| **Uses** | Beverages, medicine (tincture), fuel, solvent | Vinegar (5-8%), pickling, coagulating rubber latex |

- **Denatured alcohol:** Ethanol made unfit for drinking by adding poisonous substances (e.g., methanol, pyridine).
- **Esterification:** Acid + Alcohol --conc.H2SO4--> Ester + Water (reaction produces sweet-smelling esters used in perfumes and flavours).
- **Saponification:** Ester + NaOH --> Sodium salt of acid (soap) + Alcohol

### 1.9 Soaps and Detergents
| Aspect | Soap | Detergent |
|--------|------|-----------|
| **Composition** | Sodium/potassium salts of long-chain fatty acids | Ammonium or sulphonate salts of long-chain hydrocarbons |
| **Structure** | Hydrophilic head (COO-Na+) + Hydrophobic tail (long hydrocarbon chain) | Similar micelle structure |
| **Action in hard water** | Forms insoluble scum with Ca2+/Mg2+ (calcium/magnesium stearate) | Does NOT form scum; works effectively |
| **Biodegradability** | Biodegradable | Some detergents are non-biodegradable (branched chains) |

- **Cleansing action/Micelle formation:** In water, the hydrophobic tails of soap molecules dissolve in oil/grease (dirt), while the hydrophilic heads remain in water. This forms a micelle, which is washed away with water.

## 2. Key Definitions and Formulas

| Term | Definition/Formula |

| **Homologous Series** | Series with same functional group and general formula, differing by -CH2-. |
| **Functional Group** | Atom/group of atoms that determines chemical properties of a compound. |
| **Esterification** | Alcohol + Carboxylic Acid --conc.H2SO4--> Ester + Water |
| **Saponification** | Ester + Alkali --> Soap + Alcohol |
| **Micelle** | Spherical aggregate of soap molecules in water. |
