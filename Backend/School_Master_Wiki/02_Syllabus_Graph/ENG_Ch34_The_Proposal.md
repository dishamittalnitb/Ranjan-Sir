# Chapter 34: The Proposal (Play)

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** This one-act farce satirizes the landed gentry of 19th-century Russia, exposing their trivial quarrels, stubbornness, and absurdities. Beneath the humor lies a critique of how petty disputes over property and status can nearly derail even personal, emotional relationships like marriage.

**Summary:** Ivan Lomov, a nervous landowner, visits his neighbor Stepan Chubukov to propose marriage to Chubukov's daughter, Natalya Stepanovna. Chubukov is delighted. However, when Lomov and Natalya talk alone, they immediately get into a furious argument over the ownership of a piece of land called "Oxen Meadows." Lomov, already suffering from palpitations and a weak heart, becomes so agitated that he collapses. Natalya, realizing he had come to propose, demands her father call him back. Lomov returns, and the proposal conversation resumes, but soon they argue again — this time over whose hunting dog is superior (Lomov's "Guess" or Natalya's "Squeezer"). Lomov collapses again, seemingly dead. Chubukov, panicking, forces the marriage upon the barely conscious Lomov. The play ends with the couple happily(?) engaged but still bickering over the dogs.

## Character List

- **Ivan Vassilevitch Lomov:** A 35-year-old hypochondriac landowner; nervous, indecisive, and argumentative.
- **Natalya Stepanovna:** A 25-year-old unmarried woman; strong-willed, argumentative, and equally stubborn.
- **Stepan Stepanovitch Chubukov:** Natalya's father; a hypocritical, greedy landowner who eagerly wants the marriage.

## Key Quotes

1. "I'm trembling all over. I'll go straight to the point."
2. "Oxen Meadows are mine, not yours!"
3. "What a weight off my shoulders!"
4. "You are not a neighbor, you are a grabber!"
5. "Hurry up and get married and — well, to the devil with you!"
