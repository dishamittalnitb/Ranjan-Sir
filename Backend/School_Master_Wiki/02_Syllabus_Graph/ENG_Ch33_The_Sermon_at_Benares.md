# Chapter 33: The Sermon at Benares

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The chapter presents a profound Buddhist teaching on the universality of death and the futility of grief. Through the story of Kisa Gotami, the Buddha teaches that death is inevitable, suffering is universal, and peace comes from accepting the impermanence of life.

**Summary:** The Buddha gave his first sermon at Benares after attaining enlightenment. The chapter focuses on the story of Kisa Gotami. Her only son died, and in her grief, she carried the dead child from house to house, begging for medicine. Someone directed her to the Buddha. The Buddha asked her to bring a handful of mustard seeds from a house where no one had ever died. Kisa Gotami went from door to door, and everywhere she heard the same story — "the living are few, but the dead are many." She realized that death comes to all families. Returning to the Buddha, she understood: death is universal; it spares none. The Buddha then explained that life is brief like an earthen pot, destined to break. Those who are wise do not grieve because they understand the nature of life. Kisa Gotami attained peace and became a disciple.

## Character List

- **The Buddha (Gautama Buddha):** The Enlightened One; gives the sermon at Benares and uses a compassionate, practical method to teach Kisa Gotami.
- **Kisa Gotami:** A grieving mother who becomes the Buddha's disciple after understanding the truth about death.
- **The People of the Village:** Each house Kisa Gotami visits has experienced death; their responses collectively teach her the lesson.

## Key Quotes

1. "The life of mortals in this world is troubled and brief and combined with pain."
2. "He who seeks peace should draw out the arrow of lamentation and complaint."
3. "No house was there where some beloved one had not died."
4. "Of those who, overcome by death, depart from life, a father cannot save his son, nor kinsmen their relations."
