# Chapter 16: On Killing a Tree

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The poem presents a graphic, step-by-step account of the difficulty of truly killing a tree. It is a meditation on the resilience of nature, the violence implicit in destroying it, and an environmental critique of humankind's callousness toward the natural world. The poem also serves as a metaphor for rooting out deep-seated evils.

**Summary:** The poet explains that killing a tree is not easy — a simple jab of a knife is not enough. The tree has grown slowly over years, feeding upon the earth, absorbing sunlight, air, and water. To kill it, one must pull it out by the roots entirely, exposing the "white, wet, most sensitive, hidden" source of its life. The roots must be snapped out of the anchoring earth, then the tree must be scorched, choked, browned, hardened, twisted, and withered. Only then will the tree die, completing the "ceremony" of killing.

## Literary Devices

- **Rhyme Scheme:** Free verse — no fixed rhyme scheme or meter; deliberately matter-of-fact tone.
- **Personification:** The tree is endowed with human qualities ("leprous hide," "bleeding bark," "consuming the earth").
- **Metaphor:** "Leprous hide" for the rough bark; "sprouting leaves" = the tree's unkillable life force.
- **Irony:** The title suggests a manual on killing, but the poem actually evokes sympathy for the tree.
- **Alliteration:** "bleeding bark," "white and wet"
- **Imagery:** Vivid, often violent images — "pull it out entirely," "snapped out," "scorching, choking."
- **Enjambment:** Lines run over without punctuation, reflecting the relentless process.
- **Tone:** Clinical, instructive yet deeply ironic.

## Key Quotes

1. "It takes much time to kill a tree, / Not a simple jab of the knife / Will do it."
2. "The bleeding bark will heal / And from close to the ground / Will rise curled green twigs"
3. "The root is to be pulled out — / Out of the anchoring earth"
4. "Then the matter / Of scorching and choking / In sun and air, / Browning, hardening, / Twisting, withering, / And then it is done."
