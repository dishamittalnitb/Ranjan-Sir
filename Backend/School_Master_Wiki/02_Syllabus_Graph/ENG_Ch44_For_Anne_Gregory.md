# Chapter 44: For Anne Gregory

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The poem explores the theme of superficial love based on physical appearance versus genuine love that values a person's inner self. Through a dialogue about Anne Gregory's beautiful golden hair, the poet questions whether anyone can truly love another for who they are, beyond their external beauty.

**Summary:** The poem is a conversation between a young man (or the poet) and a young woman, Anne Gregory. The speaker tells Anne that no young man could ever love her solely for herself, and not for her beautiful yellow hair. He says men will always be captivated by her external beauty. Anne responds defiantly that she could change her appearance — she could dye her hair "brown, or black, or carrot" — so that men would love her for who she is rather than her golden locks. The speaker then closes with a poignant religious reference: only God could love her for herself alone, "and not your yellow hair."

## Literary Devices

- **Rhyme Scheme:** ABAB CDCD EFEF (alternating rhyme in three quatrains).
- **Alliteration:** "great God," "brown, or black, or carrot"
- **Repetition:** "your yellow hair" — repeated to emphasize the superficial fixation.
- **Metaphor:** "Great honey-coloured / Ramparts at your ear" = Anne's hair as a defensive wall that hides her true self.
- **Dialogue Structure:** The poem is structured as a conversation between two speakers.
- **Tone:** Gentle, philosophical, slightly melancholy.

## Key Quotes

1. "Never shall a young man, / Thrown into despair / By those great honey-coloured / Ramparts at your ear"
2. "Love you for yourself alone / And not your yellow hair."
3. "I can dye my hair... brown, or black, or carrot, / That young men in despair / May love me for myself alone"
4. "Only God, my dear, / Could love you for yourself alone / And not your yellow hair."
