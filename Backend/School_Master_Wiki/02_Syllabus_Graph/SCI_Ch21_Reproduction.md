# Chapter 21: Reproduction

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Introduction
Reproduction is a biological process by which living organisms produce offspring of their own kind. It ensures continuity of species.

### 1.2 Asexual Reproduction
Production of new organisms from a single parent without the fusion of gametes.

| Method | Description | Example Organisms |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Binary Fission** | Parent cell divides into two equal daughter cells. | Amoeba, Paramecium, bacteria (Leishmania shows longitudinal binary fission) |
| **Multiple Fission** | Parent cell divides into many daughter cells simultaneously (often under cyst formation). | Plasmodium (malaria parasite) |
| **Budding** | A small outgrowth (bud) develops on the parent body, detaches when mature. | Hydra, Yeast |
| **Fragmentation** | Body breaks into fragments; each fragment grows into a new organism. | Spirogyra (alga) |
| **Regeneration** | An organism can regrow lost body parts, which may develop into a complete organism. | Planaria (flatworm), Hydra |
| **Spore Formation** | Spores are produced in sporangia; on release each spore germinates into a new organism. | Rhizopus (bread mould), ferns |
| **Vegetative Propagation** | New plants grow from vegetative parts (root, stem, leaf). Natural: runners (strawberry), rhizomes (ginger), tubers (potato), bulbs (onion), leaf buds (Bryophyllum). Artificial: cutting, layering, grafting, tissue culture. | Potato (tuber), Ginger (rhizome), Bryophyllum (leaf buds), Rose (cutting), Mango (grafting) |

**Advantages of vegetative propagation:**
- Genetically identical to parent (preserves desirable traits).
- Faster than seed-based reproduction.
- Seedless varieties can be propagated (e.g., banana, grapes, sugarcane).

### 1.3 Sexual Reproduction in Plants
Involves fusion of male and female gametes.

#### Structure of a Flower
- **Bisexual (hermaphrodite) flower:** Contains both stamen (male) and pistil/carpel (female). E.g., Hibiscus, Mustard.
- **Unisexual flower:** Contains only stamens or only pistil. E.g., Papaya, Watermelon.

| Part | Component | Function |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Stamen (Male)** | Anther + Filament | Anther produces pollen grains (contain male gametes). |
| **Pistil/Carpel (Female)** | Stigma + Style + Ovary | Ovary contains ovules; each ovule contains an egg cell (female gamete). |

#### Pollination
Transfer of pollen grains from anther to stigma.
| Type | Pollen Source | Destination | Agent | Examples |
|------|--------------|-------------|-------|----------|
| **Self-pollination** | Same flower or same plant | Same plant | Less common | Pea, Rice, Wheat |
| **Cross-pollination** | Different plant of same species | Different plant | Wind (Anemophily), Water (Hydrophily), Insects (Entomophily), Animals (Zoophily) | Sunflower, Maize, Vallisneria |

#### Fertilisation
- Pollen grain lands on stigma --> Germinates, producing a pollen tube through the style.
- Pollen tube carries two male gametes to the ovule (inside ovary).
- **Double fertilisation:** One male gamete fuses with the egg (syngamy) --> Zygote (2n). The other male gamete fuses with polar nuclei (2n) --> Endosperm (3n) (provides nutrition to developing embryo).
- **Post-fertilisation:** Ovule --> Seed; Ovary --> Fruit; Zygote --> Embryo; Sepals/petals/stamens/style --> Wither/fall off.

### 1.4 Sexual Reproduction in Humans

#### Male Reproductive System
| Organ | Function |
|-------|----------|
| **Testes (pair)** | Produces sperms (male gametes); secretes testosterone. Located in scrotum (outside body cavity for lower temperature ~ 2-2.5 degree C below body temp). |
| **Epididymis** | Stores and matures sperms. |
| **Vas deferens** | Tube carrying sperms from epididymis to urethra. |
| **Seminal vesicle + Prostate gland + Cowper's gland** | Secrete seminal fluid (provides nutrition + medium for sperms). Sperm + fluid = Semen. |
| **Penis** | Organ for copulation; delivers sperms into female reproductive tract. |

#### Female Reproductive System
| Organ | Function |
|-------|----------|
| **Ovaries (pair)** | Produces ova (eggs/female gametes); secretes oestrogen and progesterone. One ovum released per month (ovulation). |
| **Fallopian tubes (Oviducts)** | Tube carrying ovum from ovary to uterus; site of fertilisation. |
| **Uterus (Womb)** | Pear-shaped muscular organ where embryo implants and develops. |
| **Cervix** | Lower narrow part of uterus connecting to vagina. |
| **Vagina** | Birth canal; receives sperms during copulation. |

#### Fertilisation and Development
- Zygote divides repeatedly and forms an embryo (morula --> blastocyst).
- **Implantation:** Embryo attaches to the uterine wall (endometrium).
- **Placenta:** Disc-shaped tissue connecting embryo to mother's uterus. Functions:
  - Nutrient and oxygen supply to embryo.
  - Waste removal from embryo.
  - Hormone production (e.g., progesterone).
- **Gestation period:** ~ 9 months (280 days). Child birth process is called parturition.
- **Menstruation:** If fertilisation does not occur, the uterine lining (endometrium) sheds along with blood via the vagina. Cycle ~28 days. Starts at puberty (menarche) and stops around age 45-50 (menopause).

### 1.5 Reproductive Health
- **Need:** To ensure physical, mental, and social well-being related to reproductive system.
- **Methods of family planning (Contraceptive methods):**

| Type | Method | Mechanism |
|------|--------|-----------|
| **Barrier** | Condoms, Diaphragm, Cervical caps | Prevent sperms from reaching the ovum. Also protect against STDs (condoms). |
| **Hormonal** | Oral pills, Injections, Implants | Inhibit ovulation; alter hormonal cycle. |
| **Intrauterine Device (IUD)** | Copper-T (Cu-T) | Prevents implantation; copper ions are spermicidal. |
| **Surgical (Permanent)** | Vasectomy (males: cutting vas deferens); Tubectomy (females: cutting fallopian tubes) | Permanent sterilisation by blocking gamete transport. |

### 1.6 Safe Sex vs HIV/AIDS
- **HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus):** Virus that causes AIDS (Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome).
- **Transmission:** Sexual contact, blood transfusion, sharing contaminated needles, from mother to child (during pregnancy/birth/breastfeeding).
- **Prevention:** Safe sex practices (use of condoms), screening of blood before transfusion, avoid sharing needles, public awareness.
- **Safe sex:** Sexual practices that reduce the risk of STDs and unintended pregnancy (barrier methods, mutual monogamy).

### 1.7 Child Bearing and Women's Health
- Early marriage and early pregnancy (before 18 years) pose health risks to mother and child.
- Nutritional needs increase during pregnancy (iron, calcium, protein, folic acid).
- Adequate spacing between births improves maternal and child health.
- Female foeticide (sex-selective abortion) is illegal and a crime in India.

## 2. Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Asexual Reproduction** | Reproduction involving a single parent, without gamete fusion. |
| **Sexual Reproduction** | Reproduction involving fusion of male and female gametes. |
| **Pollination** | Transfer of pollen grains from anther to stigma. |
| **Fertilisation** | Fusion of male and female gametes to form a zygote. |
| **Zygote** | Fertilised egg (single cell formed after fertilisation). |
| **Embryo** | Developing organism in the early stages (before differentiation into body parts). |
| **Foetus** | Developing organism when body parts become distinguishable (after ~8 weeks in humans). |
| **Implantation** | Attachment of embryo to the uterine wall. |
| **Placenta** | Tissue connecting foetus to mother's uterus; exchanges nutrients, gases, wastes. |
| **Menstruation** | Monthly shedding of the uterine lining when fertilisation does not occur. |
| **Contraception** | Methods used to prevent pregnancy. |
