# Chapter 20: Food Security in India

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Comprehend various aspects of food security that will ensure continuity of supply.
- Enumerate the different features of PDS that directly address food security.
- Analyse and infer the impact of the Green Revolution.
- Analyse causes and effect of famines in food security during pre and post independent India.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Meaning of Food Security**: Food security exists when all people, at all times, have physical, social, and economic access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food. It has three dimensions:
   - **Availability**: Sufficient food production and stocks.
   - **Accessibility**: People can afford to buy food.
   - **Affordability**: Adequate income to purchase required food.
2. **Why Food Security?**: The poorest sections may face food insecurity even if national production is adequate. Natural calamities, drought, floods, failure of crops cause food shortage and famine.
3. **Famines in India**: The Bengal Famine (1943) was one of the worst, killing about 3 million people. Post-independence, India has not experienced major famines due to better policy, buffer stocks, and PDS.
4. **Green Revolution**: Introduced in the 1960s, it involved using HYV seeds, chemical fertilizers, irrigation, and pesticides. Led to a massive increase in food grain production (especially wheat in Punjab, Haryana, UP). India became self-sufficient in food grains.
5. **Food Security System in India**:
   - **Buffer Stock**: Stock of food grains (wheat and rice) maintained by FCI (Food Corporation of India) for emergencies and price stabilization.
   - **PDS (Public Distribution System)**: Ration shops (Fair Price Shops) provide subsidized food grains and essential commodities to the poor. Items include wheat, rice, sugar, kerosene.
   - **NFSA (National Food Security Act, 2013)**: Entitles up to 75% of rural and 50% of urban population to receive subsidized grains (rice at Rs 3/kg, wheat at Rs 2/kg, coarse grains at Rs 1/kg).
6. **Role of Cooperatives**: Cooperative societies (like AMUL in milk, Mother Dairy) help in ensuring food security by reducing exploitation by middlemen.
7. **Challenges**: Corruption in PDS, leakages, bogus ration cards, poor quality of grains, storage losses, and exclusion of genuine beneficiaries.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Food Security**: The condition when all people, at all times, have access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food.
- **Buffer Stock**: The stock of food grains procured and maintained by the government for emergencies.
- **PDS (Public Distribution System)**: A network of ration shops distributing subsidized essential commodities.
- **FCI (Food Corporation of India)**: The government agency responsible for procurement, storage, and distribution of food grains.
- **Fair Price Shop (Ration Shop)**: A shop under PDS where subsidized food items are sold.
- **Minimum Support Price (MSP)**: The price at which the government purchases food grains from farmers.
- **Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY)**: A scheme providing highly subsidized food grains to the poorest families.
- **Revamped PDS (RPDS)**: Launched in 1992 to improve PDS infrastructure.
- **Green Revolution**: A large increase in food grain production using modern technology, from the late 1960s.
