# Chapter 26: Magnetic Effects of Current

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Magnetic Field and Field Lines
- **Magnetic field:** The region around a magnet where its influence (force) can be experienced.
- **Magnetic field lines:** Imaginary lines representing the magnetic field.
  - Direction: From North pole to South pole outside the magnet (and South to North inside).
  - They form closed continuous curves.
  - They never intersect each other (unique field direction at every point).
  - Closer lines = stronger field; wider spacing = weaker field.

### 1.2 Magnetic Field Due to a Current-Carrying Conductor
- A current-carrying conductor produces a magnetic field around it (Oersted's discovery).
- **Straight conductor:** Magnetic field lines are concentric circles around the wire.
  - Direction given by **Right-Hand Thumb Rule**: Point thumb in the direction of current; fingers curl in the direction of the magnetic field.
  - Field strength increases with current and decreases with distance from the wire.

### 1.3 Magnetic Field Due to a Current-Carrying Coil (Circular Loop)
- At the centre of a circular current loop: Magnetic field is along the axis perpendicular to the plane of the loop.
- Direction given by Right-Hand Thumb Rule.
- Field strength increases with number of turns (N) and current (I), and decreases with radius (R).

### 1.4 Solenoid
- A coil of many circular turns of insulated copper wire wound in a cylindrical shape.
- **Field inside:** Uniform, strong, parallel (like a bar magnet).
- **Field outside:** Similar to a bar magnet (one end North, other South).
- The strength of the magnetic field inside a solenoid can be increased by:
  - Increasing the current.
  - Increasing the number of turns.
  - Inserting a soft iron core inside (makes it an **electromagnet**).

### 1.5 Electromagnet
- A solenoid with a soft iron core.
- **Properties:** Temporary magnet; field strength can be varied by changing current/number of turns.
- **Applications:** Electric bells, cranes for lifting heavy metal scrap, magnetic relays, loudspeakers, MRI machines.

### 1.6 Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor in a Magnetic Field
- A current-carrying conductor experiences a force when placed in a magnetic field (Fleming's motor principle).
- **Fleming's Left-Hand Rule:** Stretch the thumb, forefinger, and middle finger of the left hand mutually perpendicular.
  - **Forefinger** (Index): Direction of magnetic field (B).
  - **Middle finger:** Direction of current (I).
  - **Thumb:** Direction of force (F) / motion.
- The direction of force reverses if either the current direction or the magnetic field direction is reversed.
- **Applications:** Electric motor.

### 1.7 Electric Motor
- Converts electrical energy into mechanical energy.
- **Principle:** A current-carrying conductor experiences a force when placed in a magnetic field.
- **Construction:** Armature coil (ABCD), strong permanent magnet (N-S), split ring commutator (reverses current every half rotation), brushes, axle.
- **Working:** Coil rotates due to force on its two arms. Split ring commutator reverses current direction every half rotation, ensuring the coil continues rotating in the same direction.

### 1.8 Electromagnetic Induction
- The process of generating an induced current (or induced emf) in a conductor by changing the magnetic field.
- Discovered by Michael Faraday.
- Induced current can be produced by:
  - Moving a conductor (coil) relative to a magnet.
  - Changing the current in a nearby coil (mutual induction).
- **Factors affecting magnitude of induced current:** Speed of relative motion, number of turns, strength of the magnetic field.

### 1.9 Fleming's Right-Hand Rule (for induced current)
- Stretch the thumb, forefinger, and middle finger of the right hand mutually perpendicular.
  - **Forefinger** (Index): Direction of magnetic field (B).
  - **Thumb:** Direction of motion of conductor.
  - **Middle finger:** Direction of induced current (I).
- Used in understanding the operation of an electric generator.

### 1.10 Electric Generator (Dynamo)
- Converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.
- **Principle:** Electromagnetic induction.
- **AC Generator:** Uses slip rings (full rings) to deliver alternating current. Current direction changes every half rotation.
- **DC Generator:** Uses split ring commutator to deliver direct current (unidirectional).

### 1.11 Direct Current (DC) and Alternating Current (AC)

| Aspect | DC (Direct Current) | AC (Alternating Current) |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------------|
| **Direction** | Unidirectional (constant polarity) | Reverses direction periodically |
| **Value** | Constant magnitude | Varies sinusoidally |
| **Frequency in India** | - | 50 Hz (direction changes 100 times/sec) |
| **Sources** | Batteries, cells, solar panels | AC generators (power plants) |
| **Transmission** | Cannot be efficiently transmitted over long distances | Can be transmitted over long distances with minimal loss (with transformers) |

### 1.12 Advantages of AC over DC
- AC voltage can be stepped up or down using transformers (minimises power loss during transmission: P_loss = I^2 R, and I is reduced at high voltage).
- AC generators are simpler and more robust.
- AC can be easily converted to DC using rectifiers.

### 1.13 Domestic Electric Circuits
- Single-phase AC supply: 220 V, 50 Hz in India.
- **Three wires:**
  - **Live/Phase (Red/Brown):** At 220 V potential.
  - **Neutral (Black/Blue):** At zero potential (completes the circuit).
  - **Earth/Ground (Green/Yellow):** Safety wire connected to a metal plate buried in the ground. Provides a low-resistance path for fault current.
- **Features:**
  - All appliances connected in **parallel** (each gets 220 V; failure of one does not affect others).
  - **Main fuse:** Connected on the live wire before the meter.
  - **Main switch (MCB - Miniature Circuit Breaker):** Safety device that trips (switches off) on overcurrent.
  - **Earthing (Grounding):** Metal body of appliances connected to earth wire to prevent electric shock.
  - **Separate circuits:** 5 A lines for low-power devices (lights, fans) and 15 A lines for high-power devices (AC, geyser, refrigerator).

### 1.14 Safety Measures
- Use proper earthing for all appliances.
- Use fuses / MCBs of appropriate ratings.
- Do not touch electrical appliances with wet hands.
- Do not overload a single socket with too many appliances.
- Use of ISI-marked appliances and wiring.
- Fuse wire should be rated for slightly more than the normal operating current of the appliance.

## 2. Key Definitions and Rules

| Term | Description |
|------|------------|
| **Magnetic Field** | Region around a magnet/current-carrying conductor where magnetic force is experienced. |
| **Solenoid** | Cylindrical coil of many turns carrying current; produces uniform magnetic field inside. |
| **Electromagnet** | Solenoid with soft iron core; temporary magnet. |
| **Electromagnetic Induction** | Production of induced emf/current by changing magnetic field. |
| **Right-Hand Thumb Rule** | Thumb = current direction; curled fingers = magnetic field direction. |
| **Fleming's Left-Hand Rule** | Thumb = Force, Index = Field, Middle = Current (Motor rule). |
| **Fleming's Right-Hand Rule** | Thumb = Motion, Index = Field, Middle = Current (Generator rule). |
| **Frequency of AC in India** | 50 Hz |
| **Domestic Voltage in India** | 220 V (Single Phase AC) |

### Common Question Patterns
- **Magnetic field lines:** Properties — closed continuous loops, direction from N to S outside, never intersect, closer lines = stronger field
- **Right-hand thumb rule:** Current-carrying straight conductor → circular magnetic field
- **Solenoid:** Coil of many turns — acts as bar magnet, field strength ∝ current × number of turns
- **Fleming's left hand rule:** Forefinger (field), centre finger (current), thumb (force/motion) — used for DC motor
- **Fleming's right hand rule:** Forefinger (field), thumb (motion), centre finger (induced current) — used for AC generator
- **DC motor:** Split rings (commutator) reverse current direction every half cycle
- **Domestic circuits:** 220V AC, 50 Hz, live/neutral/earth, fuse, MCB
- **Electromagnetic induction:** Moving magnet near coil induces current (Faraday's Law)
