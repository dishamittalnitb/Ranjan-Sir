# Chapter 25: The Beggar

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The story explores themes of compassion, redemption, and the transformative power of kindness and work. It suggests that genuine help is not about charity (giving money) but about restoring a person's dignity through meaningful work and human connection.

**Summary:** The story follows Lushkoff, a beggar in Moscow who tells lies to evoke pity. Advocate Sergei, a lawyer, catches him in his lies but, instead of turning him away, offers him work chopping wood. Lushkoff is weak and incapable, but Sergei's cook Olga does the chopping for him — silently and kindly, without seeking recognition. Over time, Sergei finds Lushkoff different jobs (errand boy, notary's assistant) and eventually helps him get steady work. Two years later, Sergei meets Lushkoff, now well-dressed and employed at a theatre ticket counter. Lushkoff credits Sergei for his transformation, but reveals the truth: it was Olga's quiet, sacrificial help — not Sergei's loud charity — that truly changed him. Her compassion and the dignity of work reformed him from a beggar into a self-respecting man.

## Character List

- **Lushkoff:** A beggar and drunkard who undergoes a remarkable transformation through work and the kindness of Olga.
- **Advocate Sergei:** A lawyer who tries to reform Lushkoff through work; sincere but a little self-congratulatory.
- **Olga (the Cook):** The true agent of change; she silently does Lushkoff's work for him while scolding him, showing tough love and genuine compassion.

## Key Quotes

1. "I am a poor student who has nothing to eat."
2. "Work, work! That is the only thing that can save you."
3. "It was not I who helped you... it was Olga, the cook."
4. "She would sit and cry, and then chop the wood herself."
5. "I am grateful to you, but it was Olga who saved me."
