# Chapter 36: Fire and Ice

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The poem ponders how the world might end — by fire (representing desire, passion, greed, hatred) or by ice (representing coldness, indifference, apathy). It suggests that both excessive emotions and emotional coldness are equally destructive and can bring about the end of humanity.

**Summary:** The poem begins with a debate: some say the world will end in fire, others in ice. The speaker sides with "fire" because he has experienced the destructive power of desire. However, he then adds that if the world had to perish twice, he thinks he knows enough about hate to say that ice "is also great / And would suffice" — meaning that cold indifference and hatred are equally capable of destroying everything.

## Literary Devices

- **Rhyme Scheme:** ABA ABC BCB (interlocking rhyme scheme, 9 lines).
- **Symbolism:**
  - "Fire" = desire, greed, passion, lust, anger, hatred.
  - "Ice" = coldness, indifference, apathy, rigid reasoning, lack of empathy.
- **Metaphor:** The entire poem is an extended metaphor — fire and ice are not literal but represent human emotions.
- **Alliteration:** "favour fire," "world will"
- **Assonance:** "fire" and "ice" — contrasting vowel sounds.
- **Tone:** Reflective, philosophical, detached yet concerned.

## Key Quotes

1. "Some say the world will end in fire, / Some say in ice."
2. "From what I've tasted of desire / I hold with those who favour fire."
3. "I think I know enough of hate / To say that for destruction ice / Is also great / And would suffice."
