# Chapter 31: Mijbil the Otter

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The story celebrates the human-animal bond, capturing the joy and companionship a pet can bring. It also highlights the adaptability of animals, the challenges of travel with a pet, and the universal curiosity and affection of people toward unusual animals.

**Summary:** The narrator, Gavin Maxwell, living in Southern Iraq, decides to keep an otter as a pet after his dog dies. With help from a local, he obtains Mijbil (Mij), an otter of a species previously unknown to science. Initially terrified of the new environment, Mij soon adapts and fills the narrator's life with playful mischief — splashing water, turning taps, and entertaining everyone. When the narrator must fly back to London, Mij has to travel in a box. During the flight, Mij escapes and causes chaos, eventually settling peacefully on the narrator's lap. In London, people are fascinated by Mij, not knowing what animal he is — most guess he is a "baby seal" or a "squirrel." The otter becomes the narrator's inseparable companion.

## Character List

- **Gavin Maxwell (Narrator):** A nature lover who adopts Mijbil and develops a deep bond with him.
- **Mijbil (Mij):** An otter of the newly discovered species Lutrogale perspicillata maxwelli; playful, intelligent, mischievous, and affectionate.
- **The Arab Friend:** Helps the narrator acquire Mijbil.
- **The Air Hostess and Passengers:** Show varied reactions to Mij on the flight.

## Key Quotes

1. "I named the otter Mijbil, or Mij for short."
2. "Mij was anything but an ordinary otter. He had a passion for water."
3. "He spent hours shuffling a rubber ball round the room like a four-footed soccer player."
4. "It is not, sir, a rat. It is an otter."
5. "Mij was neither a baby seal, nor a squirrel, nor a walrus. He was an otter."
