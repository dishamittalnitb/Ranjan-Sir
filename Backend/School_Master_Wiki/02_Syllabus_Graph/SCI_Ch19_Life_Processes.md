# Chapter 19: Life Processes

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 What is a 'Living Being'?
- Living organisms exhibit life processes: nutrition, respiration, transport, excretion, growth, reproduction, response to stimuli.
- These life processes require energy, which is obtained through nutrition.

### 1.2 Nutrition
The process of obtaining and utilising food for growth, repair, and energy.

#### Autotrophic Nutrition (Plants)
- Organisms synthesise their own food from inorganic substances (CO2 + H2O --> Glucose + O2).
- **Photosynthesis:** 6CO2 + 12H2O --light, chlorophyll--> C6H12O6 + 6O2 + 6H2O
  - Raw materials: CO2 (from atmosphere via stomata), H2O (from soil via roots).
  - Energy source: Sunlight (absorbed by chlorophyll).
  - Site: Chloroplast in mesophyll cells of leaves.
  - Products: Glucose (converted to starch for storage) and O2 (by-product).
- **Steps of photosynthesis:**
  1. Absorption of light energy by chlorophyll.
  2. Photolysis of water (splitting into H+ and O2).
  3. Reduction of CO2 to carbohydrates (dark reaction/Calvin cycle).

#### Heterotrophic Nutrition (Animals, Fungi)
Organisms obtain food from other organisms.
| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Holozoic** | Ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation, egestion of solid food | Humans, Amoeba, Paramecium |
| **Saprophytic** | Secretion of enzymes, absorption of digested food from dead/decaying matter | Fungi (bread mould), bacteria |
| **Parasitic** | Obtaining nutrition from a living host without killing it (or eventually killing) | Tapeworm, lice, Cuscuta (plant) |

#### Nutrition in Humans (Holozoic)
- **Alimentary canal:** Mouth --> Oesophagus --> Stomach --> Small Intestine --> Large Intestine --> Rectum --> Anus
- **Associated glands:** Salivary glands (saliva - amylase), Gastric glands (HCl, pepsin, mucus), Liver (bile), Pancreas (pancreatic juice - trypsin, lipase, amylase).
- **Digestion:** Complex food --> simple absorbable substances.
  - Mouth: Starch --salivary amylase--> Maltose (partial digestion).
  - Stomach: Proteins --pepsin--> Peptones. HCl creates acidic environment and kills bacteria.
  - Small Intestine (duodenum): Complete digestion of all food types (by bile + pancreatic + intestinal enzymes).
  - Absorption: Villi in small intestine increase surface area for absorption.
  - Assimilation: Absorbed nutrients used for energy, growth, repair.

### 1.3 Respiration
The process of breaking down food (glucose) to release energy (ATP).

| Type | Oxygen | Glucose breakdown | ATP yield | Site |
|------|--------|------------------|-----------|------|
| **Aerobic** | Required | Complete breakdown to CO2 + H2O | 38 ATP per glucose | Mitochondria |
| **Anaerobic** | Not required | Partial breakdown | 2 ATP per glucose | Cytoplasm |

- **Anaerobic (Yeast):** Glucose --> Pyruvate --lack of O2--> Ethanol + CO2 + 2 ATP (Fermentation)
- **Anaerobic (Muscle cells):** Glucose --> Pyruvate --lack of O2--> Lactic Acid + 2 ATP (Causes cramps)
- **Overall equation (aerobic):** C6H12O6 + 6O2 --> 6CO2 + 6H2O + Energy (ATP)

#### Respiration in Plants
- Gaseous exchange through stomata, lenticels, and root hairs.
- Plants respire all the time (photosynthesis only in daylight).
- Oxygen diffuses in, CO2 diffuses out.

#### Respiration in Animals
- **Human respiratory system:** Nostrils --> Nasal passage --> Pharynx --> Larynx --> Trachea --> Bronchi --> Bronchioles --> Alveoli.
- **Alveoli:** Tiny air sacs surrounded by capillaries; site of gaseous exchange (diffusion of O2 into blood, CO2 out).
- **Mechanism of breathing:**
  - Inhalation: Diaphragm contracts (moves down), rib cage moves up/out, lung volume increases, air rushes in.
  - Exhalation: Diaphragm relaxes (moves up), rib cage moves down/in, lung volume decreases, air pushed out.

### 1.4 Transport

#### Transport in Plants
| Tissue | Transported | Direction | Mechanism |
|--------|-------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Xylem** | Water and dissolved minerals | Upward (root to leaves) | Transpiration pull (cohesion-tension theory), root pressure |
| **Phloem** | Food (sucrose, amino acids) | Bidirectional (source to sink) | Translocation using energy from ATP |

- **Transpiration:** Loss of water in the form of water vapour from aerial parts (mainly through stomata). Creates transpiration pull for water ascent.

#### Transport in Humans
- **Blood:** Fluid connective tissue.
  - **Plasma (55%):** Water, proteins, salts. Transports nutrients, hormones, waste.
  - **RBC (Red Blood Cells):** Contain haemoglobin; transport O2.
  - **WBC (White Blood Cells):** Fight infection (immunity).
  - **Platelets (Thrombocytes):** Help in blood clotting.
- **Heart:** Four-chambered muscular pump. Double circulation.
  - Pulmonary circulation: Heart --> Lungs --> Heart (deoxygenated to oxygenated).
  - Systemic circulation: Heart --> Body --> Heart (oxygenated to deoxygenated).
- **Blood vessels:** Arteries (thick, elastic, carry blood away from heart, mostly oxygenated), Veins (thinner, have valves, carry blood to heart, mostly deoxygenated), Capillaries (thin, single-cell thick, site of exchange).
- **Lymph:** Colourless fluid; drains excess tissue fluid; transports fats from intestine.

### 1.5 Excretion

#### Excretion in Plants
- Waste products stored in vacuoles, old xylem, bark, leaves (fall off).
- Gums, resins, latex are metabolic by-products.
- Gaseous wastes diffused out through stomata.

#### Excretion in Humans
- **Excretory organs:** Kidneys (main), Lungs (CO2), Skin (sweat - water, salts, urea), Liver (bile pigments).
- **Urinary System:** Kidneys --> Ureters --> Urinary Bladder --> Urethra.
- **Kidney:** Functional unit = Nephron.
  - **Nephron:** Bowman's capsule + Glomerulus (filtration) --> Tubule (reabsorption of glucose, amino acids, salts, water) --> Collecting duct.
  - **Filtration:** Glomerular filtration (blood filtered under pressure).
  - **Reabsorption:** Useful substances reabsorbed from filtrate into blood.
  - **Secretion:** Additional wastes actively secreted into the tubule.
- **Urine:** Water, urea, uric acid, excess salts.
- **Haemodialysis:** Artificial kidney for patients with kidney failure. Blood is filtered through a dialysis membrane to remove nitrogenous wastes.

## 2. Key Definitions and Equations

| Term | Definition / Equation |
|------|----------------------|
| **Photosynthesis** | 6CO2 + 12H2O --light, chlorophyll--> C6H12O6 + 6O2 + 6H2O |

| **Anaerobic Respiration (Yeast)** | Glucose --> Ethanol + CO2 + 2 ATP |
| **Anaerobic Respiration (Muscle)** | Glucose --> Lactic Acid + 2 ATP |
| **Autotroph** | Organism that synthesises its own food from inorganic sources. |
| **Heterotroph** | Organism that obtains food from other organisms. |
| **Peristalsis** | Rhythmic contraction and relaxation of alimentary canal muscles to push food forward. |
| **Villi** | Finger-like projections in small intestine increasing surface area for absorption. |
| **Alveoli** | Tiny air sacs in lungs where gaseous exchange occurs. |
| **Haemoglobin** | Red pigment in RBC that binds and transports oxygen. |
| **Nephron** | Structural and functional unit of the kidney. |
| **Transpiration** | Loss of water vapour from aerial parts of plants. |

## 3. Board Exam Weightage & Question Pattern

Falls under **Unit II: World of Living (25 marks)** - one of the highest-weightage units in Class X.

- **VSA (1 mark):** Define photosynthesis/autotroph/heterotroph; name the enzyme/acid; where are villi/alveoli/nephron found.

- **LA (5 marks):** Explain photosynthesis with equation, site, and steps; describe human respiratory system with diagram; explain transport in plants; explain urine formation in kidneys.
