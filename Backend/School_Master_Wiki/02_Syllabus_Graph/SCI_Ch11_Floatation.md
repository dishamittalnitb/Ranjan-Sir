# Chapter 11: Floatation

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Thrust
- The force acting perpendicular to a surface.
- Also called the normal force.
- SI unit: Newton (N).

### 1.2 Pressure
- The thrust acting per unit area of a surface.
- **Pressure = Thrust / Area** or **P = F/A**
- SI unit: Pascal (Pa). 1 Pa = 1 N/m^2.
- Pressure depends on both force and area:
  - For the same force, smaller area --> greater pressure (e.g., cutting tools are sharp; nail has pointed tip).
  - For the same force, larger area --> smaller pressure (e.g., wide tyres of trucks; snow shoes; foundations of buildings).

### 1.3 Pressure in Fluids
- Fluids (liquids and gases) exert pressure on the walls of their container.
- Pressure in a fluid increases with depth: **P = h x density x g** (h = depth of fluid column).
- Pressure is exerted equally in all directions at a given depth (Pascal's Law).

### 1.4 Buoyancy (Upthrust)
- The upward force exerted by a fluid on an object immersed in it.
- Buoyant force is the reason objects feel lighter in water.
- Factors affecting buoyant force: Volume of object immersed and density of fluid.
- **Positive buoyancy:** Object floats (buoyant force > weight).
- **Neutral buoyancy:** Object stays at any depth (buoyant force = weight).
- **Negative buoyancy:** Object sinks (buoyant force < weight).

### 1.5 Archimedes' Principle
- When a body is fully or partially immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward force (buoyant force) equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.
- **Applications:**
  - Design of ships and submarines.
  - Hydrometers (to measure density of liquids).
  - Lactometers (to measure purity of milk).
  - Hot air balloons (buoyancy in air).
  - Life jackets / safety jackets.
  - Assessment of density/ purity of substances.

### 1.6 Density
- Mass per unit volume: **Density = Mass / Volume**
- SI unit: kg/m^3. Common unit: g/cm^3.
- Density of water = 1 g/cm^3 = 1000 kg/m^3.
- **Relative density (Specific gravity):** Ratio of density of a substance to density of water at 4 degree C. Has no unit.
- An object floats if its density is less than the fluid; sinks if greater.

## 2. Key Formulas/Definitions

| Term | Formula |
|------|---------|
| **Pressure** | P = F/A (Force per unit area) |
| **Pressure in a fluid column** | P = h x rho x g |
| **Density** | rho = m/V |
| **Relative Density** | Density of substance / Density of water at 4 degree C |
| **Archimedes' Principle** | Buoyant force = Weight of fluid displaced |
| **Buoyant Force** | F_b = V_immersed x rho_fluid x g |

## 6. Practical Experiments (from CBSE Syllabus)

**Experiment 8 (Unit-III):** Determination of the density of solid (denser than water) by using a spring balance and a measuring cylinder.
- **Objective:** To determine the density of a solid object with density greater than water using a spring balance (to measure weight/mass) and measuring cylinder (to measure volume by displacement).
- **Formula:** Density = Mass / Volume
- **Apparatus:** Spring balance, measuring cylinder, solid, thread, water.

**Experiment 9 (Unit-III):** Establishing the relation between the loss in weight of a solid when fully immersed in:
- (a) Tap water
- (b) Strongly salty water (brine/ saline)
  - With the weight of water displaced by it by taking at least two different solids.
- **Objective:** To verify Archimedes' principle: Loss in weight of solid = Weight of fluid displaced.
- **Key Learning:** Loss in weight (buoyant force) is greater in salt water than in tap water because salt water has higher density.
