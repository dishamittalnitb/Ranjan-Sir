# Chapter 49: Footprints Without Feet

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** This chapter is an extract from H.G. Wells' classic science fiction novel "The Invisible Man." It explores themes of scientific ambition without ethical responsibility, the misuse of knowledge, and the social consequences of unchecked power. The "invisibility" becomes a metaphor for the desire to escape social accountability.

**Summary:** The story follows Griffin, a brilliant but unscrupulous scientist who has discovered how to make the human body invisible. After experimenting on a cat and then himself, he becomes invisible. However, his invisibility brings unforeseen problems: he has to go naked (clothes remain visible), he cannot eat in public (food becomes visible inside him), and he is isolated from society. The extract describes two events: Griffin's stay in a London store where he feasts and clothes himself but is discovered; and his arrival at the village of Iping, where he takes a room at the local inn. The suspicious behavior of the invisible stranger — covered from head to toe in bandages, wearing dark glasses and a false nose — alarms the innkeeper Mrs. Hall. When mysterious events occur (furniture moves by itself, money disappears), suspicion mounts, and eventually Griffin is exposed as a dangerous invisible man.

## Character List

- **Griffin (The Invisible Man):** A brilliant scientist; eccentric, irritable, and morally corrupt; misuses his discovery for personal gain and revenge.
- **Mrs. Hall:** The innkeeper at Iping; curious and suspicious of her strange guest.
- **Mr. Jaffers:** The village constable called to arrest Griffin.
- **The Marvel:** (Referenced) Griffin's unwilling accomplice; a tramp.

## Key Quotes

1. "The two boys started in surprise at the fresh muddy imprints of a pair of bare feet."
2. "Griffin was a lawless person. He had chosen to set himself against the law."
3. "He was brilliantly successful — but the success had turned him into a monster."
4. "The visitor wore a big hat, dark glasses, and bandages all over his face."
