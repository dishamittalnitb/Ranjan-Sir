# Chapter 01: The French Revolution

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Infer how the French Revolution had an impact on the European countries in the making of nation states in Europe and elsewhere.
- Illustrate that the quest for imperialism triggered the First World War.
- Examine various sources to address imbalances that may lead to revolutions.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Background**: The French Revolution (1789) was a period of radical social and political upheaval in France that fundamentally changed the course of modern history.
2. **Causes**: Political (absolute monarchy of Louis XVI), Social (three estates system, privileges of clergy and nobility), Economic (empty treasury, rising bread prices, taxation burden on Third Estate), and Intellectual (ideas of philosophers like Rousseau, Montesquieu, Voltaire).
3. **Key Events**: Storming of the Bastille (14 July 1789), Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen, abolition of feudal privileges, formation of the National Assembly.
4. **Rise of Napoleon**: After the Reign of Terror (1793-94) and the Directory, Napoleon Bonaparte seized power in 1799, later declaring himself Emperor of France (1804).
5. **Impact**: Spread of ideas of liberty, equality, and fraternity; abolition of feudalism; inspiration for anti-colonial movements; rise of nationalism across Europe.
6. **Women and the Revolution**: Women actively participated, formed political clubs, and fought for equal rights; however, they did not gain voting rights.
7. **Abolition of Slavery**: The revolution debated slavery in French colonies; it was abolished in 1794 but reinstated by Napoleon in 1802; finally abolished in French colonies in 1848.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Estates General**: A political assembly of the three estates of pre-revolutionary France.
- **Tithe**: A tax levied by the Church, comprising one-tenth of agricultural produce.
- **Taille**: A direct tax paid by the Third Estate to the state.
- **Bourgeoisie**: The middle class, including merchants, lawyers, and professionals.
- **Reign of Terror**: The period under Robespierre (1793-94) marked by mass executions of "enemies of the revolution."
- **Suffrage**: The right to vote.
- **Directory**: The five-member executive body that governed France after the Reign of Terror, before Napoleon's rise.
- **Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen**: A foundational document of the Revolution, proclaiming equality and freedom.
