# Chapter 27: Forest and Wildlife Resources

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Examine the importance of conserving forests and wildlife and their interdependency in maintaining the ecology for the sustainable development of India.
- Analyse the role of grazing and wood cutting in the development and degradation.
- Summarise the reasons for conservation of biodiversity under sustainable development.
- Discuss how developmental works, grazing, and wood cutting have impacted the forests.
- Use art integration to summarise and present the reasons for conservation of biodiversity in India under sustainable development.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Biodiversity**: India is one of the world's richest countries in terms of biological diversity. It has nearly 8% of the world's total species on 2.4% of the world's land area.
2. **Flora and Fauna in India**: Rich in species diversity; home to tigers, lions, elephants, rhinoceros, and numerous species of plants and birds. The peacock is the national bird; the tiger is the national animal.
3. **Vanishing Forests**: The forest cover in India is about 24.56% of the total geographical area (India State of Forest Report). Degradation is caused by:
   - Agricultural expansion.
   - Enrichment plantation (single commercially valuable species replacing diverse forests).
   - Development projects (dams, roads, mining).
   - Overgrazing and fuel wood collection.
   - Shifting cultivation (Jhum) in northeastern states.
4. **Classification of Forests by Ownership**: Reserved Forests (more than half of total forest area), Protected Forests (almost one-third), and Unclassed Forests (remaining).
5. **Conservation of Forest and Wildlife**:
   - **Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972**: Legal framework for wildlife protection.
   - **Project Tiger (1973)**: One of the most successful conservation projects.
   - **Project Elephant (1992)**.
   - National Parks (106), Wildlife Sanctuaries (567), Biosphere Reserves.
   - **Chipko Movement (1970s)**: A grassroots forest conservation movement in Uttarakhand, led by local women hugging trees to prevent felling.
   - **Joint Forest Management (JFM)**: A programme since 1988 involving local communities in forest protection and management.
6. **Community and Conservation**: Many traditional communities (Bishnois of Rajasthan, tribals of Odisha and Bihar) have sacred groves where they protect trees and wildlife. In Sariska Tiger Reserve, villagers opposed hunting.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Biodiversity**: The variety of plant and animal life in a particular habitat.
- **Sacred Groves**: Forest patches protected by local communities due to religious/cultural beliefs.
- **Endangered Species**: Species at high risk of extinction.
- **Vulnerable Species**: Species whose population has declined to levels likely to become endangered.
- **Endemic Species**: Species found only in a particular area.
- **Extinct Species**: Species that no longer exist.
- **Chipko Movement**: A non-violent forest conservation movement that began in 1970s Uttarakhand.
- **JFM (Joint Forest Management)**: A forest governance model involving local communities.
- **IUCN**: International Union for Conservation of Nature, maintains the Red List of threatened species.
