# Chapter 30: Glimpses of India

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The chapter consists of three travelogue-style sketches celebrating the diversity of India — its cuisine, its natural beauty, and its cultural richness. Each part offers a distinct "glimpse" into a different region, collectively painting a portrait of India's pluralistic identity.

**Summary (Part I - A Baker from Goa):** The narrator recalls the traditional Goan baker (pader), a central figure in village life since Portuguese times. Bakers were known by the musical jingling of bamboo sticks announcing their arrival. Their bread — especially the sweet bread bol — was essential to every occasion. The baker had a prosperous, plump physique and dressed in the traditional knee-length frock (kabai). The narrative nostalgically portrays a vanishing tradition.

**Summary (Part II - Coorg):** Set in the Kodagu district of Karnataka, this piece describes Coorg as a heavenly land of coffee plantations, rainforests, and martial traditions. The Coorgi people, possibly of Greek or Arab ancestry, are known for their hospitality, valour (General Cariappa, first Indian Army Chief, was a Coorgi), and adventure tourism — river rafting, trekking, and the panoramic views from Brahmagiri hills.

**Summary (Part III - Tea from Assam):** Two friends, Pranjol and Rajvir, travel through Assam's lush tea gardens. Rajvir, an enthusiastic tea lover, shares fascinating facts about tea — its Chinese origins, various legends (Bodhidharma cutting his eyelids to stay awake), and the immense tea production of Assam. They arrive at Dhekiabari Tea Estate where Pranjol's father is the manager.

## Character List

- **The Goan Baker (Pader):** A traditional figure; central to village community life in Goa.
- **The Narrator (Part I):** A Goan reflecting on childhood memories of the village baker.
- **Pranjol:** A boy from Assam, whose father manages a tea estate.
- **Rajvir:** Pranjol's friend from Delhi; enthusiastic about tea, full of facts and stories.
- **General Cariappa:** Mentioned as a distinguished Coorgi, first Chief of the Indian Army.

## Key Quotes

1. "The baker or the pader was an essential part of the village."
2. "The jingling thud of his bamboo woke us up."
3. "Coorg is a piece of heaven that must have drifted from the kingdom of God."
4. "We have an Indian legend too about tea. Bodhidharma, an ancient Buddhist ascetic, cut off his eyelids because he felt sleepy during meditation."
5. "Tea really is very popular!"
