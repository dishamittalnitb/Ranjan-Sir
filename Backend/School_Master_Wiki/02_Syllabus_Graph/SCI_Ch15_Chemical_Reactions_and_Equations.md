# Chapter 15: Chemical Reactions and Equations

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Chemical Reactions
- A process in which one or more substances (reactants) are converted into new substances (products) with different properties.
- **Indicators of a chemical reaction:**
  - Change in state
  - Change in colour
  - Evolution of gas
  - Change in temperature
  - Formation of precipitate

### 1.2 Chemical Equations
- A symbolic representation of a chemical reaction.
- **Skeletal equation:** Unbalanced equation showing chemical formulae.
- **Balanced equation:** Number of atoms of each element is equal on both sides (Law of Conservation of Mass).
- **Balancing rules:** Adjust coefficients only; never change subscripts.

### 1.3 Types of Chemical Reactions
| Type | Definition | Generic Form | Example |
|------|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Combination** | Two or more substances combine to form a single product | A + B --> C | CaO + H2O --> Ca(OH)2; C + O2 --> CO2 |
| **Decomposition** | A single compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances | AB --> A + B | CaCO3 --heat--> CaO + CO2; 2FeSO4 --heat--> Fe2O3 + SO2 + SO3 |
| **Displacement** | A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound | A + BC --> AC + B | Fe + CuSO4 --> FeSO4 + Cu; Zn + CuSO4 --> ZnSO4 + Cu |
| **Double Displacement** | Ions of two compounds exchange places to form two new compounds | AB + CD --> AD + CB | Na2SO4 + BaCl2 --> BaSO4 + 2NaCl |
| **Precipitation** | A reaction that produces an insoluble solid (precipitate) | Sub-type of double displacement | AgNO3 + NaCl --> AgCl (pp) + NaNO3 |

### 1.4 Endothermic and Exothermic Reactions
| Endothermic | Exothermic |
|------------|------------|
| Absorbs heat energy | Releases heat energy |
| Surroundings get colder | Surroundings get warmer |
| e.g., CaCO3 --heat--> CaO + CO2; Photosynthesis | e.g., CH4 + 2O2 --> CO2 + 2H2O + Heat; Respiration; Quicklime + water |

### 1.5 Oxidation and Reduction (Redox Reactions)
- **Oxidation:** Gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen by a substance.
  - e.g., 2Cu + O2 --heat--> 2CuO (Cu is oxidised)
- **Reduction:** Loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen by a substance.
  - e.g., CuO + H2 --heat--> Cu + H2O (CuO is reduced)
- **Redox reaction:** A reaction in which both oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously.
  - Oxidising agent: The substance that causes oxidation (is itself reduced).
  - Reducing agent: The substance that causes reduction (is itself oxidised).

### 1.6 Effects of Oxidation in Daily Life
- **Corrosion:** Slow destruction of metals by the action of air, moisture, acids, etc. (e.g., rusting of iron - Fe2O3.xH2O; tarnishing of silver - Ag2S; green coating on copper - CuCO3.Cu(OH)2).
- **Rancidity:** Oxidation of fats and oils in food, causing unpleasant smell and taste. Prevented by: adding antioxidants (BHA, BHT), flushing with nitrogen gas, refrigeration, vacuum packing.

## 2. Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Chemical Reaction** | Transformation of substances into new substances with different properties. |
| **Reactant** | Substance that takes part in a chemical reaction. |
| **Product** | New substance formed as a result of a chemical reaction. |
| **Balanced Equation** | Chemical equation with equal number of atoms of each element on both sides. |
| **Oxidation** | Gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen. |
| **Reduction** | Loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen. |
| **Precipitate** | Insoluble solid formed during a chemical reaction. |
| **Corrosion** | Gradual destruction of metals due to environmental exposure. |
| **Rancidity** | Spoilage of fats/oils due to oxidation. |
