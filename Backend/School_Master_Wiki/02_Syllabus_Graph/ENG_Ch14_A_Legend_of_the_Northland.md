# Chapter 14: A Legend of the Northland

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** This ballad-style poem teaches a moral lesson about greed and selfishness through a folk legend. It conveys that selfishness is punished while generosity is rewarded. The poem also reflects the characteristics of a legend — a traditional story passed down through generations, often with a supernatural element.

**Summary:** The poem narrates a legend from the Northland, a cold region where days are short and nights long. Saint Peter, while traveling and preaching, feels faint from hunger and asks a woman baking cakes for one. The woman, though she has plenty, refuses each time — first the large cake, then the medium, and finally even a tiny cake that she thought was too big to give away. Enraged by her greed, Saint Peter curses her, saying she is unfit for human form and must build her own home and search for food by boring into wood. The woman transforms into a woodpecker, flying out of the chimney. The poem ends with the observation that even today, one can see her in the forest, still boring into wood for food.

## Literary Devices

- **Rhyme Scheme:** ABAB (alternating rhyme) quatrains, typical of ballads.
- **Ballad Form:** Narrative poem telling a story with a moral, short stanzas, and repetition.
- **Alliteration:** "faint with fasting," "boring and boring," "scarlet cap"
- **Repetition:** "boring and boring for food" — emphasizes the eternal nature of the punishment.
- **Simile:** The child compares the woman's clothes burning "like a flame."
- **Imagery:** Vivid descriptions of Northland (snow, reindeer, sledges) and the woman's transformation.
- **Tone:** Didactic, folk-like, cautionary.

## Key Quotes

1. "Away, away in the Northland, / Where the hours of the day are few"
2. "And being faint with fasting, / For the day was almost done, / He asked her, from her store of cakes, / To give him a single one."
3. "Then she took a tiny scrap of dough, / And rolled and rolled it flat"
4. "You shall build as the birds do, / And shall get your scanty food / By boring, and boring, and boring, / All day in the hard, dry wood."
