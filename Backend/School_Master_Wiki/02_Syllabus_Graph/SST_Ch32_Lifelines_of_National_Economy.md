# Chapter 32: Lifelines of National Economy

**[MAP POINTING ONLY IN BOARD EXAMINATION]**
This chapter is evaluated through map pointing only in the Board Examination. It is also part of the Interdisciplinary Project with History Chapter 3 (The Making of a Global World) and Economics Chapter 4 (Globalisation and the Indian Economy), assessed internally for 5 marks.

## Learning Outcomes
Refer to Annexure III-B of the syllabus. Key outcomes include:
- Analyse the implication of globalisation for local economies.
- Discuss how globalisation is experienced differently by different social groups.
- Enumerate how transportation works as a lifeline of the economy.
- Analyse and infer the impact of roadways and railways on the national economy.
- Analyse and infer the challenges faced by the roadways and railway sector in India.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Transport: The Lifeline**: An efficient transport network is critical for the movement of goods and people, and thus for economic development. Modes -- roadways, railways, waterways, airways, and pipelines.
2. **Roadways**:
   - India has the second-largest road network in the world (about 62 lakh km).
   - Golden Quadrilateral (5,846 km): Connecting Delhi-Mumbai-Chennai-Kolkata.
   - North-South and East-West Corridors.
   - Border Roads Organisation (BRO) builds and maintains roads in strategic border areas.
3. **Railways**:
   - India has one of the largest railway networks in the world (about 67,000 km).
   - Railways are the principal mode for freight and long-distance passenger travel.
   - Challenges: delayed projects, ticket-less travel, accidents, aging infrastructure.
4. **Waterways**:
   - Cheapest mode of transport.
   - National Waterways: NW-1 (Ganga-Bhagirathi-Hugli), NW-2 (Brahmaputra), NW-3 (West Coast Canal, Kerala).
5. **Airways**: Fastest and most expensive mode. Pawan Hans Helicopter Limited provides services to remote areas. UDAN scheme promotes regional air connectivity.
6. **Pipelines**: Used for oil, gas, and water transport. Major pipelines: HBJ gas pipeline, Jamnagar-Loni LPG pipeline.
7. **Major Sea Ports**: Kandla, Mumbai, Marmagao (Goa), New Mangalore, Kochi, Tuticorin, Chennai, Visakhapatnam, Paradip, Haldia (Kolkata).
8. **International Airports**: Amritsar, Delhi (IGI), Mumbai (CSIA), Chennai, Kolkata (NSCB), Hyderabad (RGIA).
9. **Communication**: India has one of the largest postal networks and telephone networks. The internet and mobile communication have expanded rapidly.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Golden Quadrilateral**: A network of four/six-lane highways connecting Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, and Kolkata.
- **National Highway**: Major highways connecting important cities across states, maintained by NHAI.
- **Broad Gauge**: The standard railway track width in India (1.676 m).
- **BRO (Border Roads Organisation)**: An organization that constructs and maintains roads in border regions.
- **Hinterland**: The inland region served by a port.
- **Pipeline Transport**: The movement of liquids and gases through pipes.
- **UDAN (Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik)**: A government scheme to make air travel affordable and connect regional airports.
- **SEZ (Special Economic Zone)**: A designated area with special economic regulations to attract foreign investment.
