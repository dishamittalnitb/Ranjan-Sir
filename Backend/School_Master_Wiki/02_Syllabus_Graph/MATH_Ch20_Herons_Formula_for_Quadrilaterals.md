# Chapter 20: Heron's Formula for Quadrilaterals [FORMATIVE ONLY]

## Concept Summary

This formative-only topic extends Heron's formula to find areas of quadrilaterals:

1. **Application of Heron's Formula**: A quadrilateral can be divided into two triangles by drawing a diagonal. The area of the quadrilateral is then the sum of the areas of the two triangles, each found using Heron's formula.

2. **Requirements**: All four sides and one diagonal (or both diagonals) of the quadrilateral must be known. Alternatively, all four sides and one angle.

3. **Procedure**:
   - Divide the quadrilateral into two triangles using a diagonal
   - Calculate the area of each triangle using Heron's formula
   - Add the two areas

4. **Applications**: 
   - Finding area of irregular quadrilateral plots/fields
   - Practical surveying problems

## Key Formulas/Theorems

**Heron's Formula** (for each triangle):
- s = (a + b + c) / 2
- Area = sqrt(s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c))

**Area of Quadrilateral ABCD** (with diagonal AC):
- Triangle ABC: sides a, b, AC
- Triangle ADC: sides c, d, AC
- Area(ABCD) = Area(ABC) + Area(ADC)

**Special Cases**:
- Rhombus: Can also use (1/2) * d1 * d2
- Trapezium: Can also use (1/2) * (sum of parallel sides) * height
