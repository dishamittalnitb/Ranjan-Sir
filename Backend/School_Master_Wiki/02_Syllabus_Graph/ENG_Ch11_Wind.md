# Chapter 11: Wind

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The poem uses wind as a metaphor for life's hardships and adversities. It emphasizes that strength, resilience, and preparation are necessary to face challenges — the weak crumble, while the strong become stronger. The poet ultimately advocates befriending the wind (adversity) rather than fighting it.

**Summary:** The poet directly addresses the wind, asking it to "come softly" and not break the shutters of the windows, scatter papers, or throw down books. He describes how the wind destroys everything weak — crumbling houses, doors, rafters, even weak hearts and bodies. But the poet realizes that complaining is futile. Instead, he resolves to build strong homes, firm bodies, and steadfast hearts. The wind, which extinguishes a weak fire, makes a strong fire "roar and flourish." The poem concludes that friendship with the wind is good, but one must be strong to earn that friendship.

## Literary Devices

- **Rhyme Scheme:** Free verse (no fixed rhyme scheme)
- **Personification:** The wind is addressed as a living entity ("Wind, come softly").
- **Metaphor:** Wind = difficulties/challenges of life.
- **Anaphora:** Repeated use of "You..." at the beginning of lines ("You tore the pages...", "You brought rain...")
- **Alliteration:** "weak windows," "crumbling crumbling"
- **Symbolism:** "Fire" symbolizes willpower/ambition; "strong homes" symbolize inner strength.
- **Tone:** Begins pleading, shifts to defiant and resolute.

## Key Quotes

1. "Wind, come softly. / Don't break the shutters of the windows."
2. "He makes strong fires roar and flourish."
3. "The wind blows out weak fires. / He makes strong fires roar and flourish."
4. "Let's make the heart steadfast."
