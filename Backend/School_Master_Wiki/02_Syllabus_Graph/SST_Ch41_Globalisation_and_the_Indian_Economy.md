# Chapter 41: Globalisation and the Indian Economy

**[EXAM + INTERDISCIPLINARY PROJECT]**
Exam subtopics: "What is Globalisation?" and "Factors that have enabled Globalisation." Remaining subtopics (Production across countries, Chinese toys in India, WTO, Struggle for a Fair Globalisation) are part of the Interdisciplinary Project, internally assessed for 5 marks.

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Enumerate the concept of globalisation and its definition, evolution, and impact on the global economy.
- Evaluate the key role of the major drivers of globalisation and their role in shaping the global economic landscape in various countries.
- Comprehend the significance of the role of G20 and its significance in the light of India's role.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **What is Globalisation?** (Board Exam):
   - Globalisation is the increasing integration and interconnection between countries through trade, investment, technology, and movement of people.
   - It results in the creation of a single global market where goods, services, capital, and labour move freely across borders.
   - **MNCs (Multinational Corporations)**: Companies that own or control production in more than one country. They set up production where costs are low and markets are near.
2. **Factors Enabling Globalisation** (Board Exam):
   - **Technology**: Rapid advancement in ICT (Information and Communication Technology) -- internet, mobile phones, satellite communication.
   - **Transportation**: Faster and cheaper air travel, container shipping.
   - **Liberalisation**: Removal of trade barriers by governments. In India, the New Economic Policy of 1991 liberalized trade and investment rules, reduced import tariffs, and opened sectors to foreign investment.
   - **WTO (World Trade Organization)**: An international body promoting free trade by reducing trade barriers among member countries.
3. **Production Across Countries** (IDP):
   - MNCs spread production by buying local companies, placing orders with small producers, and setting up joint ventures.
   - Example: Ford Motors in India, Chinese toys flooding Indian markets.
4. **Impact of Globalisation on India** (IDP):
   - Positive: Greater choice for consumers, increased foreign investment, growth of IT and service sectors, higher economic growth, job creation in certain sectors.
   - Negative: Increased competition for small producers (e.g., toy industry, handloom), loss of livelihoods, exploitation of workers in unorganised sectors, growing inequality.
5. **World Trade Organisation (WTO)** (IDP):
   - Founded in 1995; promotes international trade by reducing tariffs and non-tariff barriers.
   - Criticized for biases towards developed countries and for not adequately protecting developing countries' interests.
6. **Struggle for a Fair Globalisation** (IDP):
   - Need for fair trade rules, protection of labour rights, environmental standards.
   - Role of government in providing safety nets, skill development, and infrastructure.
   - People's movements and NGOs advocating for equitable globalization.
7. **India's Role in G20**: India assumed the G20 presidency in 2023, highlighting its growing role in shaping the global economic agenda.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Globalisation**: The process of increasing integration and interconnection of economies, cultures, and populations worldwide.
- **MNC (Multinational Corporation)**: A company that operates and controls production in more than one country.
- **Liberalisation**: The removal or reduction of government restrictions on economic activities and trade.
- **Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)**: Investment by a company based in one country into a company based in another country.
- **Trade Barrier**: Any government regulation that restricts international trade (e.g., tariffs, quotas).
- **WTO (World Trade Organization)**: An international body that deals with global rules of trade between nations.
- **Privatization**: The transfer of ownership of businesses from the public sector to the private sector.
- **Special Economic Zones (SEZs)**: Designated areas with liberal economic and trade laws to attract foreign investment.
- **G20**: An international forum of 19 countries and the European Union, representing the world's major economies.
