# Chapter 25: Electricity

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Electric Current
- The rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor.
- **I = Q / t** (I = current in Amperes, Q = charge in Coulombs, t = time in seconds).
- **SI unit:** Ampere (A). 1 A = 1 C/s (flow of 1 coulomb of charge per second).
- Direction: Conventionally, the direction opposite to the flow of electrons (from positive to negative terminal).

### 1.2 Electric Potential and Potential Difference
- **Electric potential:** Work done to bring a unit positive charge from infinity to a point. Unit: Volt (V).
- **Potential difference:** Work done to move a unit charge between two points: **V = W / Q**.
  - 1 V = 1 J/C.
- Measured with a **voltmeter** connected in **parallel**.

### 1.3 Ohm's Law
- At constant temperature, the current (I) flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference (V) across its ends.
- **V = I R** or **V/I = constant = R**
- **Resistance (R):** The property of a conductor to oppose the flow of current. Unit: Ohm (Omega).
  - 1 Ohm = 1 V / 1 A.
- Factors on which resistance depends:
  - **Length (l):** R directly proportional to l
  - **Cross-sectional area (A):** R inversely proportional to A
  - **Material (resistivity rho):** R = rho (l / A)
  - **Temperature:** For metals, resistance increases with temperature.

### 1.4 Resistivity
- The resistance of a conductor of unit length and unit cross-sectional area.
- **rho = R (A / l)**
- SI unit: Ohm-metre.
- Resistivity is a characteristic property of the material (independent of shape/size).
- **Alloys** (e.g., nichrome) have higher resistivity than their constituent metals. They do NOT oxidise easily at high temperatures (used in heating elements).

### 1.5 Combination of Resistors

#### Series Combination
- Resistors connected end-to-end. Same current flows through all.
- **R_eq = R1 + R2 + R3 + ...** (Equivalent resistance = sum of all resistances).
- Total V across combination = sum of voltage drops across each resistor: V = V1 + V2 + V3.
- If one component fails, the entire circuit breaks (e.g., series-connected decorative lights - disadvantage).

#### Parallel Combination
- Resistors connected across the same two points. Same voltage across all.
- **1/R_eq = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3 + ...**
- Total current = sum of currents through each branch: I = I1 + I2 + I3.
- Equivalent resistance is always LESS than the smallest individual resistance.
- If one component fails, others continue to work (e.g., household wiring - advantage).

### 1.6 Heating Effect of Electric Current
- When electric current passes through a conductor, heat is produced (Joule heating / Joule's Law).
- **H = I^2 R t**
  - H = heat produced (Joules), I = current (A), R = resistance (Ohm), t = time (s).
- Also can be written as: H = V I t = (V^2 / R) t

#### Applications of Heating Effect
- **Electric bulb:** Filament (tungsten - high MP ~3380 degree C) heats up to emit light. Bulb filled with inert gas (N2, Ar) to prevent oxidation.
- **Electric fuse:** Safety device; melts and breaks the circuit when excessive current flows (thin wire of low MP, e.g., tin-lead alloy).
- **Electric iron / Heater / Toaster:** Nichrome coil produces heat.
- **Short circuit:** When live and neutral wires come into direct contact; very high current; can cause fire.
- **Overloading:** When too many appliances are connected simultaneously, drawing current beyond the capacity of the circuit.

### 1.7 Electric Power
- The rate at which electrical energy is consumed or dissipated.
- **P = W / t = V I = I^2 R = V^2 / R**
- SI unit: Watt (W). 1 W = 1 J/s = 1 VA.
- Larger units: Kilowatt (kW) = 1000 W; Megawatt (MW) = 10^6 W.
- Interrelation: P = V I, P = I^2 R, P = V^2 / R.

## 6. Practical Experiments (from CBSE Syllabus)

**Experiment 4 (Unit-IV):** Studying the dependence of potential difference (V) across a resistor on the current (I) passing through it and determine its resistance. Also plotting a graph between V and I.
- **Objective:** Verify Ohm's law experimentally.
- **Apparatus:** Resistor, ammeter (series), voltmeter (parallel), battery, rheostat, connecting wires.
- **Key Learning:** V-I graph is a straight line passing through origin (Ohmic conductor). Slope = resistance.

**Experiment 5 (Unit-IV):** Determination of the equivalent resistance of two resistors when connected in series and parallel.
- **Objective:** Experimentally verify: R_series = R1 + R2 and 1/R_parallel = 1/R1 + 1/R2.
