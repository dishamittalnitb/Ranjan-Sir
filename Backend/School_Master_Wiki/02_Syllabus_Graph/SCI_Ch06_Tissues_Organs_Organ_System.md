# Chapter 06: Tissues, Organs, Organ System, Organism

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Levels of Organization
Cells --> Tissues --> Organs --> Organ Systems --> Organism

A **tissue** is a group of similar cells that work together to perform a specific function.

### 1.2 Plant Tissues
Plant tissues are broadly classified as:

#### A. Meristematic Tissue (Dividing Tissue)
- Cells actively divide; small, cuboidal, with dense cytoplasm, thin cellulose walls, no intercellular spaces.
- **Location:** Root tips, shoot tips (apical meristem); base of leaves/intercalary (intercalary meristem); girth increase (lateral meristem/cambium).
- **Function:** Growth in length and girth.

#### B. Permanent Tissue (Non-dividing)
Formed from meristematic tissue when cells lose the ability to divide.

| Type | Sub-type | Structure | Function | Location |
|------|----------|-----------|----------|----------|
| **Simple Permanent** | Parenchyma | Living cells; thin cell walls; large intercellular spaces | Storage, photosynthesis (chlorenchyma), buoyancy (aerenchyma) | Cortex, pith, mesophyll |
| | Collenchyma | Living cells; unevenly thickened cell walls at corners | Mechanical support, flexibility | Below epidermis in stems, petioles |
| | Sclerenchyma | Dead cells; thick lignified walls; no intercellular spaces | Strength and rigidity | Coconut husk, seed coats, veins of leaves |
| **Complex Permanent** | Xylem (conducts water & minerals) | Tracheids, vessels (dead), xylem parenchyma (living), xylem fibres (dead) | Transport of water and minerals upward from roots | Throughout plant |
| | Phloem (conducts food) | Sieve tubes, companion cells (living), phloem parenchyma (living), phloem fibres (dead) | Transport of food (sucrose) bidirectionally | Throughout plant |

#### Epidermis (Protective Tissue)
- Outermost layer of cells; single layer; covered with cutin (cuticle) to reduce water loss.
- In roots, epidermis has root hairs for absorption.
- Stomata: Pores in leaf epidermis for gaseous exchange and transpiration; guard cells regulate opening/closing.

### 1.3 Animal Tissues
Only four types of tissues in animals are part of the syllabus:

#### A. Epithelial Tissue (Covering/Protective)
| Type | Structure | Function | Location |
|------|-----------|----------|----------|
| **Squamous** | Flat, thin cells | Diffusion, filtration | Alveoli, blood vessels |
| **Cuboidal** | Cube-shaped cells | Secretion, absorption | Kidney tubules, glands |
| **Columnar** | Tall, pillar-like cells | Secretion, absorption | Lining of stomach, intestine |
| **Ciliated Columnar** | Columnar cells with cilia | Movement of mucus/substances | Respiratory tract |
| **Stratified** | Multiple layers | Protection | Skin, oesophagus |

#### B. Connective Tissue (Support/Connect)
| Type | Structure | Function |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Blood** | Fluid matrix (plasma) with RBC, WBC, platelets | Transport of gases, nutrients, immunity, clotting |
| **Bone** | Hard matrix with calcium/phosphorus salts | Support, protection, movement |
| **Cartilage** | Solid, flexible matrix; cells (chondrocytes) in lacunae | Flexibility, cushioning (nose, ear, trachea, joints) |
| **Tendon** | Dense, fibrous; connects muscle to bone | Transmits force |
| **Ligament** | Elastic, fibrous; connects bone to bone | Provides flexibility at joints |
| **Areolar** | Loose, gel-like matrix; fibroblasts, macrophages | Packing tissue, repair |
| **Adipose** | Fat-storing cells | Insulation, energy storage |

#### C. Muscular Tissue (Movement)
| Type | Structure | Function | Control |
|------|-----------|----------|---------|
| **Striated (Skeletal)** | Long, cylindrical, unbranched, multinucleate, striations visible | Voluntary movement | Voluntary |
| **Smooth (Unstriated)** | Spindle-shaped, uninucleate, no striations | Involuntary movements (peristalsis, etc.) | Involuntary |
| **Cardiac** | Cylindrical, branched, uninucleate, striated, intercalated discs | Rhythmic contraction of heart | Involuntary |

#### D. Nervous Tissue (Control/Coordination)
- **Neuron (Nerve cell):** Basic unit. Consists of cell body (cyton/soma), dendrites (receive impulses), axon (conducts impulses away).
- Function: Reception and transmission of electrochemical signals.

## 2. Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Tissue** | Group of similar cells performing a specific function. |
| **Meristematic Tissue** | Actively dividing plant tissue; responsible for growth. |
| **Permanent Tissue** | Differentiated tissue that has lost the ability to divide. |
| **Xylem** | Complex plant tissue; transports water and minerals upward. |
| **Phloem** | Complex plant tissue; transports food bidirectionally. |
| **Stomata** | Microscopic pores on leaf surface for gas exchange and transpiration. |
| **Neuron** | Structural and functional unit of the nervous system. |

## 3. Board Exam Weightage & Question Pattern

Falls under **Unit II: Organization in the Living World (22 marks)**.

- **VSA (1 mark):** Name tissue type from description; identify muscle type; define differentiation.
- **SA (2-3 marks):** Differentiate types of tissues (e.g., parenchyma vs sclerenchyma, bone vs cartilage); differentiate xylem and phloem; explain structure of neuron.
- **LA (5 marks):** Classify plant tissues with diagrams; classify animal tissues with examples; describe types of muscular tissue.
- **Diagram-based:** Neuron, types of plant tissues, muscle fibres, stomata.
