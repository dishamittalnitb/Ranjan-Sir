# Chapter 39: Sectors of the Indian Economy

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Analyse and infer how the economic activities in different sectors contribute to the overall growth and development of the Indian economy.
- Propose solutions to identified problems in different sectors based on their understanding.
- Summarise how the organised and unorganised sectors are providing employment.
- Enumerate the role of the unorganised sector in impacting Per Capita Income currently and propose suggestive steps to reduce the unorganised sector for more productive contributions to GDP.
- Enumerate and infer the essential role of the Public and Private sectors.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Sectors of the Economy**:
   - **Primary Sector**: Activities that directly use natural resources -- agriculture, fishing, forestry, mining. Also called the agricultural sector.
   - **Secondary Sector**: Activities that transform natural products into manufactured goods -- industries, construction. Also called the industrial sector.
   - **Tertiary Sector**: Activities that provide services -- banking, transport, education, health, communication. Also called the service sector.
2. **Comparison of Sectors**: In India, the primary sector employs the largest share of the workforce (about 45-50%), while the tertiary sector contributes the largest share to GDP (about 55%). This indicates disguised unemployment in agriculture.
3. **Organised vs Unorganised Sector**:
   - **Organised Sector**: Registered enterprises with formal employment, regular salary, job security, and benefits (PF, pension, leave, medical). Examples: government jobs, large private companies.
   - **Unorganised Sector**: Small, unregistered enterprises; informal employment; irregular wages, no job security or benefits. Examples: agricultural labourers, street vendors, domestic workers, small shops.
   - **Challenges**: Over 90% of India's workforce is in the unorganised sector, contributing less to GDP and lacking social security.
4. **Public and Private Sectors**:
   - **Public Sector**: Owned and operated by the government. Objective is social welfare, not just profit. Examples: Railways, Post Office, BSNL, SAIL.
   - **Private Sector**: Owned and operated by private individuals/companies. Objective is profit. Examples: Reliance, TCS, Infosys.
   - **Mixed Economy**: India follows a mixed economy with both public and private sectors coexisting.
5. **Shift in Sectors**:
   - Historically, economies shift from primary to secondary to tertiary dominance.
   - In India, the tertiary sector has grown rapidly due to IT, banking, and services.
   - However, the agricultural workforce has not shifted proportionately -- structural transformation is incomplete.
6. **Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MNREGA, 2005)**:
   - Guarantees 100 days of wage employment in a year to every rural household whose adult members are willing to do unskilled manual work.
   - Provides social security and creates productive assets in rural areas.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Primary Sector**: Activities related to extraction and production of natural resources.
- **Secondary Sector**: Activities related to manufacturing and processing.
- **Tertiary Sector**: Activities that provide services.
- **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)**: The total value of goods and services produced in a country.
- **Organised Sector**: Enterprises with formal employment and legal protection.
- **Unorganised Sector**: Unregistered, small-scale enterprises with informal employment.
- **Disguised Unemployment**: A situation where more people work than required (marginal productivity near zero).
- **Public Sector**: Industries/services owned and run by the government.
- **Private Sector**: Industries/services owned and run by private individuals.
- **Mixed Economy**: An economic system combining public and private sectors.
