# Chapter 27: Nelson Mandela - Long Walk to Freedom

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The chapter is an autobiographical account of Nelson Mandela's inauguration as South Africa's first Black president. It explores themes of freedom, equality, racial discrimination, courage, and reconciliation. Mandela reflects on how apartheid oppressed both the oppressed and the oppressor, and defines true freedom as being able to live with dignity and respect.

**Summary:** The extract opens with Mandela's inauguration on 10 May 1994 at the Union Buildings in Pretoria, attended by world dignitaries. Mandela speaks of the birth of a new South African democracy, free from the brutal apartheid system. He pays tribute to the thousands of South Africans who sacrificed for this moment. He reflects on the meaning of freedom — not just for himself but for all his people — and how his understanding of freedom evolved from personal freedoms of boyhood to the collective freedom of a nation. He also acknowledges the two national anthems sung at the ceremony — "Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika" and "Die Stem" — symbolizing unity between Blacks and Whites. The chapter ends with Mandela's famous line about the twin obligations every person has — to family and to the community/country.

## Character List

- **Nelson Mandela:** First Black President of South Africa; a leader of the anti-apartheid movement; believes in reconciliation, not revenge.
- **Zenani (Mandela's daughter):** Accompanied him at the inauguration.
- **Thabo Mbeki, F.W. de Klerk:** First and second Deputy Presidents respectively.
- **South African people:** Both Black and White citizens witnessing the birth of a new nation.

## Key Quotes

1. "The policy of apartheid created a deep and lasting wound in my country and my people."
2. "The oppressed and the oppressor alike are robbed of their humanity."
3. "Freedom is indivisible; the chains on anyone of my people were the chains on all of them."
4. "A man who takes away another man's freedom is a prisoner of hatred."
5. "I knew that the oppressor must be liberated just as surely as the oppressed."
