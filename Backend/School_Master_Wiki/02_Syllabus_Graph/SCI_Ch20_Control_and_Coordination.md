# Chapter 20: Control and Coordination

## 1. Concept Summary

Control and coordination in living organisms is achieved through two systems:
- **Nervous system** (rapid, electrical, short-lived) - in animals
- **Chemical coordination** (slow, chemical, long-lasting) - hormones in both plants and animals

### 1.2 Tropic Movements in Plants
Directional growth responses of plants to external stimuli.

| Tropism | Stimulus | Response | Example |
|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| **Phototropism** | Light | Shoot bends toward light (positive); root away from light (negative) | Shoot tip growing toward window |
| **Geotropism (Gravitropism)** | Gravity | Root grows downward (positive); shoot grows upward (negative) | Root penetrating soil |
| **Hydrotropism** | Water | Roots grow toward water (positive) | Roots growing toward moisture |
| **Chemotropism** | Chemicals | Growth response to chemicals | Pollen tube growth toward ovule |
| **Thigmotropism** | Touch | Growth response to touch | Tendrils of pea/grapevine wrapping around support |

- **Mechanism:** Mediated by plant hormones (auxins). In phototropism, auxin moves to the shaded side, causing cells there to elongate more, bending the shoot toward light.

**Nastic movements:** Non-directional responses (e.g., folding of Mimosa pudica/touch-me-not leaves on touch - thigmonasty; opening/closing of flowers - photonasty).

### 1.3 Plant Hormones (Phytohormones)
| Hormone | Function |
|---------|----------|
| **Auxin** | Promotes cell elongation; promotes apical dominance; involved in phototropism and geotropism. |
| **Gibberellins** | Promote stem elongation, seed germination, flowering, fruit growth. |
| **Cytokinins** | Promote cell division (cytokinesis); delay leaf senescence (ageing). |
| **Abscisic Acid (ABA)** | Growth inhibitor; causes wilting/leaf fall (abscission); promotes seed dormancy; closes stomata during stress. Synthesised in response to adverse conditions. |
| **Ethylene (Gaseous)** | Promotes fruit ripening; promotes leaf and flower senescence. |

### 1.4 Nervous System in Animals
The nervous system provides rapid communication and coordination.

#### Structure of a Neuron
- **Dendrites:** Short, branched extensions that receive impulses and transmit toward the cell body.
- **Cell body (Cyton/Soma):** Contains nucleus and cytoplasm.
- **Axon:** Long, single fibre that carries impulses away from the cell body.
- **Myelin sheath:** Insulating layer around axon (formed by Schwann cells); speeds up impulse transmission.
- **Synaptic knob/Terminal buttons:** End of axon; releases neurotransmitter chemicals.

#### Types of Neurons
- **Sensory (Afferent) Neuron:** Carries impulses from receptor (sense organ) to CNS.
- **Motor (Efferent) Neuron:** Carries impulses from CNS to effector (muscle/gland).
- **Relay (Interneuron/Association) Neuron:** Connects sensory and motor neurons within CNS.

#### Synapse
- The junction between two neurons.
- Electrical impulse travelling along axon triggers release of neurotransmitter (chemical) from synaptic knob.
- Neurotransmitter diffuses across synapse and binds to receptors on the next neuron, generating a new electrical impulse.
- **Significance:** Ensures one-way transmission of nerve impulses.

#### Reflex Action and Reflex Arc
- **Reflex action:** An involuntary, automatic, rapid response to a stimulus (e.g., withdrawing hand from a hot object, knee-jerk reflex, sneezing, coughing).
- **Reflex arc:** The neural pathway followed during a reflex action:
  - Receptor (senses stimulus) --> Sensory Neuron --> Spinal Cord (or Brain) --> Motor Neuron --> Effector (Muscle/Gland)
- Reflex arcs are protective and allow quick responses without conscious thought.

#### Human Nervous System
| Division | Parts | Function |
|----------|-------|----------|
| **Central Nervous System (CNS)** | Brain and Spinal Cord | Processing centre; integration of information |
| **Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)** | Cranial nerves (12 pairs), Spinal nerves (31 pairs) | Communication links between CNS and the rest of the body |

#### Brain (Major Parts)
| Part | Location | Functions |
|------|----------|-----------|
| **Forebrain (Cerebrum)** | Largest part | Thinking, memory, intelligence, consciousness, voluntary actions; sensory reception (sight, sound, smell, touch, taste). |
| **Midbrain** | Below cerebrum | Controls reflex movements of head, neck, and eyes. |
| **Hindbrain** | Back of brain | Includes: **Cerebellum** (balance, coordination, precise voluntary movements); **Medulla Oblongata** (involuntary actions: breathing, heartbeat, blood pressure, sneezing, vomiting, coughing); **Pons** (relay centre; regulates respiration). |

### 1.5 Voluntary, Involuntary, and Reflex Actions
| Type | Control | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Voluntary** | Conscious control; cerebrum | Walking, writing, speaking |
| **Involuntary** | No conscious control; medulla | Heartbeat, breathing, digestion |
| **Reflex** | Automatic, rapid; spinal cord (sometimes brain) | Knee jerk, withdrawing hand from hot object |

### 1.6 Chemical Coordination - Animal Hormones
Hormones are chemical messengers secreted by endocrine glands, transported via blood, and act on target organs.

| Gland | Hormone(s) | Function | Deficiency Disorder | Excess Disorder |
|-------|-----------|----------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Pituitary (Master gland)** | Growth Hormone (GH), TSH, ACTH, etc. | Regulates growth; controls other glands. | Dwarfism (childhood GH deficiency) | Gigantism; Acromegaly |
| **Thyroid** | Thyroxine (T3, T4) | Regulates metabolism (carbohydrate, protein, fat). Contains iodine. | Goitre (iodine deficiency); Cretinism (in childhood) | Hyperthyroidism (exophathalmic goitre) |
| **Parathyroid** | Parathormone (PTH) | Regulates calcium and phosphate levels in blood. | Tetany (low Ca2+) | - |
| **Pancreas** | Insulin; Glucagon | Insulin lowers blood sugar; Glucagon raises blood sugar. | Diabetes mellitus (high blood sugar) | Hypoglycemia |
| **Adrenal** | Adrenaline (Epinephrine) | "Fight or flight" hormone; increases heart rate, breathing rate, blood flow to muscles. | - | Tachycardia |
| **Testes (Male)** | Testosterone | Development of secondary sexual characters, sperm production. | - | - |
| **Ovaries (Female)** | Oestrogen; Progesterone | Oestrogen: development of secondary female characters. Progesterone: maintains pregnancy. | - | - |

**Note on Iodised Salt:** Iodised salt contains potassium iodide. Iodine is essential for thyroxine production. Iodine deficiency causes goitre (enlarged thyroid gland).

**Feedback Mechanism:** Hormone secretion is regulated by feedback. E.g., High blood sugar --> Insulin secretion --> Blood sugar lowers --> Insulin secretion stops.

## 2. Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Tropism** | Directional growth movement of a plant part in response to a stimulus. |
| **Phytohormone** | Chemical substance (hormone) produced in a plant that regulates growth and development. |
| **Neuron** | Structural and functional unit of the nervous system. |
| **Synapse** | Junction between two neurons where signal is transmitted chemically. |
| **Reflex Action** | Involuntary, rapid, automatic response to a stimulus. |
| **Reflex Arc** | The neural pathway followed in a reflex action. |
| **Hormone** | Chemical messenger secreted by endocrine glands, transported via blood to target organs. |
| **Endocrine Gland** | Ductless gland that secretes hormones directly into the bloodstream. |
| **Feedback Mechanism** | Regulatory process where the output controls the activity (e.g., hormone secretion regulated by blood levels). |
