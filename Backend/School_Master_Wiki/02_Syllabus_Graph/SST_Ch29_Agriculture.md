# Chapter 29: Agriculture

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Examine the crucial role played by agriculture in our economy and society.
- Analyse the challenges faced by the farming community in India.
- Identify various aspects of agriculture, including crop production, types of farming, etc.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Importance of Agriculture**: Agriculture is the primary occupation for about 58% of India's population (2011). It contributes about 17% to India's GDP. It provides food security, raw materials for industries, and exports.
2. **Types of Farming in India**:
   - **Primitive Subsistence Farming**: Practiced on small patches with primitive tools; depends on monsoon (Jhum/shifting cultivation in NE, Bewar in MP, Podu in AP).
   - **Intensive Subsistence Farming**: High labour and inputs on small land plots; commonly practiced in densely populated areas.
   - **Commercial Farming**: Done for market sale; high doses of HYV seeds, fertilizers, pesticides (e.g., Punjab, Haryana).
   - **Plantation Farming**: Single crop on large estates; tea, coffee, rubber, sugarcane; mainly in Assam, WB, Kerala, Karnataka.
3. **Cropping Seasons**:
   - **Rabi (October to March)**: Sown in winter, harvested in spring. Major crops: wheat, barley, peas, gram, mustard.
   - **Kharif (June to September)**: Sown with monsoon, harvested in autumn. Major crops: rice, maize, jowar, bajra, cotton, jute.
   - **Zaid (April to June)**: Short season between rabi and kharif. Crops: watermelon, cucumber, vegetables, fodder.
4. **Major Crops**:
   - **Food Grains**: Rice (staple crop, requires high rainfall and temperature), Wheat (rabi, requires cool winter), Millets (Jowar, Bajra, Ragi -- dry regions), Maize (used for food and fodder), Pulses (protein source).
   - **Cash Crops**: Sugarcane (UP, Maharashtra), Oilseeds (groundnut, mustard, coconut), Cotton (black soil, Gujarat, Maharashtra), Jute (West Bengal, Assam), Tea (hills of Assam, WB), Coffee (Karnataka hills), Rubber (Kerala).
   - **Horticulture Crops**: Fruits and vegetables; India is a leading producer.
5. **Technological and Institutional Reforms**:
   - Green Revolution (1960s), White Revolution (milk), Land reforms (abolition of zamindari, consolidation of holdings).
   - Government schemes: Minimum Support Price (MSP), crop insurance (PM Fasal Bima Yojana), Kisan Credit Card, soil health card, agricultural extension services.
6. **Challenges**: Small and fragmented land holdings, dependence on monsoon, lack of modernization, insufficient irrigation, declining soil fertility, exploitation by middlemen, and lack of institutional credit.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Cropping Pattern**: The proportion of area under different crops at a point in time.
- **Rabi Crops**: Winter-sown crops harvested in spring.
- **Kharif Crops**: Monsoon-sown crops harvested in autumn.
- **Zaid Crops**: Short-duration crops grown between rabi and kharif seasons.
- **MSP (Minimum Support Price)**: The price at which the government purchases crops from farmers.
- **Green Revolution**: A large increase in food grain production from the late 1960s due to HYV seeds and modern inputs.
- **Shifting Cultivation**: Clearing forest land for cultivation and moving to a new plot when fertility declines.
- **Horticulture**: The cultivation of fruits, vegetables, and flowers.
