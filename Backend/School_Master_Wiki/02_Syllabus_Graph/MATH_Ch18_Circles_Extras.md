# Chapter 18: Circles Extras [FORMATIVE ONLY]

## Concept Summary

This formative-only topic introduces fundamental circle definitions and additional properties:

1. **Definition of Circle**: Through examples, arrive at the definition of a circle as a collection of all points in a plane that are at a fixed distance (radius) from a fixed point (center).

2. **Circle Terminology**:
   - **Radius**: Distance from center to any point on the circle
   - **Circumference**: The boundary of the circle
   - **Diameter**: A chord passing through the center; length = 2r
   - **Chord**: A line segment joining any two points on the circle
   - **Arc**: A portion of the circumference
   - **Sector**: Region bounded by two radii and an arc
   - **Segment**: Region bounded by a chord and an arc
   - **Secant**: A line that intersects the circle at two points
   - **Tangent**: A line that touches the circle at exactly one point
   - **Subtended Angle**: An angle formed at a point by lines from that point to the endpoints of a chord/arc

3. **Circle Through Three Non-Collinear Points** (stated without proof): There is one and only one circle passing through three given non-collinear points. This circle is called the circumcircle.

## Key Formulas/Theorems

**Diameter**: d = 2r

**Circumference**: C = 2 * pi * r

**Area of Circle**: A = pi * r^2

**Three Non-Collinear Points**: There exists exactly one circle passing through them.

**Collinear Points**: No circle passes through three collinear points (or understand as a circle of infinite radius).
