# Chapter 04: Coordinate Geometry (Class IX)

## Concept Summary

This chapter introduces the Cartesian coordinate system:

1. **The Cartesian Plane**: Rene Descartes' invention combining algebra and geometry. Two perpendicular number lines (axes) intersecting at the origin (0, 0). The horizontal axis is the x-axis, the vertical axis is the y-axis.

2. **Coordinates of a Point**: Every point P in the plane is represented by an ordered pair (x, y) where x is the x-coordinate (abscissa) and y is the y-coordinate (ordinate).

3. **Quadrants**: The axes divide the plane into four quadrants:
   - Quadrant I: (+, +)
   - Quadrant II: (-, +)
   - Quadrant III: (-, -)
   - Quadrant IV: (+, -)
   - Points on axes: (x, 0) or (0, y) -- not in any quadrant.

4. **Notations and Terminology**: Origin, axes, coordinates, abscissa, ordinate, quadrants.

## Key Formulas/Theorems

**Plotting a Point P(x, y)**: From the origin, move |x| units along the x-axis (right if x > 0, left if x < 0), then move |y| units parallel to the y-axis (up if y > 0, down if y < 0).

**Coordinates of Origin**: (0, 0)

**Points on Axes**:
- On x-axis: (x, 0) for any real x
- On y-axis: (0, y) for any real y
