# Chapter 48: A Question of Trust

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The story is an ironic tale about a thief who is himself deceived. It explores the themes of trust among thieves, the irony of a criminal being outwitted by another criminal, and the idea that there is indeed "honor among thieves" — though not necessarily in the way one expects.

**Summary:** Horace Danby is a successful lock-maker by profession, but secretly a thief who commits one safe-cracking burglary every year to fund his hobby of collecting rare books. One summer, while robbing a house at Shotover Grange, he is interrupted by a young, attractive woman who claims to be the lady of the house. She compliments his work, flatters him, and convinces him that she needs her jewels from the safe, but has forgotten the combination. Trusting the charming "lady," Horace opens the safe for her. She takes the jewels and leaves. Two days later, Horace is arrested — his fingerprints are all over the safe. He realizes the "lady" was also a thief who had tricked him. In prison, Horace is considered a dishonest man by the very people he tried to rob, and he now works as an assistant librarian — the irony being that the man who broke trust for a lifetime of reading is surrounded by books he cannot enjoy.

## Character List

- **Horace Danby:** A 50-year-old bachelor; a skilled locksmith and a respectable citizen by day, but a thief once a year; kind-hearted and trusting (to his downfall).
- **The Young Woman (The Lady in Red):** A charming and cunning fellow thief who outwits Horace by posing as the homeowner.
- **Sherry (the dog):** The household watchdog who is friendly to Horace.

## Key Quotes

1. "Horace Danby was good and respectable — but not completely honest."
2. "You must have a great deal of courage to be a burglar."
3. "I have always liked the wrong kind of people."
4. "He was arrested two days later — for the jewel robbery at Shotover Grange."
