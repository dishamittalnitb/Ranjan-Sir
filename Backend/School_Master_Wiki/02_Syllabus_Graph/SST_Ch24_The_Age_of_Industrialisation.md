# Chapter 24: The Age of Industrialisation

**[PERIODIC ASSESSMENT ONLY]**
This chapter is assessed as part of Periodic Assessment (Pen Paper Test, 5 marks) only. It is not included in the Board Examination.

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Enumerate economic, political, social features of Pre and Post Industrialization.
- Analyse and infer how industrialization impacted colonies with a specific focus on India.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Background**: The Industrial Revolution began in Britain in the late 18th century and spread to other parts of the world. It fundamentally changed production, society, and the global economy.
2. **Pre-Industrialization Features**:
   - Proto-industrialization: production by artisans and peasants in rural areas for urban merchants.
   - Guilds controlled urban crafts.
   - Hand-made goods were the norm.
3. **Key Inventions and Changes**:
   - Spinning Jenny (James Hargreaves), Water Frame (Richard Arkwright), and the power loom revolutionized textile production.
   - Steam engine (James Watt) enabled mechanized factories.
   - Railways and steamships accelerated transportation.
4. **Impact on Workers**: Factory system replaced home-based work; long working hours, poor conditions, child labour, and low wages. Workers formed unions; movements like Luddism (machine-breaking) emerged.
5. **Industrialization and Colonies**:
   - Colonial powers (especially Britain) used colonies as sources of raw materials (cotton from India) and markets for manufactured goods.
   - Indian textile industry declined under British rule due to import duties favoring British goods and flooding of Indian markets with machine-made cloth.
   - Factories in India emerged slowly, first in cotton textiles (Bombay, 1850s) and jute (Bengal).
6. **Small-Scale Industries and Handicrafts**: Despite industrialization, hand labour and small-scale production persisted and even grew in many sectors.
7. **Advertisements and Marketing**: New consumer culture -- advertisements, packaging, labelling, and calendars helped market goods and shape demand.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Industrial Revolution**: The transition from hand production methods to machines, new chemical manufacturing, and iron production processes (late 18th to early 19th century).
- **Proto-Industrialisation**: The phase of industrial production before factories, where rural artisans produced goods for merchants.
- **Luddism**: A movement of English workers who destroyed machines in protest against unemployment.
- **Spinning Jenny**: A multi-spindle spinning frame invented by James Hargreaves (1764).
- **Gomastha**: An Indian agent appointed by the East India Company to supervise weavers and collect supplies.
- **Jobber**: An Indian worker at a mill who recruited other workers and acted as an intermediary.
- **Orient**: A term used by Europeans to refer to Asian countries, especially China, Japan, and India.
- **Import Duties**: Taxes imposed on goods brought from foreign countries.
