# Chapter 13: The Lake Isle of Innisfree

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The poem expresses a deep yearning for peace, solitude, and a return to nature away from the noise and chaos of urban life. It captures the Romantic ideal of finding spiritual fulfillment in a simple, self-sufficient life close to nature.

**Summary:** The speaker declares his intention to go to Innisfree, a small island in Lough Gill, Ireland. He imagines building a small cabin of clay and wattles, planting nine bean rows, and keeping a hive of honeybees to live alone in the "bee-loud glade." He envisions the peace of the place — the slow-dropping veils of morning mist, the cricket singing, the glimmer of midnight, the purple glow of noon. Most powerfully, he hears the "lake water lapping with low sounds by the shore" even while standing on the roadway and the grey pavements of the city — the call of nature is deep in his heart's core.

## Literary Devices

- **Rhyme Scheme:** ABAB (alternating rhyme) in each quatrain.
- **Alliteration:** "lake water lapping with low sounds," "pavements grey"
- **Assonance:** "noon a purple glow," "dropping slow"
- **Imagery:** Vivid nature imagery — "bee-loud glade," "midnight's all a glimmer," "purple glow"
- **Anaphora:** "I will arise and go now" — repeated at the beginning of the first and last stanzas.
- **Symbolism:** Innisfree symbolizes an ideal world of peace and harmony.
- **Tone:** Calm, yearning, meditative.

## Key Quotes

1. "I will arise and go now, and go to Innisfree"
2. "Nine bean-rows will I have there, a hive for the honey-bee"
3. "I hear lake water lapping with low sounds by the shore"
4. "While I stand on the roadway, or on the pavements grey, / I hear it in the deep heart's core."
