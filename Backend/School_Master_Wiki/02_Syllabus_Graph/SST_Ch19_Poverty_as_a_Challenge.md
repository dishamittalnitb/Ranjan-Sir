# Chapter 19: Poverty as a Challenge

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Comprehend the reasons for poverty in the rural and urban areas.
- Evaluate the efficacy of the government to eradicate poverty.
- Correlate the link between education and poverty.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Definition of Poverty**: A state in which an individual cannot fulfill even the basic necessities of life (food, clothing, shelter, education, healthcare). It is a multi-dimensional problem.
2. **Poverty Line**: The estimated minimum level of income needed to secure the basic necessities. In India, it is calculated based on calorie requirements:
   - Rural: 2400 calories per person per day.
   - Urban: 2100 calories per person per day.
   - Revised method (Tendulkar Committee): Based on consumption expenditure.
3. **Extent of Poverty in India**: According to 2011-12 data (Tendulkar methodology), about 21.9% of India's population lived below the poverty line. There is significant variation among states: Bihar and Odisha have the highest poverty rates; Kerala, Goa, and Punjab have the lowest.
4. **Poverty Trends**: India has seen a consistent decline in poverty from about 55% (1973-74) to about 22% (2011-12). However, the absolute number of poor remains high.
5. **Causes of Poverty**:
   - Lack of land resources and low agricultural productivity.
   - Underemployment and unemployment.
   - High illiteracy and lack of skills.
   - Social factors like the caste system and discrimination.
   - Indebtedness to moneylenders.
   - Inequality of income and assets.
   - Large family size.
6. **Anti-Poverty Programmes**:
   - **MNREGA (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005)**: Guarantees 100 days of wage employment to rural households.
   - **Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana**: Housing for the rural and urban poor.
   - **National Food Security Act (NFSA, 2013)**: Subsidized food grains to about 2/3rd of the population.
   - **Swachh Bharat Mission**: Sanitation and cleanliness.
   - **PM Jan Dhan Yojana**: Financial inclusion through bank accounts.
7. **Challenges**: Targeted schemes often suffer from leakages, corruption, and exclusion errors. Effective implementation and monitoring are essential.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Poverty Line**: The minimum income threshold below which a person is considered poor.
- **BPL (Below Poverty Line)**: A government classification used to identify households eligible for certain welfare benefits.
- **Calorie Requirement**: The minimum energy intake needed for survival (2400 rural, 2100 urban).
- **Vulnerability**: The degree to which a population, individual, or household is at risk of falling into poverty.
- **Inter-State Disparities**: Variations in poverty levels across different states.
- **MNREGA**: A flagship employment guarantee scheme providing 100 days of wage work.
- **NFSA (National Food Security Act, 2013)**: Legislation providing subsidized food grains to the poor.
- **Social Exclusion**: The process through which certain groups are systematically disadvantaged and discriminated against.
