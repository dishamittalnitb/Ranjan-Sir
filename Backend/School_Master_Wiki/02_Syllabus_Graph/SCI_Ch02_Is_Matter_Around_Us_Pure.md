# Chapter 02: Is Matter Around Us Pure

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Pure and Impure Substances
- **Pure substance:** Consists of a single type of particles (elements or compounds). Has a fixed composition.
- **Mixture:** Contains more than one substance mixed in any proportion. May be homogeneous or heterogeneous.

### 1.2 Elements, Compounds, and Mixtures
| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Element** | Pure substance made of only one kind of atom; cannot be broken down. | Iron, oxygen, gold |
| **Compound** | Pure substance formed by chemical combination of two or more elements in fixed proportion; can be broken down chemically. | Water (H2O), NaCl, CO2 |
| **Mixture** | Physical combination of two or more substances; variable composition; can be separated by physical methods. | Air, alloys, salt + sand |

### 1.3 Types of Mixtures
- **Homogeneous mixture:** Uniform composition throughout (e.g., sugar in water, salt in water, alloys).
- **Heterogeneous mixture:** Non-uniform composition (e.g., oil and water, sand and iron filings).

### 1.4 Solutions, Colloids, and Suspensions
| Property | True Solution | Colloid | Suspension |
|----------|-------------|---------|------------|
| **Particle size** | < 1 nm | 1-100 nm | > 100 nm |
| **Transparency** | Transparent | Translucent (Tyndall effect) | Opaque |
| **Filtration** | Cannot be filtered | Cannot be filtered with ordinary filter paper | Can be filtered |
| **Stability** | Very stable | Stable | Settle on standing |
| **Tyndall effect** | No | Yes | May show |

### 1.5 Physical and Chemical Changes
| Physical Change | Chemical Change |
|-----------------|----------------|
| No new substance formed | New substance formed |
| Usually reversible | Usually irreversible |
| Change in physical properties only | Change in chemical composition |
| e.g., melting of ice, dissolving salt | e.g., rusting of iron, burning of magnesium |
- **Note:** Separating the components of a mixture is excluded from the assessment syllabus.

### 1.6 Concentration of Solution
- **Concentration** = (Amount of solute / Amount of solution) x 100
- **Mass by mass percentage:** (Mass of solute / Mass of solution) x 100
- **Mass by volume percentage:** (Mass of solute / Volume of solution) x 100

## 2. Key Formulas/Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Pure Substance** | Material consisting of a single type of particle (element or compound). |
| **Mixture** | Physical combination of two or more pure substances in any ratio. |
| **Solution** | Homogeneous mixture of two or more substances. Solute + Solvent. |
| **Colloid** | Heterogeneous mixture with particle size 1-100 nm; shows Tyndall effect. |
| **Suspension** | Heterogeneous mixture with visible particles that settle over time. |
| **Tyndall Effect** | Scattering of light by colloidal particles making the path of light visible. |
| **Concentration (% w/w)** | (Mass of solute / Mass of solution) x 100 |

## 3. Board Exam Weightage & Question Pattern

Falls under **Unit I: Matter - Its Nature and Behaviour (25 marks)**. Question pattern:

- **VSA (1 mark):** Define element, compound, mixture; identify colloid/suspension; define concentration.
- **SA (2-3 marks):** Differentiate homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures; compare true solution, colloid, suspension; distinguish physical and chemical changes; calculate concentration.
- **LA (5 marks):** Describe types of mixtures with examples; explain Tyndall effect; classify substances.
- **Assertion-Reasoning:** Based on properties of solutions, colloids, and suspensions.
