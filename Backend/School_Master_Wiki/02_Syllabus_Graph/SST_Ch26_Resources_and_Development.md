# Chapter 26: Resources and Development

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Enumerate how the resources are interdependent, justify how planning is essential in judicious utilisation of resources and the need to develop them in India.
- Infer the rationale for development of resources.
- Analyse and evaluate data and information related to non-optimal land utilization in India.
- Suggest remedial measures for optimal utilization of underutilized resources.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Resources**: Everything available in our environment that satisfies human needs is a resource, provided it is technologically accessible, economically feasible, and culturally acceptable.
2. **Classification of Resources**:
   - **On the basis of origin**: Biotic (living: flora, fauna) and Abiotic (non-living: rocks, minerals).
   - **On the basis of exhaustibility**: Renewable (solar, wind, water, forests) and Non-Renewable (coal, petroleum, minerals).
   - **On the basis of ownership**: Individual (personal land, house), Community-owned (village ponds, grazing grounds), National (forests, minerals, water resources), International (ocean resources beyond 200 nautical miles).
   - **On the basis of status of development**: Potential (not yet utilized), Developed (surveyed and being used), Stock (available but not usable due to lack of technology), Reserves (can be used with current technology but kept for future).
3. **Resource Planning in India**: Essential for sustainable development. Involves identification and inventory, matching resource availability to development plans, and conservation. Gandhiji's philosophy: "There is enough for everybody's need, but not for anybody's greed."
4. **Land Resources**: India has diverse land use patterns -- net sown area, forest area, fallow lands, culturable wasteland, and land not available for cultivation. About 46% of total geographical area is under net sown area.
5. **Land Degradation**: Caused by deforestation, overgrazing, mining, over-irrigation (waterlogging), and industrial effluents. Measures include afforestation, proper grazing management, stabilization of sand dunes, and control of mining activities.
6. **Soil as a Resource**: Soil formation takes millions of years. Major soil types of India and their distribution:
   - **Alluvial Soil**: Northern Plains, river valleys; fertile, rich in potash.
   - **Black Soil (Regur)**: Deccan plateau; cotton-growing; rich in iron, lime, calcium.
   - **Red and Yellow Soil**: Eastern and southern Deccan; iron-rich.
   - **Laterite Soil**: Highlands of Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats; good for tea, coffee.
   - **Arid Soil**: Rajasthan, Gujarat; sandy, saline.
   - **Forest/Mountain Soil**: Himalayan region; acidic, humus-rich.
7. **Soil Erosion and Conservation**: Sheet, gully, and wind erosion. Conservation through contour ploughing, terrace farming, strip cropping, shelter belts, and afforestation.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Resource**: A substance or object that satisfies human needs.
- **Sustainable Development**: Development that meets present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet their own needs.
- **Renewable Resource**: A resource that can be replenished naturally over time.
- **Non-Renewable Resource**: A resource that does not regenerate at a sufficient rate for sustainable economic extraction.
- **Net Sown Area**: The land on which crops are actually grown.
- **Gross Cropped Area**: Net sown area multiplied by the number of crops grown, accounting for multiple cropping.
- **Alluvial Soil**: Soil formed by river deposits; the most extensive and fertile soil in India.
- **Contour Ploughing**: Ploughing along the contour lines of a slope to prevent soil erosion.
