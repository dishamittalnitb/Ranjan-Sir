# Chapter 42: Fog

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The poem captures the quiet, mysterious arrival and departure of fog through a deceptively simple metaphor. It reflects on the power of nature to transform familiar surroundings and suggests that silence and stillness can be as powerful as noise and motion.

**Summary:** In just six lines, the poet compares fog to a cat. Fog comes "on little cat feet" — silently and stealthily. It sits looking over the harbor and city "on silent haunches," and then, just as quietly, moves on. The entire poem is an extended metaphor, with the cat's movements perfectly mirroring the behavior of fog — soft, soundless, observant, and fleeting.

## Literary Devices

- **Rhyme Scheme:** Free verse — no rhyme scheme; short, compact lines.
- **Extended Metaphor:** Fog is compared to a cat throughout the entire poem.
- **Personification:** Fog is given the attributes of a living cat — it comes, sits, looks, and moves.
- **Imagery:** Vivid visual imagery — "little cat feet," "silent haunches," "harbour and city."
- **Alliteration:** "little cat feet" — the 't' sounds are soft and hushed, mimicking the fog's silence.
- **Tone:** Quiet, meditative, observant, calm.

## Key Quotes

1. "The fog comes / on little cat feet."
2. "It sits looking / over harbour and city / on silent haunches"
3. "and then moves on."
