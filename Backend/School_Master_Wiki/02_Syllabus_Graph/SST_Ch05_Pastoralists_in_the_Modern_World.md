# Chapter 05: Pastoralists in the Modern World

**[PERIODIC ASSESSMENT ONLY]**
This chapter is assessed as part of Periodic Assessment (Pen Paper Test, 5 marks) only. It is not included in the Board Examination.

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Examine the situations that have created nomadic societies, highlighting the key factors played by climatic conditions and topography.
- Analyse varying patterns of developments within pastoral societies in different places in India.
- Comprehend the impact of colonialism on Pastoralists in India and Africa.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Who are Pastoralists?**: Pastoralists are communities whose livelihood depends primarily on the rearing of livestock. They move seasonally (nomadic pastoralism) in search of pastures and water.
2. **Pastoral Nomads of India**:
   - **Gujjar Bakarwals of Jammu & Kashmir**: Move between low Shiwaliks (winter) and high Bugyals (summer meadows).
   - **Gaddi Shepherds of Himachal Pradesh**: Similar seasonal migration between low and high pastures.
   - **Dhangars of Maharashtra**: Move between central plateau (monsoon) and Konkan coastal region (post-monsoon).
   - **Raikas of Rajasthan**: Camel herders who move across the Thar Desert; also combine sheep/goat herding with cultivation.
   - **Bhotiyas, Sherpas, Kinnara**: Pastoral communities of the Himalayas.
3. **Pastoralism in Africa**: The Maasai community in Kenya and Tanzania, the Bedouins, Berbers, and others depend on livestock.
4. **Colonial Impact on Pastoralists**:
   - Forest Acts restricted access to grazing lands.
   - Criminal Tribes Act (1871) classified many pastoral communities as "criminal" and restricted their movement.
   - Grazing tax was imposed on every head of cattle.
   - Agricultural expansion reduced pasturelands.
   - Reservation of forests for timber restricted pastoral access.
5. **Effects of Restrictions**: Loss of grazing lands, decline in livestock, increased poverty, and forced settlement of nomadic communities.
6. **Adaptation and Survival**: Some pastoralists adapted by reducing herd size, finding new grazing routes, combining pastoralism with settled agriculture, or migrating to cities for work.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Pastoralism**: A livelihood system based on the raising and herding of livestock.
- **Nomadism**: A way of life characterized by seasonal movement in search of resources.
- **Bugyals**: High-altitude summer grazing meadows in the Himalayas.
- **Bhabar**: A dry region south of the Shiwaliks where streams disappear.
- **Transhumance**: Seasonal movement of people with their livestock between fixed summer and winter pastures.
- **Criminal Tribes Act (1871)**: A colonial law that branded certain nomadic communities as "habitually criminal" and restricted their movement.
- **Grazing Tax**: A tax imposed by the colonial government on pastoralists for each animal grazed on public lands.
- **Maasai**: A pastoral community in East Africa (Kenya and Tanzania) who were severely affected by colonial policies.
