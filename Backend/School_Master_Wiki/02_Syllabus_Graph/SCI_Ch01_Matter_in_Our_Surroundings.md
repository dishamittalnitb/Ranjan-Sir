# Chapter 01: Matter in Our Surroundings

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Definition of Matter
- Anything that occupies space and has mass is called matter.
- Matter is made up of tiny particles.

### 1.2 Particulate Nature of Matter
- Particles of matter are continuously moving (possess kinetic energy).
- Particles of matter have spaces between them.
- Particles of matter attract each other.

### 1.3 States of Matter
| State | Characteristics |
|-------|----------------|
| **Solid** | Definite shape and volume; particles tightly packed; least kinetic energy; strong intermolecular forces; incompressible. |
| **Liquid** | Definite volume, no definite shape (takes shape of container); particles less tightly packed; moderate kinetic energy; weaker intermolecular forces than solids; nearly incompressible. |
| **Gas** | Neither definite shape nor volume; particles far apart; highest kinetic energy; negligible intermolecular forces; highly compressible. |

### 1.4 Change of State
- **Melting (Fusion):** Solid to liquid; occurs at melting point; heat is absorbed (endothermic). Latent heat of fusion is the heat energy required to change 1 kg of solid into liquid at atmospheric pressure at its melting point.
- **Freezing:** Liquid to solid; occurs at freezing point; heat is released (exothermic).
- **Vaporisation:** Liquid to gas; boiling occurs at boiling point throughout the liquid; latent heat of vaporisation is the heat energy required to change 1 kg of liquid to gas at atmospheric pressure at its boiling point.
- **Condensation:** Gas to liquid; heat is released.
- **Sublimation:** Solid directly to gas without passing through liquid state (e.g., camphor, naphthalene, ammonium chloride).
- **Evaporation:** A surface phenomenon where liquid changes to vapour at any temperature below its boiling point.
  - **Factors affecting evaporation:** Surface area, temperature, humidity, wind speed.
  - **Cooling by evaporation:** During evaporation, particles absorb energy from the surroundings, causing cooling.

### 1.5 Key Differences
- **Boiling vs Evaporation:** Boiling is a bulk phenomenon occurring at boiling point throughout the liquid; evaporation is a surface phenomenon occurring at all temperatures.
- The SI unit of temperature is Kelvin (K). 0 K = -273.15 degree C (absolute zero). K = degree C + 273.

## 2. Key Formulas/Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Latent Heat of Fusion** | Energy required to convert 1 kg of solid to liquid at its melting point (without temperature change). Unit: J/kg. |
| **Latent Heat of Vaporisation** | Energy required to convert 1 kg of liquid to vapour at its boiling point (without temperature change). Unit: J/kg. |
| **Sublimation** | Direct change of state from solid to gas (or vice versa). |
| **Evaporation** | Surface phenomenon; liquid to vapour below boiling point. |
| **Temperature Conversion** | K = degree C + 273.15; degree C = K - 273.15 |
