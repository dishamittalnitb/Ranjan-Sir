## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Health and Its Failure
- **Health:** A state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity (WHO definition).
- **Personal and community health:** Both are interrelated. Community health depends on clean environment, hygiene, nutrition, and healthcare facilities.
- **Factors affecting health:** Genetics, environment, hygiene, nutrition, socio-economic conditions, healthcare access.

### 1.2 Disease and Its Causes
- **Disease:** A condition of the body or part of it in which functions are disturbed or damaged.
- **Acute diseases:** Last for short duration (e.g., common cold, fever).
- **Chronic diseases:** Last for long duration or lifetime (e.g., diabetes, hypertension, arthritis).
- **Causes of diseases:** Pathogens (biological), nutritional deficiency, genetic disorders, environmental pollution, lifestyle factors.

### 1.3 Infectious and Non-Infectious Diseases
| Infectious (Communicable) | Non-Infectious (Non-Communicable) |
|---------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Caused by pathogens | Not caused by pathogens |
| Can spread from person to person | Do not spread from person to person |
| e.g., Tuberculosis, COVID-19, Malaria, Dengue, Cholera | e.g., Cancer, Diabetes, Heart disease, Genetic disorders |

### 1.4 Diseases Caused by Microbes
| Microbe Type | Diseases | Mode of Transmission | Prevention |
|-------------|----------|---------------------|------------|
| **Virus** | Common cold, Influenza, Dengue, Chikungunya, AIDS/HIV, Polio, Measles, Hepatitis | Air, water, food, vectors (mosquitoes), sexual contact, blood | Vaccination, hygiene, vector control |
| **Bacteria** | Tuberculosis (TB), Typhoid, Cholera, Diarrhoea | Air, contaminated water/food | Proper sanitation, clean water, antibiotics |
| **Protozoans** | Malaria (Plasmodium), Kala-azar (Leishmania), Amoebic dysentery | Vector (mosquito, sandfly), contaminated water | Vector control, sanitation |

### 1.5 Principles of Treatment
- **Symptom-directed treatment:** Reduces symptoms (e.g., fever, pain).
- **Pathogen-directed treatment:** Kills the pathogen (e.g., antibiotics for bacteria). Note: Antibiotics do NOT work against viruses.
- **Rest and nutrition:** Support the body's immune response.

### 1.6 Principles of Prevention
- **General prevention:** Clean environment, safe drinking water, proper nutrition, personal hygiene, proper sanitation.
- **Specific prevention:** Immunization/vaccination (e.g., BCG for TB, OPV/IPV for Polio, DPT, MMR, Hepatitis B vaccine).
- **Vector control:** Eliminate mosquito breeding sites, use of mosquito nets/repellents.

### 1.7 Pulse Polio Programme
- Launched in India in 1995 to eradicate polio.
- Oral Polio Vaccine (OPV) administered to all children under 5 years.
- India was declared polio-free by WHO in 2014.

## 2. Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Health** | A state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being (WHO). |
| **Disease** | Any disturbance in the structure or function of the body or its parts. |
| **Pathogen** | A disease-causing microorganism (virus, bacterium, protozoan, fungus). |
| **Communicable Disease** | Disease that can be transmitted from one person to another. |
| **Non-Communicable Disease** | Disease that does not spread from person to person. |
| **Incubation Period** | Time between entry of pathogen and the appearance of first symptoms. |
| **Vaccination/Immunization** | Administration of weakened/inactivated pathogen to stimulate immune response. |
| **Vector** | An organism (typically insect) that carries and transmits a pathogen. |
| **Antibiotic** | A medicine that kills or inhibits the growth of bacteria. |
