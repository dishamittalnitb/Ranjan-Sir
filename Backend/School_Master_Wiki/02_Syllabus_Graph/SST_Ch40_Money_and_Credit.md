# Chapter 40: Money and Credit

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Enumerate how money plays as a medium of exchange in all transactions of goods and services from ancient times to the present times.
- Analyse and infer various sources of Credit.
- Summarise the significance and role of self-help groups in the betterment of the economic condition of rural people/women.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Money as a Medium of Exchange**: Money eliminates the problem of double coincidence of wants in the barter system. It serves as a medium of exchange, a store of value, and a unit of account.
2. **Modern Forms of Money**:
   - Currency (paper notes and coins): Issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on behalf of the government. Legal tender.
   - Bank deposits: Demand deposits in bank accounts can be withdrawn on demand and used for payments through cheques, debit cards, UPI, NEFT, RTGS.
3. **Banking System**:
   - Banks accept deposits and lend money. The difference between interest on loans and interest on deposits is the bank's main income.
   - Only about 15% of deposits are kept as cash reserve (Cash Reserve Ratio - CRR); the rest is lent out.
   - RBI supervises the banking system.
4. **Credit (Loans)**:
   - Credit refers to an agreement where a lender provides money or resources to a borrower with the promise of future repayment with interest.
   - **Situations**: Credit can be a positive force (helps a farmer buy inputs and increase production) or a debt trap (when crop fails, the farmer cannot repay and sinks deeper into debt).
5. **Sources of Credit in India**:
   - **Formal Sector**: Banks, cooperatives. Regulated by RBI. Lower interest rates (about 10-15%). Includes Kisan Credit Card, SHG-bank linkage.
   - **Informal Sector**: Moneylenders, landlords, traders, relatives. Unregulated. High interest rates (can be 60% or more). Often leads to debt traps.
   - **Access**: In rural India, informal sources still account for a large share of credit. Expansion of formal banking is essential.
6. **Self-Help Groups (SHGs)**:
   - SHGs are small groups of people (usually 15-20 women) who pool their savings and lend to members.
   - They are linked with banks for larger loans.
   - SHGs promote financial inclusion, especially for poor women in rural areas. They have reduced dependence on moneylenders.
   - Example: Grameen Bank (Bangladesh) -- a pioneering microfinance institution, founded by Muhammad Yunus.
7. **Role of RBI**: Issues currency, supervises the banking system, sets monetary policy (repo rate, CRR, SLR), and maintains price stability.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Money**: Any item or verifiable record generally accepted as payment for goods and services.
- **Double Coincidence of Wants**: In barter, both parties must want what the other offers. Money eliminates this.
- **Demand Deposits**: Deposits in a bank account that can be withdrawn on demand.
- **Cheque**: A written order instructing a bank to pay a specified amount from an account.
- **Credit**: An agreement in which a lender supplies money/resources now in exchange for future repayment.
- **Collateral**: An asset (land, property, deposits) that a borrower offers as security for a loan.
- **SHG (Self-Help Group)**: A voluntary association of people who pool savings and provide loans to members.
- **Microfinance**: Financial services (small loans, savings, insurance) for low-income groups who lack access to formal banking.
- **Repo Rate**: The rate at which RBI lends to commercial banks.
- **CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio)**: The portion of deposits that banks must keep in reserve with RBI.
