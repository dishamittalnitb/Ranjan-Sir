# Chapter 02: Socialism in Europe and the Russian Revolution

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Compare the situations that led to the rise of Russian and French Revolutions.
- Examine the situations that led to the establishment of Lenin's communism and Stalin's collectivization.
- Analyse the role played by the varied philosophers and leaders that shaped the revolution.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Rise of Socialism**: Socialism emerged as a response to the inequalities created by industrial capitalism. Early socialists like Robert Owen, Louis Blanc, and Karl Marx envisioned a society based on collective ownership.
2. **The Russian Empire in 1914**: Russia was an autocracy ruled by Tsar Nicholas II. It was a vast empire with extreme inequality, an agrarian economy, and emerging industries in cities.
3. **Causes of the Russian Revolution**: Political autocracy, economic distress, impact of World War I, food shortages, and growing discontent among peasants and workers.
4. **February Revolution (1917)**: Strikes in Petrograd led to the abdication of Tsar Nicholas II. A Provisional Government was formed, but it failed to address popular demands.
5. **October Revolution (1917)**: Led by Lenin and the Bolsheviks, it overthrew the Provisional Government. Land was redistributed, banks nationalized, and Russia withdrew from WWI.
6. **Civil War and the Making of the USSR**: The Bolsheviks fought a civil war against the Whites (1918-20). In 1922, the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR) was formed.
7. **Stalin's Collectivization**: After Lenin's death (1924), Stalin introduced five-year plans, forced collectivization of agriculture, and rapid industrialization. This led to massive social upheaval and famines.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Autocracy**: A system of government where one person holds absolute power.
- **Bolsheviks**: The radical majority faction of the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party, led by Lenin.
- **Mensheviks**: The minority faction that supported a broad-based party and gradual reform.
- **Soviet**: An elected council of workers and soldiers formed during the revolution.
- **Communism**: A political and economic ideology advocating classless society and collective ownership.
- **Collectivization**: The consolidation of small peasant holdings into large collective farms under state control.
- **Duma**: The elected legislative assembly in Russia, created after the 1905 Revolution.
- **Kolkhoz**: A collective farm in the USSR.
- **Comintern**: The Communist International, founded in 1919 to promote world revolution.
