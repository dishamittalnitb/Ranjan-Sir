# Chapter 07: Physical Features of India

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Justify how the Physical Features of India influence the livelihoods, culture, and the biodiversity of the region.
- Examine the geological processes that played a crucial role in the formation of diverse physical features in India.
- Analyse the conditions and relationships of the people living in different physiographic areas.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Major Physiographic Divisions**: India's physical features can be grouped into six major divisions:
   - The Himalayan Mountains
   - The Northern Plains
   - The Peninsular Plateau
   - The Indian Desert
   - The Coastal Plains
   - The Islands
2. **The Himalayas**: Young fold mountains stretching 2,400 km across the northern boundary. Divided into three parallel ranges: Himadri (Great Himalayas), Himachal (Lesser Himalayas), and Shiwaliks (Outer Himalayas). The Purvanchal comprises the northeastern extension.
3. **The Northern Plains**: Formed by the Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra river systems with their tributaries. Divided into: Punjab Plains (Indus system), Ganga Plains, and Brahmaputra Plains. Characterized by fertile alluvial soil, making it densely populated.
4. **The Peninsular Plateau**: Oldest landmass; composed of igneous and metamorphic rocks. Divided into the Central Highlands (Malwa Plateau, Bundelkhand, Baghelkhand, Chota Nagpur Plateau) and the Deccan Plateau (bounded by Western and Eastern Ghats).
5. **The Indian Desert (Thar Desert)**: Located in western India in Rajasthan. Characterized by sandy soil, low rainfall, and arid conditions. Luni is the only significant river.
6. **The Coastal Plains**: Western Coastal Plain (Konkan, Kannad, Malabar coasts) -- narrow, between Western Ghats and Arabian Sea. Eastern Coastal Plain (Northern Circar, Coromandel Coast) -- broader, between Eastern Ghats and Bay of Bengal.
7. **The Islands**: Lakshadweep (coral islands in Arabian Sea) and Andaman & Nicobar Islands (in Bay of Bengal, including India's only active volcano at Barren Island).

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Fold Mountains**: Mountains formed by the folding of the earth's crust due to tectonic compression (e.g., the Himalayas).
- **Alluvial Soil**: Fertile soil deposited by rivers, found extensively in the Northern Plains.
- **Divergent/Convergent Boundaries**: Tectonic plate boundaries where plates move apart or collide respectively.
- **Gondwanaland**: An ancient supercontinent that included present-day India, Africa, South America, Australia, and Antarctica.
- **Tethys Sea**: An ancient sea between the Indian plate and the Eurasian plate (now the Himalayas).
- **Western Ghats (Sahyadris)**: A mountain range running parallel to India's west coast.
- **Eastern Ghats**: A discontinuous mountain range along India's east coast.
- **Karakoram**: A mountain range in the northernmost part of India, containing K2.
