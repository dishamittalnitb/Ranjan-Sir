# Chapter 33: Power-sharing

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Enumerate the need for power sharing in democracy.
- Analyse the challenges faced by countries like Belgium and Sri Lanka ensuring effective power sharing.
- Compare and contrast the power sharing of India with Sri Lanka and Belgium.
- Summarise the purpose of power sharing in preserving the unity and stability of a country.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Why Power Sharing?**: Power sharing is the spirit of democracy. It reduces the possibility of conflict, ensures political stability, and upholds the principle that all groups have a say in governance.
2. **Belgium -- A Case Study**:
   - Geographical distribution: Dutch-speaking (59%) in Flanders (north), French-speaking (40%) in Wallonia (south), German-speaking (1%).
   - In Brussels, Dutch-speaking are a minority but French-speaking are a majority.
   - Tensions between communities led to constitutional reforms (1970-1993).
   - Accommodation: equal representation of both language groups in central government; separate governments for communities; community government elected by each language community; state governments not subordinate to central government.
3. **Sri Lanka -- A Case Study**:
   - Sinhala-speaking (74%) majority and Tamil-speaking (18%) minority (Sri Lankan Tamils 13% and Indian Tamils 5%).
   - Majoritarianism: Sinhala supremacy -- Sinhala as the official language (1956 Act), state protection for Buddhism, university positions favouring Sinhalese.
   - Alienation of Tamils led to demand for autonomy, which escalated into a civil war (1983-2009).
4. **Forms of Power Sharing**:
   - **Horizontal**: Among different organs of government (legislature, executive, judiciary). Each checks the others -- separation of powers.
   - **Vertical**: Among governments at different levels -- central, state, and local governments (federal division of powers).
   - **Among different social groups**: Reservations for SCs, STs, OBCs, women in legislatures (community government model).
   - **Among political parties, pressure groups, and movements**: Competition and coalition governments; interest groups influence decision-making.
5. **Prudential and Moral Reasons**: Prudential -- power sharing reduces conflict and maintains stability. Moral -- power sharing is the very spirit of democracy; people have the right to be consulted on how they are governed.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Power Sharing**: A system of political arrangement in which all groups and sections of society share decision-making power.
- **Majoritarianism**: A belief that the majority community should be able to rule a country in whichever way it wants.
- **Community Government**: A government elected by members of a particular language community (in Belgium) to decide on cultural, educational, and language-related issues.
- **Civil War**: An armed conflict between different groups within a country.
- **Federalism**: A system of government in which power is divided between a central authority and constituent political units.
- **Horizontal Distribution of Power**: Sharing of power among different organs of government at the same level.
- **Vertical Division of Power**: Sharing of power among governments at different levels.
- **Coalition Government**: A government formed by an alliance of two or more political parties.
