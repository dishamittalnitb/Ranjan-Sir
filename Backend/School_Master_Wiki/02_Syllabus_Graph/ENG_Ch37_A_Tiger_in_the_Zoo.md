# Chapter 37: A Tiger in the Zoo

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The poem contrasts the life of a tiger in captivity with its natural existence in the wild. It explores themes of freedom versus confinement, the dignity of wild creatures, and the cruelty of caging animals for human entertainment. The tiger in the zoo is a symbol of suppressed natural instincts.

**Summary:** The poem's first stanza describes the tiger in the zoo — he "stalks" the length of his cage in quiet rage, his vivid stripes visible. The second stanza imagines what the tiger should be doing in the wild: lurking in shadow, sliding through long grass near a water hole, waiting to hunt plump deer. The third stanza imagines the tiger at the jungle's edge baring his white fangs and claws, terrorizing the nearby village. The fourth stanza returns to the grim reality — the tiger is locked in a concrete cell, pacing behind bars, ignoring visitors. The final stanza says he hears the "last voice" at night — the sound of the patrolling cars, and stares at the brilliant stars with his brilliant eyes, longing for freedom.

## Literary Devices

- **Rhyme Scheme:** ABCB (ballad-like alternating rhyme in each stanza, with some variation), 5 quatrains.
- **Alliteration:** "stalks the length," "plump deer pass," "brilliant stars," "jungle's edge"
- **Metaphor:** The tiger = all wild creatures; the cage = oppression of freedom; "brilliant eyes staring at brilliant stars" = longing for freedom.
- **Personification:** The tiger is endowed with human emotions ("quiet rage").
- **Imagery:** Vivid contrasts between the natural jungle and the concrete cell.
- **Tone:** The poem shifts between angry/sympathetic (zoo scenes) and wild/free (imagined jungle scenes).

## Key Quotes

1. "He stalks in his vivid stripes / The few steps of his cage"
2. "He should be lurking in shadow, / Sliding through long grass"
3. "He should be snarling around houses / At the jungle's edge"
4. "He hears the last voice at night, / The patrolling cars"
