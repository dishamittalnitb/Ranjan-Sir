# Chapter 24: Real Numbers (Class X)

## Concept Summary

This chapter extends the understanding of real numbers with a focus on number theory:

1. **Review**: Euclid's Division Lemma -- Given positive integers a and b, there exist unique integers q and r such that a = bq + r, where 0 <= r < b.

2. **Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic**: Every composite number can be expressed (factorized) as a product of prime numbers uniquely, apart from the order of the factors. This is also called the Unique Factorization Theorem.

3. **Applications of FTA**:
   - Computing HCF and LCM using prime factorization: 
     - HCF(a, b) = Product of the smallest power of each common prime factor
     - LCM(a, b) = Product of the greatest power of each prime factor
   - Relationship: HCF(a, b) * LCM(a, b) = a * b (for two numbers)

4. **Proofs of Irrationality**:
   - Proving sqrt(2), sqrt(3), sqrt(5) are irrational using contradiction
   - Proving expressions like 3 + 2*sqrt(5) are irrational

5. **Rational Numbers and Decimal Expansions**: Using prime factorization of denominator to determine if decimal expansion terminates (denominator of form 2^n * 5^m) or repeats (otherwise).

## Key Formulas/Theorems

**Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic**: Every composite number has a unique prime factorization.

**HCF-LCM Relation**: HCF(a, b) * LCM(a, b) = a * b

**Irrationality Proof Template**:
1. Assume sqrt(p) is rational, i.e., sqrt(p) = a/b in lowest terms
2. Then p = a^2 / b^2, so a^2 = pb^2
3. Therefore p divides a^2, hence p divides a
4. Let a = pc, then p^2*c^2 = pb^2, so b^2 = pc^2
5. So p divides b^2, hence p divides b
6. Contradiction (a and b have common factor p, but were assumed coprime)
7. Therefore sqrt(p) is irrational
