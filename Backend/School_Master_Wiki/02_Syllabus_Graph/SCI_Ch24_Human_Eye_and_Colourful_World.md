# Chapter 24: Human Eye and Colourful World

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 The Human Eye
The human eye is a natural optical instrument. It functions like a camera, forming an inverted real image on the light-sensitive retina.

#### Structure and Functioning
| Part | Function |
|------|----------|
| **Cornea** | Transparent front part; allows light to enter; major refraction occurs here. |
| **Aqueous Humour** | Fluid behind cornea; maintains intraocular pressure and provides nutrients. |
| **Iris** | Coloured muscular diaphragm; controls pupil size. |
| **Pupil** | Central opening in iris; regulates amount of light entering (enlarges in dim light, contracts in bright light). |
| **Crystalline Lens** | Biconvex lens; fine-tunes focus on retina by changing shape (accommodation). |
| **Ciliary Muscles** | Alter the curvature (focal length) of the lens for accommodation. |
| **Vitreous Humour** | Gel-like fluid filling the eyeball; maintains shape. |
| **Retina** | Light-sensitive screen (contains rods and cones); image is formed here. |
| **Rods** (photoreceptors) | Sensitive to dim light; no colour detection. |
| **Cones** (photoreceptors) | Detect colour; require bright light. |
| **Optic Nerve** | Carries visual information (electrical impulses) from retina to brain. |
| **Blind Spot** | Point where optic nerve leaves the eye; no photoreceptors. |

### 1.2 Accommodation
- The ability of the eye to adjust its focal length to see objects at different distances.
- Ciliary muscles relax --> lens becomes thin (focal length increases) --> see distant objects.
- Ciliary muscles contract --> lens becomes thick (focal length decreases) --> see near objects.
- **Near point (Least distance of distinct vision):** ~25 cm for a normal adult eye.
- **Far point:** Infinity for a normal eye.

### 1.3 Defects of Vision and Their Corrections

| Defect | Cause | Image Formation | Correction Lens |
|--------|-------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Myopia (Nearsightedness)** | Eyeball too long OR lens too converging. Can see near objects but not distant. | Image forms before retina. | **Concave lens** (diverging) of suitable power. |
| **Hypermetropia (Farsightedness)** | Eyeball too short OR lens too diverging. Can see distant objects but not near. | Image forms behind retina. | **Convex lens** (converging) of suitable power. |
| **Presbyopia** | Age-related loss of accommodation power (ciliary muscles weaken; lens loses flexibility). | Difficulty in seeing both near and distant. | **Bifocal lens** (both concave and convex). |

- **Cataract:** Clouding of the crystalline lens; corrected by surgical removal and replacement with artificial lens.

### 1.4 Applications of Spherical Mirrors and Lenses
- **Concave mirrors:** Shaving mirrors, makeup mirrors, dentist's mirrors (enlarged, erect image when object within focus), reflectors in torches/headlights (parallel beam), solar furnaces.
- **Convex mirrors:** Rear-view mirrors in vehicles (wider field of view, always erect).
- **Convex lenses:** Magnifying glasses, cameras, microscopes, telescopes.
- **Concave lenses:** Spectacles for myopia.

### 1.5 Refraction of Light Through a Prism
- A prism is a transparent medium with two inclined plane refracting surfaces.
- When white light passes through a prism, it splits into its constituent colours (dispersion).
- **Angle of deviation (delta):** The angle between the incident ray and the emergent ray.
- **Angle of prism (A):** The angle between the two refracting surfaces.

### 1.6 Dispersion of Light
- The splitting of white light into its seven constituent colours (VIBGYOR) when it passes through a prism.
  - Violet (least wavelength, most deviated), Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red (longest wavelength, least deviated).
- **Cause:** Different colours have different wavelengths, and hence different speeds (refractive indices) in the prism material.
- **Rainbow:** Natural dispersion of sunlight by water droplets in the atmosphere. Red at top (least deviated), violet at bottom (most deviated).

### 1.7 Atmospheric Refraction
- The refraction of light by the Earth's atmosphere (density varies with altitude).
- **Twinkling of stars:** Starlight undergoes continuous refraction as it passes through layers of varying density. The apparent position of the star flickers (stars are point sources).
- **Planets do not twinkle:** They are extended sources (disc-like), so the twinkling from different parts averages out.
- **Advanced sunrise and delayed sunset:** Sun is visible about 2 minutes before actual sunrise and about 2 minutes after actual sunset due to atmospheric refraction (apparent shift ~0.5 degree).

### 1.8 Scattering of Light
- The phenomenon in which light is absorbed and re-radiated in different directions by particles in its path.
- **Tyndall effect:** Scattering of light by colloidal particles. Path of light becomes visible.
- **Why sky appears blue:** Shorter wavelengths (blue/violet) scatter more (Rayleigh scattering) than longer wavelengths (red). Blue is scattered in all directions, making the sky appear blue.
- **Why clouds appear white:** Water droplets in clouds are much larger than wavelength; all colours scatter equally (Mie scattering), making clouds appear white.
- **Note from syllabus:** Colour of the sun at sunrise and sunset is excluded from the assessment syllabus.
