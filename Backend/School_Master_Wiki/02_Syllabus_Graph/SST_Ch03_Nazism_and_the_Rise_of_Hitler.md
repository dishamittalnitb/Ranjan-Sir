# Chapter 03: Nazism and the Rise of Hitler

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Analyse the role of the "Treaty of Versailles" in the rise of Hitler to power.
- Analyse the genocidal war waged against the "undesirables" by Hitler.
- Compare and contrast the characteristics of Hitler and Gandhi.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Background**: Germany's defeat in World War I, the harsh Treaty of Versailles (1919), economic depression, and political instability paved the way for Hitler's rise.
2. **The Weimar Republic**: The democratic government established after WWI faced severe problems -- hyperinflation (1923), French occupation of the Ruhr, and constant political violence.
3. **Rise of Hitler**: Hitler joined the German Workers' Party, which became the National Socialist German Workers' Party (Nazi Party). After the failed Munich Putsch (1923), he rebuilt the party and came to power legally in 1933.
4. **The Nazi Ideology**: Based on racial hierarchy with the "Nordic German Aryan" race at the top. Jews, Slavs, Gypsies, and others were considered "undesirables."
5. **Hitler's Policies**: Abolition of democratic institutions, creation of a police state (Gestapo, SS), control over media, propaganda, aggressive nationalism, and economic recovery through rearmament.
6. **The Holocaust**: The systematic, state-sponsored persecution and murder of approximately 6 million Jews, along with other targeted groups. This genocide was carried out through ghettos, concentration camps, and extermination camps.
7. **World War II**: Began with Germany's invasion of Poland (1939). Ended with Germany's defeat in 1945 and the Nuremberg Trials.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Treaty of Versailles**: The 1919 peace treaty that imposed harsh terms on Germany, including war guilt, territorial losses, reparations, and military restrictions.
- **Weimar Republic**: Germany's democratic government from 1919 to 1933.
- **Nazism**: The ideology and practices of the Nazi Party, characterized by extreme nationalism, racism, and totalitarianism.
- **Fuhrer**: Title used by Hitler, meaning "Leader."
- **Gestapo**: The secret state police of Nazi Germany.
- **Holocaust**: The genocide of approximately 6 million Jews by the Nazi regime during WWII.
- **Propaganda**: Biased information used to shape public opinion. The Nazis used films, posters, radio, and speeches extensively.
- **Genocide**: The deliberate killing of a large number of people from a particular nation or ethnic group.
- **Nuremberg Trials**: International military tribunals held after WWII to prosecute Nazi war criminals.
