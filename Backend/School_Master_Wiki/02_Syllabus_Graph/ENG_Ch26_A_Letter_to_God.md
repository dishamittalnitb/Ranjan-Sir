# Chapter 26: A Letter to God

## Theme & Summary

**Theme:** The story explores themes of faith, innocence, and the irony of human nature. It portrays the unwavering faith of a simple farmer, the kindness of strangers, and the humorous tragedy of blind faith that fails to recognize human goodness when it appears.

**Summary:** Lencho is a hardworking farmer whose field of ripe corn is completely destroyed by a hailstorm. With unshakeable faith in God, he writes a letter addressed to "God" asking for a hundred pesos to sow his field again and feed his family until the next harvest. The post office employees, initially amused, are moved by Lencho's faith. The postmaster collects money from his colleagues and sends seventy pesos to Lencho, signing it "God." When Lencho receives the money, instead of being grateful, he becomes angry — he firmly believes God would never make a mistake. He writes another letter to God, asking Him to send the remaining thirty pesos, but not through the mail, because "the post office employees are a bunch of crooks."

## Character List

- **Lencho:** A poor but hardworking farmer; his faith in God is unshakeable but ironically makes him blind to human kindness.
- **Lencho's Wife:** A supportive partner who shares Lencho's faith and hardship.
- **The Postmaster:** A kind-hearted man who organizes the collection of money for Lencho; wants to preserve Lencho's faith.
- **Post Office Employees:** Initially laugh at Lencho's letter but later contribute generously.

## Key Quotes

1. "These aren't raindrops falling from the sky, they are new coins."
2. "God, if you don't help me, my family and I will go hungry this year."
3. "He had such faith in God that he could not believe that God would make a mistake."
4. "The post office employees are a bunch of crooks."
