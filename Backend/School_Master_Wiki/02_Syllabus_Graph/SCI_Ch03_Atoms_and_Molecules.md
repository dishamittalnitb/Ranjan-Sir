# Chapter 03: Atoms and Molecules

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Atoms
- The smallest particle of an element that can take part in a chemical reaction.
- Atom is the basic building block of all matter.
- Atoms are extremely small (radius ~ 10^-10 m, measured in nanometers).

### 1.2 Molecules
- A group of two or more atoms chemically bonded together.
- **Molecule of an element:** Same type of atoms bonded (e.g., O2, N2, H2, P4, S8).
- **Molecule of a compound:** Different types of atoms bonded (e.g., H2O, CO2, NaCl).

### 1.3 Laws of Chemical Combination
| Law | Statement |
|-----|-----------|
| **Law of Conservation of Mass** | Mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products. |
| **Law of Constant Proportions (Law of Definite Proportions)** | A chemical compound always contains the same elements combined together in the same proportion by mass. |

### 1.4 Dalton's Atomic Theory (Key Postulates)
- All matter is made of very tiny particles called atoms.
- Atoms are indivisible (later modified due to discovery of sub-atomic particles).
- Atoms of a given element are identical in mass and chemical properties.
- Atoms of different elements have different masses and chemical properties.
- Atoms combine in the ratio of small whole numbers to form compounds.
- In a chemical reaction, atoms are rearranged.

### 1.5 Atomic Mass and Molecular Mass
- **Atomic mass:** The mass of a single atom of an element. Measured in atomic mass units (u). Carbon-12 atom is taken as the standard: 1 u = 1/12th the mass of one C-12 atom.
- **Molecular mass:** The sum of atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule. E.g., H2O = 2(1) + 16 = 18 u.

### 1.6 Ions and Chemical Formula
- **Ion:** A charged particle formed when an atom gains or loses electrons.
  - **Cation:** Positively charged (loses electrons), e.g., Na+, Ca2+, Al3+.
  - **Anion:** Negatively charged (gains electrons), e.g., Cl-, O2-, SO4 2-.
- **Valency:** The combining capacity of an element.
- **Chemical formula:** Represents the composition of a compound using symbols and subscripts.

### 1.7 Mole Concept
- **Mole:** Amount of substance containing 6.022 x 10^23 (Avogadro's number) particles (atoms, molecules, or ions).
- **Molar mass:** Mass of one mole of a substance in grams; numerically equal to atomic/molecular mass in u.
- **Number of moles (n)** = Given mass (g) / Molar mass (g/mol)
- **Number of particles** = n x Avogadro's number (NA)

## 2. Key Formulas/Definitions

| Term | Formula / Definition |
|------|---------------------|
| **Atomic Mass Unit (u)** | 1/12th the mass of one C-12 atom |
| **Molecular Mass** | Sum of atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule |
| **Mole (n)** | n = Given mass (g) / Molar mass (g/mol) |
| **Avogadro's Number (NA)** | 6.022 x 10^23 particles per mole |
| **Number of particles** | N = n x NA |
| **Law of Conservation of Mass** | Mass of reactants = Mass of products |
| **Law of Constant Proportions** | Elements in a compound are always present in a fixed ratio by mass |

### Common Chemical Formulas to Remember
- Water: H2O
- Carbon dioxide: CO2
- Sodium chloride: NaCl
- Calcium oxide: CaO
- Magnesium chloride: MgCl2
- Aluminium oxide: Al2O3
- Sodium hydroxide: NaOH
- Hydrochloric acid: HCl
- Sulphuric acid: H2SO4
- Nitric acid: HNO3

## 6. Practical Experiments (from CBSE Syllabus)

**Experiment 11 (Unit-III, but maps to Unit-I content):** Verification of the law of conservation of mass in a chemical reaction.
- **Objective:** To experimentally verify that mass is conserved during a chemical reaction.
- **Apparatus:** Conical flask, test tube, thread, cork, balance, solutions of reactants (e.g., BaCl2 and Na2SO4, or AgNO3 and NaCl).
- **Key Learning:** The total mass before and after the reaction remains constant.
