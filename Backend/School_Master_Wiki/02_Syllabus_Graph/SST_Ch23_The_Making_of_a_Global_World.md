# Chapter 23: The Making of a Global World

**[EXAM + INTERDISCIPLINARY PROJECT]**
Subtopics 1 to 1.3 (Pre-Modern World to Conquest, Disease and Trade) are evaluated in the Board Examination. Subtopics 2 to 4.4 (The nineteenth century to end of Bretton Woods) are part of the Interdisciplinary Project assessed internally for 5 marks.

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Summarise the changes that transformed the world in different areas.
- Depict the global interconnectedness from the Pre-modern to the present day.
- Enumerate the destructive impact of colonialism on the livelihoods of colonised people.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **The Pre-Modern World (Board Exam Subtopics 1 to 1.3)**:
   - **Silk Routes**: Ancient trade routes connecting China, Central Asia, India, and Europe; facilitated movement of goods, ideas, religion (Buddhism, Christianity), and technology.
   - **Food Travels**: Introduction of crops across continents -- potatoes from Americas to Europe, chillies to India, pasta/noodles from China to Italy.
   - **Conquest, Disease, and Trade**: European conquest of Americas (16th century) brought diseases (smallpox) that decimated native populations. The discovery of the Americas transformed global trade and power.
2. **The Nineteenth Century (1815-1914)**: [IDP Subtopics]
   - Industrial Revolution in Britain led to massive demand for raw materials and markets.
   - Improved transportation (steamships, railways) and technology (refrigeration) accelerated global trade.
   - Colonialism extracted resources and created new trade patterns.
   - Migration of indentured labour from India and China to plantations in the Caribbean, Africa, and Southeast Asia.
3. **The Inter-War Economy**: [IDP Subtopics]
   - World War I disrupted global economy.
   - The Great Depression (1929) caused worldwide economic collapse; affected India through falling agricultural prices.
4. **Rebuilding the World Economy: The Post-War Era**: [IDP Subtopics]
   - Bretton Woods Conference (1944) established IMF and World Bank.
   - Post-WWII reconstruction and decolonization.
   - End of Bretton Woods system in the 1970s and the beginning of globalization.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Globalisation**: The increasing interconnection and integration of economies, cultures, and populations across the world.
- **Silk Routes**: A network of trade routes connecting East Asia to Europe.
- **Indentured Labour**: Labourers hired under contract for a specific period to work in plantations abroad.
- **Great Depression**: A severe worldwide economic downturn that began in 1929.
- **Bretton Woods Institutions**: The IMF and World Bank, established in 1944 to regulate the international monetary system.
- **Tariff**: A tax imposed on imported goods.
- **Corn Laws**: British laws that restricted import of grains to protect domestic agriculture; repealed in 1846.
- **Colonialism**: The policy of acquiring and exploiting colonies for economic gain.
- **Plantation**: A large farm that grows a single cash crop, often using hired or slave labour.
