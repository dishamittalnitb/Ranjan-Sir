# Chapter 21: The Rise of Nationalism in Europe

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Infer how the French Revolution had an impact on the European countries in the making of a nation state.
- Comprehend the nature of the diverse social movements of the time.
- Analyse and infer the evolution of the idea of nationalism which led to the formation of nation states in Europe and elsewhere.
- Evaluate the reasons which led to the First World War.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **The French Revolution and the Idea of the Nation (1789)**:
   - Introduced concepts of citizenship, equal rights, sovereignty, and the nation as a collective identity.
   - National symbols: tricolor flag, National Assembly, "La Marseillaise" anthem.
   - Napoleon spread revolutionary ideas across Europe through conquest; later defeated at Waterloo (1815).
2. **The Making of Nationalism in Europe**:
   - Europe was divided into kingdoms, duchies, and empires; no national identity.
   - Aristocracy was socially and politically dominant.
   - Rise of the middle class, liberalism, and demand for constitutional government.
   - Economic nationalism: Zollverein (1834) -- customs union of German states, led by Prussia.
3. **Revolutionaries and the Age of Revolutions (1830-1848)**:
   - July Revolution (1830) in France.
   - Greek War of Independence (1821-32).
   - Revolutions of 1848 -- demand for constitutional rule, national unification, and liberal rights.
4. **Unification of Germany (1866-1871)**:
   - Led by Prussia under Otto von Bismarck.
   - Three wars over seven years: against Denmark, Austria, and France.
   - German Empire proclaimed in 1871 at Versailles.
5. **Unification of Italy (1859-1871)**:
   - Led by Count Cavour (Prime Minister of Sardinia-Piedmont), Giuseppe Garibaldi (Red Shirts), and Giuseppe Mazzini (Young Italy).
   - Victor Emmanuel II became King of a unified Italy in 1861.
6. **Visualizing the Nation**: Allegories like Marianne (France) and Germania (Germany) represented the nation. National flags, anthems, and language played unifying roles.
7. **Nationalism and Imperialism**: By the late 19th century, nationalism became aligned with imperialism. Competing nationalist ambitions among European powers led to World War I (1914-1918).

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Nation-State**: A state in which the majority of citizens share a common identity -- history, language, culture, or ethnicity.
- **Liberalism**: A political ideology advocating individual freedom, equality, and constitutional government; in economic terms, freedom of markets and abolition of state restrictions.
- **Zollverein**: A customs union formed by German states in 1834 under Prussian leadership to promote free trade.
- **Conservatism**: A political philosophy supporting tradition, established institutions, and gradual change rather than revolution.
- **Allegory**: An abstract idea expressed through a person or thing. Marianne (France) and Germania (Germany) were female allegories of the nation.
- **Romanticism**: A cultural movement that emphasized emotion, imagination, and national folk traditions.
- **Balkan Nationalism**: Nationalism among various ethnic groups in the Balkan region, contributing to the tensions leading to WWI.
