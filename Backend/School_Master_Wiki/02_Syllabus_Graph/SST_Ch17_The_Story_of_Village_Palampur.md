# Chapter 17: The Story of Village Palampur

**[PERIODIC ASSESSMENT ONLY]**
This chapter is assessed as part of Periodic Assessment (Pen Paper Test, 5 marks) only. It is not included in the Board Examination.

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Enlist the requirements of production and comprehend the interdependence of these requirements.
- Correlate farming and non-farming activities to economic growth.
- Comprehend how the significance of conditions of farming and the factors of production impact economic development.
- Find solutions to foster an equitable society.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Overview**: Palampur is a hypothetical village that illustrates the functioning of a typical Indian rural economy. It shows the organization of production and how different resources are mobilized.
2. **Factors of Production**: Four essential requirements:
   - **Land**: Fixed natural resource; includes soil, water, forests, minerals.
   - **Labour**: Human effort -- skilled and unskilled. Includes hired labour and family labour.
   - **Physical Capital**: Tools, machines, buildings (fixed capital) + raw materials and money (working capital).
   - **Human Capital**: Knowledge and enterprise to combine the other three factors.
3. **Farming in Palampur**:
   - Land is fixed and has been fully cultivated since the 1960s.
   - Multiple cropping (growing more than one crop per year) is practiced.
   - Modern farming methods: use of HYV (High Yielding Variety) seeds, chemical fertilizers, pesticides, tubewell irrigation, and tractors (Green Revolution technology).
   - Small farmers borrow from large farmers or moneylenders at high interest.
   - Medium and large farmers produce a surplus and sell it in markets.
4. **Non-Farming Activities**: 
   - Dairy farming (milk sold to nearby town).
   - Small-scale manufacturing (handicrafts, jaggery production).
   - Shopkeeping and trade.
   - Transport (rickshaws, jeeps, tongas).
   - Computer education at a local center.
5. **Sustainability Concerns**: Overuse of chemical fertilizers and groundwater is depleting soil fertility and water tables. Sustainable farming practices are needed.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Factors of Production**: The inputs used in the production of goods or services.
- **Fixed Capital**: Long-lasting inputs like tools, machinery, buildings.
- **Working Capital**: Raw materials and money used in production that gets consumed or transformed.
- **Multiple Cropping**: Growing more than one crop on the same piece of land in a year.
- **HYV (High Yielding Variety) Seeds**: Seeds that produce significantly higher yields per unit area.
- **Green Revolution**: A set of technological initiatives (1960s-70s) that greatly increased agricultural production, using HYV seeds, irrigation, fertilizers, and pesticides.
- **Surplus**: The portion of farm produce that is sold in the market after self-consumption.
- **Subsistence Farming**: Farming for self-consumption with little or no surplus.
