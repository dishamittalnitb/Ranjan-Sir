# Chapter 12: What is Democracy? Why Democracy?

## Learning Outcomes
The students will be able to:
- Examine the concept of structural components of Democracy and its forms/features.
- Compare and contrast the working of democracies of India and some other countries and discuss differences and similarities in each country.
- Analyse and infer on the different historical processes and forces that have contributed to the promotion of democracy.

## Concept Summary / Key Points
1. **Definition of Democracy**: Derived from Greek words "demos" (people) and "kratia" (rule). Democracy is a form of government in which rulers are elected by the people. Abraham Lincoln defined it as "government of the people, by the people, for the people."
2. **Features of Democracy**:
   - Free and fair elections at regular intervals.
   - Rule of law and respect for rights.
   - One person, one vote, one value (universal adult franchise).
   - Major decisions are taken by elected leaders.
   - Protection of fundamental rights.
   - An independent judiciary.
   - Freedom of expression and association.
3. **Why Democracy?**: Democracy is preferred because it:
   - Ensures accountability and responsiveness of the government.
   - Enhances the dignity of citizens.
   - Provides a method to resolve conflicts peacefully.
   - Allows for correction of mistakes.
   - Promotes equality and freedom.
4. **Non-Democratic Regimes**: Countries like China (single-party rule), Saudi Arabia (monarchy), Myanmar (military rule), and Zimbabwe (under Robert Mugabe) are examples where democratic principles are not fully practiced.
5. **Distinction Between Democratic and Non-Democratic Governments**: Democratic governments are accountable, responsive, and legitimate. Non-democratic governments do not provide the people the power to choose or change their rulers.
6. **Limitations of Democracy**: Slow decision-making, influence of money and muscle power, political instability, corruption, and delays in implementation.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **Democracy**: A system of government in which supreme power is vested in the people and exercised directly by them or through their elected representatives.
- **Universal Adult Franchise**: The right of all adult citizens to vote, irrespective of caste, creed, religion, gender, or economic status.
- **Rule of Law**: The principle that all persons, institutions, and entities (including the government) are accountable to the law.
- **Referendum**: A direct vote in which an entire electorate is asked to either accept or reject a particular proposal.
- **Representative Democracy**: A form of democracy where citizens elect representatives to make decisions on their behalf.
- **Direct Democracy**: A form of democracy where citizens participate directly in decision-making (e.g., ancient Athens).
- **Accountability**: The obligation of the government to answer to the people for its actions and decisions.
- **Legitimacy**: The acceptance and recognition of a government's right to rule by the people.
