# Chapter 22: Heredity and Evolution

## 1. Concept Summary

### 1.1 Heredity
The transmission of traits (characteristics) from parents to offspring.

### 1.2 Key Terminology
| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Gene** | Functional unit of heredity; segment of DNA that codes for a protein/ trait. |
| **Allele** | Alternative forms of a gene (e.g., tall T and dwarf t are alleles for plant height). |
| **Genotype** | Genetic constitution of an organism (e.g., TT, Tt, tt). |
| **Phenotype** | Observable physical characteristics (e.g., tall or dwarf). |
| **Homozygous** | Having identical alleles for a trait (TT or tt). Also called pure/purebred. |
| **Heterozygous** | Having different alleles for a trait (Tt). Also called hybrid. |
| **Dominant trait** | The allele that expresses itself even in heterozygous condition (represented by capital letter, e.g., T for tall). |
| **Recessive trait** | The allele that expresses only in homozygous condition (represented by small letter, e.g., t for dwarf). |
| **F1 Generation** | First filial generation - offspring of the parental cross (P). |
| **F2 Generation** | Second filial generation - offspring of F1 x F1 cross. |

### 1.3 Mendel's Contribution - Laws of Inheritance
Gregor Johann Mendel (1822-1884), the "Father of Genetics", conducted experiments on garden pea (Pisum sativum).

**Why Pea Plant?**
- Easy to grow; short life cycle.
- Many contrasting traits (tall/dwarf, round/wrinkled seeds, yellow/green seeds, violet/white flowers, etc.).
- Self-pollinating (pure lines) and can be cross-pollinated artificially.

#### Mendel's Monohybrid Cross (Single Trait)
Cross between tall (TT) and dwarf (tt) pea plants.

**P generation:** Tall (TT) x Dwarf (tt)
**F1 generation:** All Tall (Tt) - only dominant trait appears.
**F1 self-pollinated (Tt x Tt):**
**F2 generation:**
| | T | t |
|---|---|---|
| **T** | TT (Tall) | Tt (Tall) |
| **t** | Tt (Tall) | tt (Dwarf) |

- **Phenotypic ratio:** 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf
- **Genotypic ratio:** 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt

#### Mendel's Dihybrid Cross (Two Traits)
Cross between Round Yellow (RRYY) and Wrinkled Green (rryy).

**F1:** All Round Yellow (RrYy).
**F2 Phenotypic ratio:** 9 Round Yellow : 3 Round Green : 3 Wrinkled Yellow : 1 Wrinkled Green

#### Mendel's Laws
| Law | Statement |
|-----|-----------|
| **Law of Dominance** | In a heterozygous condition, one allele (dominant) masks the presence of the other allele (recessive). |
| **Law of Segregation** | Alleles for a trait separate (segregate) during gamete formation such that each gamete carries only one allele for each trait. |
| **Law of Independent Assortment** | Alleles for different traits segregate independently of one another during gamete formation (applies to genes on different chromosomes). |

### 1.4 Sex Determination
- **In humans:** The sex is determined by the sex chromosomes (X and Y).
  - Male: 44 autosomes + XY (heterogametic - produces 50% X-bearing and 50% Y-bearing sperms)
  - Female: 44 autosomes + XX (homogametic - produces only X-bearing eggs)
  - Child's sex is determined by the father's sperm (whether it carries X or Y).
- **In some reptiles/birds:** ZW system (female: ZW, male: ZZ).
- **In some insects:** XO system (male: X, female: XX).

**Sex determination cross:**
| | X | X |
|---|---|---|
| **X** | XX (Female) | XX (Female) |
| **Y** | XY (Male) | XY (Male) |

- Probability of a male child = 50%, female child = 50%.

### 1.5 Evolution (Brief Introduction)
- **Evolution:** The gradual change in the characteristics of a species over many generations.
- **Sources of variation:** Mutation (change in DNA sequence), recombination during sexual reproduction.
- **Mechanism:** Natural selection (Darwin's theory) - organisms with advantageous traits are more likely to survive and reproduce, passing those traits to the next generation.

#### Evidence of Evolution
- **Homologous organs:** Same basic structure, different functions. Indicate common ancestry. E.g., forelimbs of human, horse, whale, bat, frog.
- **Analogous organs:** Different basic structure, similar function. Indicate adaptation to similar environments, NOT common ancestry. E.g., wings of bird, bat, insect.
