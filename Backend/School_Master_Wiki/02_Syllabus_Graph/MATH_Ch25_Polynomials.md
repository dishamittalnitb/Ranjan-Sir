# Chapter 25: Polynomials (Class X)

## Concept Summary

This chapter studies the zeros of polynomials and their relationship with coefficients:

1. **Recap**: Polynomial, degree, linear, quadratic, cubic polynomials. Zeros of a polynomial -- a real number k such that p(k) = 0.

2. **Geometrical Meaning of Zeros**: For a polynomial p(x), the x-coordinates of points where the graph y = p(x) intersects the x-axis are the zeros. 
   - Linear polynomial: exactly one zero ( intersects x-axis at one point)
   - Quadratic polynomial: at most two zeros (parabola shape)
   - Cubic polynomial: at most three zeros

3. **Relationship Between Zeros and Coefficients** (for quadratic polynomials):
   - If alpha and beta are zeros of ax^2 + bx + c:
     - Sum of zeros: alpha + beta = -b/a
     - Product of zeros: alpha * beta = c/a

4. **Application**: Forming a quadratic polynomial when zeros are given, verifying the relationship, and solving problems.

5. **Division Algorithm for Polynomials**: If p(x) and g(x) are any two polynomials with g(x) != 0, there exist polynomials q(x) and r(x) such that p(x) = g(x) * q(x) + r(x), where r(x) = 0 or degree of r(x) < degree of g(x).

## Key Formulas/Theorems

**For Quadratic Polynomial ax^2 + bx + c** (with zeros alpha, beta):
- alpha + beta = -b/a
- alpha * beta = c/a

**Quadratic Formula Relation**:
- Zeros = [-b +/- sqrt(b^2 - 4ac)] / 2a
- Sum = -b/a, Product = c/a

**Polynomial Construction**: If zeros are alpha and beta, polynomial is:
- k[x^2 - (alpha + beta)x + alpha * beta], where k is any non-zero real number
